0.5  Polynomials

Definition 0.5.1 (Polynomial)

A polynomial over a field F in the indeterminate x is an expression of the form: p(x)=anxn+an−1xn−1+⋯+a1x+a0=∑k=0nakxk where a0,a1,…,an∈F are called the coefficients of p(x).

Definition 0.5.2 (Degree)

Let p(x)=anxn+an−1xn−1+⋯+a1x+a0 be a nonzero polynomial with an≠0. The degree of p(x), denoted deg⁡p(x), is n. The coefficient an is called the leading coefficient.

By convention, the zero polynomial p(x)=0 has degree −∞.

Definition 0.5.3 (Polynomial Ring)

The set of all polynomials over a field F is denoted F[x]: F[x]={∑k=0nakxk:n∈N,ak∈F} This set forms a ring under polynomial addition and multiplication. (You can omit what ring means here.)

Example (Elements of the Polynomial Ring)

The following are elements of R[x]: 3x2−2x+1,x5+πx3−2,7,0 Note that constant polynomials (including 0) are also polynomials.

In linear algebra, we often work with polynomials of bounded degree.

Definition 0.5.4 (Polynomials of Degree at Most n)

Let F be a field and n∈N. The set of all polynomials over F of degree at most n is denoted: F[x]≤n={p(x)∈F[x]:deg⁡p(x)≤n} Equivalently: F[x]≤n={anxn+an−1xn−1+⋯+a1x+a0:ak∈F}

Example (Polynomials of Degree at Most 2)

The set R[x]≤2 consists of all polynomials of degree at most 2: R[x]≤2={ax2+bx+c:a,b,c∈R} Examples include 3x2−2x+1, 5x+7, and −4.

Definition 0.5.5 (Polynomials of Degree Exactly n)

We use F[x]=n to denote polynomials of degree exactly n: F[x]=n={p(x)∈F[x]:deg⁡p(x)=n} Equivalently: F[x]=n={anxn+an−1xn−1+⋯+a1x+a0:an≠0,ak∈F}

Remark. Note the distinction:

  • F[x]≤n contains polynomials of degree ≤n (including the zero polynomial)
  • F[x]=n contains polynomials of degree exactly n (so an≠0)

Thus F[x]≤n⊋F[x]=n, and in fact F[x]≤n=F[x]=n∪F[x]≤n−1.

Example (Comparing Notations)

The following examples highlight the difference between the notations introduced above:

  • R[x]≤2={ax2+bx+c:a,b,c∈R} includes x2+1, 3x−2, and 5
  • R[x]=2={ax2+bx+c:a≠0,a,b,c∈R} includes x2+1 but not 3x−2 or 5
