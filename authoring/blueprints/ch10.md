# Blueprint: Chapter 10 — Canonical Forms

**Directory:** `src/ch10-canonical-forms/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Canonical Forms".

**Purpose.** Finish the similarity problem. Every operator whose characteristic polynomial splits has a Jordan form, unique up to the order of its blocks. Every operator over any field has a rational canonical form. These give complete similarity invariants. On the way:
- generalized eigenspaces;
- the structure of nilpotent operators (partitions and Young diagrams);
- the Weyr form;
- cyclic subspaces and companion matrices;
- modules over F[x] and the Smith normal form of xI − A as the computational route;
- the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition;
- functions of matrices, the matrix exponential and linear ODEs;
- convergent and power-bounded matrices (without norms, via the Jordan form entrywise).

**Pays off promises:**
- Ch 4: similarity is decided in general (Jordan / rational forms).
- Ch 6: canonical forms from primary decomposition.
- Ch 7: complete similarity invariants.
- Ch 9: the best matrix when g < a; generalized eigenspaces named.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–9. No inner products, no norms (Ch 16): convergence of matrix sequences is entrywise. For e^A, the convergence of the exponential series is proved entrywise for Jordan blocks via the explicit formula, and for general A via A = PJP⁻¹. Where a real-analysis fact is needed (the absolute convergence of Σ tᵏ/k!, the derivative of a power series), state it in a clearly marked "What we assume from analysis" remark, as in Ch 6 §05.

**Notation:**
- G_λ(T) generalized eigenspace (NOTATION.md already has it: n = dim V as the exponent);
- J_k(λ) Jordan block (1 on the **super**diagonal);
- C(p) companion matrix (convention: the last column carries −a₀, …, −a_{n−1}; state it and match Ch 7 §10's C(p) — read it);
- e^{A};
- invariant factors d₁ | d₂ | ⋯ | d_r with **d_r = m_T**; elementary divisors.

## 01-generalized-eigenspaces.md — Generalized eigenspaces

- **Hook:** [[λ, 1], [0, λ]] has too few eigenvectors, but (T − λI)² kills everything. Enlarge the eigenspace.
- **Definition** `def-generalized-eigenvector`, `def-generalized-eigenspace` (**major**): G_λ = ⋃_k ker(T − λI)^k.
- **Stabilization** — `thm-kernel-chain-stabilizes`: the chain ker N ⊆ ker N² ⊆ ⋯ stabilizes by k = dim V, and once two consecutive kernels agree, all later ones agree. Hence G_λ = ker(T − λI)^n.
- **Generalized eigenspace decomposition** — `thm-generalized-eigenspace-decomposition`: if p_T splits, V = ⊕ G_λ; each G_λ is invariant; T − λI is nilpotent on G_λ; dim G_λ = a(λ). Proof via Ch 9 `thm-primary-decomposition` (read it) plus a dimension count using the characteristic polynomial of the restriction (Ch 9 `thm-invariant-subspace-matrix`).
- **Worked:** a 4×4 with eigenvalues 2, 2, 2, 5 — `exm-generalized-eigenspaces-4x4` (verify privately).
- **Warning:** G_λ ≠ E_λ in general, and "generalized eigenvector" includes the ordinary ones.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute G_λ for listed matrices; show that the image chain im N ⊇ im N² ⊇ … stabilizes too; dim G_λ = a(λ) on an example.
  - **C:** V = ker N^n ⊕ im N^n for any operator N (Fitting decomposition); if S commutes with T, then S maps each G_λ(T) into itself.

## 02-nilpotent-operators.md — Nilpotent operators

- **Definition** `def-nilpotent` (recall earlier uses: Ch 2 D on polynomials, Ch 8 exercises); the index of nilpotency; N^n = 0.
- **Jordan chains** — `def-jordan-chain`: v, Nv, …, N^{k−1}v with N^k v = 0. Read the columns of a Jordan block: the "read the columns" hook.
- **Structure theorem for nilpotent operators** — `thm-nilpotent-structure`: V is a direct sum of N-cyclic subspaces spanned by Jordan chains; in the corresponding basis, [N] = J_{k₁}(0) ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ J_{k_r}(0). Proof by induction on dim V (or via a basis of ker N extended through im N). The drafter chooses one fully rigorous proof, with an Idea block and a TikZ picture of the chains as a staircase / Young diagram.
- **Uniqueness via ranks** — `thm-nilpotent-block-sizes-from-ranks`: the number of blocks of size ≥ k is rank N^{k−1} − rank N^k. Hence the partition (k₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ k_r) is a similarity invariant, and two nilpotent matrices are similar ⇔ they have the same ranks of powers.
- **Young diagrams / partitions** — `def-partition-of-n`, with the dual partition as the sequence of kernel dimension jumps.
- **Worked:** find the Jordan basis of a 4×4 nilpotent — `exm-nilpotent-jordan-basis`.
- **Warning:** the successive differences r_{k−1} − r_k count blocks of size ≥ k, not blocks of size k; subtract twice for exact counts.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** block sizes from ranks; the number of similarity classes of nilpotent 4×4 matrices (partitions of 4: five); find a Jordan basis.
  - **C:** N and Nᵀ are similar; a nilpotent N with N^{n−1} ≠ 0 is similar to J_n(0).

## 03-jordan-canonical-form.md — The Jordan canonical form

- **Goal stated first; the "dream comes true" framing** (do not attribute).
- **Jordan block** `def-jordan-block`; Jordan matrix.
- **Jordan Canonical Form Theorem** — `thm-jordan-canonical-form`: if p_T splits, there is a basis in which [T] = ⊕ J_{k_i}(λ_i), unique up to the order of the blocks. Proof: generalized eigenspace decomposition, plus the nilpotent structure on each G_λ, plus uniqueness from ranks of (T − λI)^k.
- **Reading invariants** — `cor-jordan-invariants`: a(λ) = the total size of the λ-blocks; g(λ) = the number of λ-blocks; the exponent of (x − λ) in m_T = the largest λ-block size. Diagonalizable ⇔ all blocks have size 1 (recall Ch 9).
- **Similarity criterion** — `cor-similar-iff-same-jordan-form`: pays off the Ch 4 and Ch 7 promises.
- **Algorithm:** eigenvalues → ranks of (A − λI)^k → block sizes → chains → P. **Worked:** a 5×5 with two eigenvalues and mixed blocks — `exm-jordan-form-5x5`, verified privately, P and J shown.
- **Warning:** the Jordan form depends on the field (it needs p to split); it is unstable numerically (forward pointer to Ch 20).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** Jordan forms from given p and m; the Jordan form and P of listed matrices; decide similarity of pairs.
  - **C:** A is similar to Aᵀ (over a splitting field; the general case is in §07); all possible Jordan forms with p = (x − 1)³(x − 2)² and m = (x − 1)²(x − 2).

## 05-cyclic-subspaces-and-companion-matrices.md — Cyclic subspaces and companion matrices

- `thm-cyclic-subspace-basis`: dim Z(v; T) = deg of the T-annihilator of v; the basis (v, …, T^{k−1}v); [T|_Z] = C(annihilator).

- **Cyclic vectors** — `thm-cyclic-iff-min-equals-char`: T has a cyclic vector ⇔ m_T = p_T. Prove both directions; the ⇒ direction was an exercise in Ch 9.
- **Worked:** find a cyclic vector and the companion form of a 3×3.

## 06-rational-canonical-form.md — The rational canonical form

- **Theorem** `thm-cyclic-decomposition`: V = Z(v₁) ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ Z(v_r) with annihilators d₁ | ⋯ | d_r (invariant factors), d_r = m_T, and ∏ d_i = p_T. Uniqueness of the invariant factors. The proof is the hardest in the chapter:
  - **Step roadmap:** existence via a maximal-annihilator vector and an invariant complement (T-admissible subspaces), or via primary decomposition + the nilpotent-like structure for p^e; uniqueness via dimension counts of ker p(T)^k on the pieces.
  - The drafter chooses one route and makes it complete; the primary-decomposition route with elementary divisors is acceptable.
- **Rational canonical form** — `thm-rational-canonical-form`: [T] = C(d₁) ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ C(d_r) (in the order d₁ | ⋯ | d_r), over any field. `cor-similar-iff-same-invariant-factors`.
- **Elementary divisors form** — `thm-elementary-divisor-form` (companions of the prime powers p_i^{e_{ij}}); relation to Jordan (C((x − λ)^k) ~ J_k(λ)).
- **Similarity is independent of the field** — `cor-similarity-field-independent`: matrices over K ⊆ L similar over L are similar over K. Via invariant factors, which are computed in K[x].
- **Worked:** the rational form of a 4×4 over ℚ with p = (x² + 1)²; both the invariant-factor and elementary-divisor forms.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**

  - **B:** invariant factors from given data; rational forms of listed matrices; count similarity classes of 3×3 matrices over 𝔽₂ with given p.
  - **C:** a matrix is similar to a companion matrix ⇔ m = p; the number of similarity classes of 2×2 matrices over 𝔽₂.

## 07-smith-normal-form.md — Modules over F[x] and the Smith normal form

- **V as an F[x]-module** (x acts as T), introduced as a viewpoint with a one-paragraph definition of a module (no general theory). Submodules = invariant subspaces; cyclic submodules = cyclic subspaces.
- **Elementary row and column operations over F[x]** (swap, multiply by a non-zero **scalar**, add a polynomial multiple) — `def-polynomial-elementary-operations`; equivalence of polynomial matrices.
- **Smith normal form** — `thm-smith-normal-form`: every n×n matrix over F[x] is equivalent to diag(d₁, …, d_n) with d_i | d_{i+1}, monic (or zero), and unique. Existence by the division algorithm on the lowest-degree entry. Uniqueness via determinantal divisors (gcds of k×k minors, invariant under the operations: recall Ch 7 Cauchy–Binet).
- **The key theorem** — `thm-similar-iff-xi-minus-a-equivalent`: A ~ B over F ⇔ xI − A and xI − B are equivalent over F[x]; the invariant factors of A are the non-unit Smith diagonal entries of xI − A. Prove it carefully. This is the rigorous computational route: explain that the proof of ⇐ constructs P using a division of matrix polynomials by (xI − A).
- `cor-a-similar-to-transpose`: A ~ Aᵀ (the Smith forms of xI − A and its transpose agree, via determinantal divisors). Pays off Ch 4's exercise pointer.
- **Worked:** the Smith form of xI − A for a 3×3 by hand, with every operation named — `exm-smith-form-3x3`, verified privately.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** Smith forms of small polynomial matrices; invariant factors via determinantal divisors; decide similarity.
  - **C:** the Smith normal form over ℤ (same algorithm) and the structure of finite abelian groups — stated, one example only; a 2×2 proof of uniqueness.

## 08-jordan-chevalley.md — Semisimple operators and the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition

- **Semisimple** — `def-semisimple-operator`: m_T has no repeated irreducible factors (over a perfect field; say what this means in one remark: characteristic 0 or finite fields suffice), equivalently diagonalizable over a splitting field.
- **Jordan–Chevalley decomposition** — `thm-jordan-chevalley`: when p_T splits, T = S + N with S diagonalizable, N nilpotent, SN = NS, uniquely; S and N are polynomials in T. Proof via the generalized eigenspace projections (Ch 9 primary decomposition projections are polynomials in T).
- **Uniqueness** uses commuting diagonalizable + nilpotent facts (Ch 9 §09).
- **Worked:** the S + N decomposition of a 3×3 — `exm-jordan-chevalley-3x3`.
- **Warning:** the "obvious" split of a matrix into diagonal part + off-diagonal part is not the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition (it does not commute); an example.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**

  - **B:** find S and N for listed matrices; show N = 0 ⇔ T is diagonalizable; S and N for a block diagonal matrix.
  - **C:** the multiplicative version for invertible T (T = S(I + N′) with unipotent part); if T commutes with X, then so do S and N.

## 09-functions-of-matrices-and-odes.md — Functions of matrices, the exponential and linear ODEs

- **Polynomials to functions:** f(A) for f analytic near the spectrum, defined via the Jordan form — `def-matrix-function-jordan`: f(J_k(λ)) is upper triangular with f^{(j)}(λ)/j! on the j-th superdiagonal. Well-definedness: independent of the choice of P. Prove via the fact that f(A) = p(A) for the Hermite interpolating polynomial p (Ch 6 §06 CRT/Hermite; read it).
- **Matrix exponential** — `def-matrix-exponential`: e^A = Σ A^k/k!, with entrywise convergence justified via the Jordan form and the "What we assume from analysis" remark.
  - `thm-exponential-properties`: e^{A+B} = e^A e^B when AB = BA (warning: false otherwise, with a 2×2 counterexample); det e^A = e^{tr A}; (e^{tA})′ = Ae^{tA}.
- **Linear ODEs** — `thm-linear-ode-solution`: x′ = Ax, x(0) = x₀ has the unique solution e^{tA}x₀. Uniqueness via the derivative of e^{−tA}x(t). Worked: a 2×2 system with complex eigenvalues (oscillation), and a Jordan-block case (resonance, te^{λt}); a second-order scalar ODE via the companion matrix.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute e^{tA} for diagonalizable and Jordan-block 2×2 matrices; solve x′ = Ax; √A for a positive diagonalizable A via f(A).
  - **C:** e^{A+B} ≠ e^A e^B worked; all solutions of x′ = Ax tend to 0 ⇔ every eigenvalue has negative real part (the ⇒ and ⇐ via the Jordan form entrywise).

## 10-convergent-matrices.md — Convergent and power-bounded matrices

≈2,500–3,000 words.

- **Entrywise limits of A^k** via the Jordan form: J_k(λ)^m has entries C(m, j)λ^{m−j}.
- **Theorem** `thm-matrix-powers-converge-to-zero`: A^m → 0 ⇔ every eigenvalue has |λ| < 1.
- **Theorem** `thm-matrix-powers-bounded`: (A^m) is bounded ⇔ every eigenvalue has |λ| ≤ 1 and those with |λ| = 1 have only 1×1 blocks.
- **Theorem** `thm-matrix-powers-converge`: A^m converges ⇔ every eigenvalue has |λ| < 1, or λ = 1 with only 1×1 blocks; the limit is the projection onto E₁ along the other generalized eigenspaces.
- Connect to Ch 9 §11 Markov chains (convergence now fully characterized).
- **Neumann series** Σ A^k converges ⇔ |λ| < 1 for all λ, and then equals (I − A)⁻¹ — `thm-neumann-series-spectral`.
- **Forward:** spectral radius and norms, Ch 16.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** decide convergence of A^m for listed matrices; compute lim A^m; (I − A)⁻¹ via the Neumann series for a small A.
  - **C:** A^m bounded but not convergent example; the powers of a stochastic matrix are bounded (via |λ| ≤ 1 from Ch 9 and the block-size condition — prove the size-1 blocks for |λ| = 1 using boundedness of stochastic powers directly).
