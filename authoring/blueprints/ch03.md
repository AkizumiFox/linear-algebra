# Blueprint: Chapter 3 — Linear Maps

**Directory:** rename `src/ch02-linear-transformations/` → `src/ch03-linear-maps/`. **Chapter title:** "Linear Maps".

**Redirects:** `ch02-linear-transformations/` → `ch03-linear-maps/`. Any old file names that change also get single-file entries.

**Purpose.** Linear maps are the morphisms. The chapter goes from definition to Rank–Nullity, and from isomorphisms to matrices of maps, change of basis and similarity. It ends with rank factorization and the normal form under equivalence, quotients, and projections. Its **signature moves** are:
- injective via the kernel;
- count with Rank–Nullity instead of checking;
- abstract space → write down a matrix;
- the change-of-basis square.

**Notation change.** Linear maps are always T, S (the old text mixed f and T). Composition is ST. The old `\f(\v)` misuse of the bold macro must disappear.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–2 (all labels). **No determinants, no eigenvalues.**

## 02-kernel-image.md — Kernel and image

**Keep labels:** `def-kernel`, `def-image`, `sec-kernel-image-examples` (a heading id; keep it), `exm-differentiation-kernel-image`, `exm-trace-kernel-image`, `thm-prop-kernel`, `thm-prop-image`.

- **Hook:** kernel and image exist to measure injectivity and surjectivity.
- **Definitions;** they are subspaces (`thm-prop-kernel`, `thm-prop-image`).
- **Injective ⇔ ker T = {0}** — `thm-injective-iff-trivial-kernel`: "big fan of kernel", the one-line proof via T(u) = T(v) ⇒ T(u − v) = 0.
- **Image of a spanning list spans the image** — `thm-image-spanned-by-basis-images`.
- **Matrix case:** ker of x ↦ Ax is null(A) and im is col(A). Link to Ch 2 labels.
- **Examples:**
  - differentiation on ℝ[x]≤n;
  - trace;
  - projection;
  - `exm-integration`-type maps;
  - the shift on sequences (injective, not surjective): pays off the Ch 1 section 08 pointer.
  - **Degenerate:** the zero map and the identity.
- **Injective maps preserve independence** — `thm-injective-preserves-independence`; surjective maps preserve spanning.
- **Warnings:** ker ⊆ domain but im ⊆ codomain; don't mix them up. im T is not "the codomain".
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute kernels and images with bases (matrix map, T(p) = p′ + p, T(A) = A − Aᵀ); decide injectivity.
  - **C:** ker(ST) ⊇ ker T with an example of strictness; T injective ⇒ ST injective iff S is injective on im T.

## 03-rank-nullity.md — The Rank–Nullity Theorem

**Keep labels:** `def-nullity`, `def-rank`, `exm-nullity-rank-diff`, `thm-rank-nullity`.

- **Goal stated first.**
- **Rank–Nullity** — `thm-rank-nullity`, via the **dimension-formula move**:
  - take a basis of ker T, extend it to a basis of V;
  - Big Claim: the images of the new vectors form a basis of im T;
  - spanning, then independence; count.
  - Idea block with a picture.
- **Corollaries** — `cor-rank-nullity-consequences`: if dim V = dim W, then injective ⇔ surjective ⇔ bijective (count instead of check); no injective map when dim V > dim W; no surjective map when dim V < dim W.
- **Worked applications:**
  - **Polynomial interpolation** revisited: the evaluation map F[x]≤n → F^{n+1} is injective, hence surjective.
  - **Existence** of p ∈ ℝ[x]≤3 with prescribed p(0), p(1), p′(0), p′(1).
  - **Dimension of solution spaces** (Ch 2 matrix rank–nullity recovered).
- **Warning:** Rank–Nullity needs V finite-dimensional (W may not be). "Injective ⇔ surjective" also needs dim V = dim W finite; the shift is the counterexample.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** verify Rank–Nullity for three maps; show a map is an isomorphism by kernel alone; find dim of {p ∈ ℝ[x]≤4 : p(1) = p(2) = 0} via a surjective map.
  - **C:** rank(ST) ≥ rank S + rank T − dim V (Sylvester's inequality) with guided parts; if T: V → V with V finite-dimensional satisfies ker T = im T, then dim V is even, with an example.

## 05-isomorphisms.md — Invertible maps and isomorphisms

- **Hook:** when are two spaces "the same"?
- **Definitions:** `def-invertible-linear-map`; the inverse of a linear bijection is linear (`thm-inverse-is-linear`); `def-isomorphism`, `def-isomorphic`.
- **Classification** — `thm-isomorphic-iff-same-dimension`: finite-dimensional V ≅ W ⇔ dim V = dim W. `cor-coordinate-isomorphism`: V ≅ Fⁿ via v ↦ [v]_𝓑. Recall Ch 1 `thm-coordinates-linear`.
- **One-sided inverses** — `thm-one-sided-inverse-maps`: for dim V = dim W finite, ST = I ⇒ TS = I. Compare with Ch 2 `thm-one-sided-inverse`.
- **The grown TFAE** — `thm-invertible-operator-tfae` for T ∈ 𝓛(V).
- **Warning:** "isomorphic" is not "equal". An isomorphism is extra data; there is no natural isomorphism Fⁿ → V (forward pointer to duality in Ch 4).
- **Examples:** ℝ[x]≤n ≅ ℝ^{n+1}; M_{m×n} ≅ F^{mn}; ℂ ≅ ℝ² over ℝ.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find an explicit isomorphism between the symmetric 2×2 matrices and ℝ[x]≤2; show T(p) = p + p′ is an isomorphism of ℝ[x]≤n and find its inverse (Σ(−1)^k D^k); decide isomorphism of pairs of spaces.
  - **C:** if S, T ∈ 𝓛(V) and ST is invertible, then both are (finite-dimensional), with a counterexample in infinite dimensions (shifts).

## 06-matrix-of-a-map.md — The matrix of a linear map

- **Hook:** abstract space → write down a matrix. What does the choice of basis buy?
- **Definition** `def-matrix-of-linear-map`: \( \mtx{T}{\sB}{\sC} \), whose j-th column is \( \coord{T\v_j}{\sC} \).
- **Theorem** `thm-matrix-of-map-coordinates`: \( \coord{T\v}{\sC} = \mtx{T}{\sB}{\sC}\coord{\v}{\sB} \), with a commutative square picture (TikZ).
- **Composition** — `thm-matrix-of-composition`: \( \mtx{ST}{\sB}{\sD} = \mtx{S}{\sC}{\sD}\mtx{T}{\sB}{\sC} \). This is where matrix multiplication "comes from"; it pays off the Ch 0 hook.
- **𝓛(V, W) ≅ M_{m×n}(F)** — `thm-linear-maps-isomorphic-to-matrices`; dim 𝓛(V, W) = mn.
- **Worked examples:**
  - D on ℝ[x]≤3 in the monomial basis;
  - reflection across the line y = x in the standard basis and in a basis adapted to the line;
  - the transpose map on M₂;
  - rank and invertibility read from the matrix: `thm-rank-map-equals-rank-matrix`.
- **Warnings:** the ordered basis matters; the columns are images of basis vectors (not rows); the input basis is the subscript and the output basis the superscript (NOTATION.md).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** matrices of maps in given bases (T(p) = p(x + 1), T(A) = BA on M₂); recover T from a matrix; compute [ST] both ways.
  - **C:** [T]_𝓑 is upper triangular ⇔ span(v₁, …, v_k) is T-invariant for each k (define invariance in the exercise); the matrix of D on span(e^x, xe^x, x²e^x).

## 07-change-of-basis.md — Change of basis and similarity

- **Hook:** the same map looks simpler in a better basis (the reflection from 06).
- **Change-of-coordinates matrix** `def-change-of-coordinates-matrix` \( \mtx{\id}{\sB}{\sC} \); invertible, with inverse \( \mtx{\id}{\sC}{\sB} \) — `thm-change-of-coordinates`.
- **Change of basis for maps** — `thm-change-of-basis-maps`: \( \mtx{T}{\sB'}{\sC'} = \mtx{\id}{\sC}{\sC'}\mtx{T}{\sB}{\sC}\mtx{\id}{\sB'}{\sB} \). For operators, \( [T]_{\sB'} = P^{-1}[T]_{\sB}P \). Draw the commutative square (TikZ) and make it a named move.
- **Similarity** — `def-similar-matrices` (recall Ch 0 `exm-similarity`). `thm-similar-iff-same-operator`: A ~ B ⇔ they represent the same operator in two bases.
- **Similarity invariants:** rank, trace (`thm-trace-similarity-invariant`), A² = 0, and so on.
  - **Warning:** shared invariants do not imply similarity. I₂ vs [[1, 1], [0, 1]]: same trace and rank, not similar (proof: P⁻¹IP = I).
- **Worked:** diagonalize a reflection or projection by choosing a basis adapted to im and ker, as a preview of eigenvectors in Ch 8.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** change-of-coordinates matrices in ℝ² and ℝ[x]≤2; [T] in a new basis both ways; decide similarity for simple pairs using invariants or an explicit P.
  - **C:** show that only scalar matrices are similar only to themselves; the matrices [[0,1],[0,0]] and [[0,0],[1,0]] are similar.

## 08-rank-factorization.md — Rank, equivalence and factorizations

- **Column–row factorization** — `thm-column-row-factorization`: A = CR with C the pivot columns (m×r) and R the non-zero rows of the RREF (r×n). Row rank = column rank again, now as a factorization.
- **Equivalence of matrices** — `def-equivalent-matrices`: B = QAP with Q, P invertible.
  - **Normal form** — `thm-rank-normal-form`: A is equivalent to \( \begin{pmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \), with r = rank A. Two m×n matrices are equivalent ⇔ they have the same rank.
  - **Proof via maps:** choose bases adapted to ker and im (the Rank–Nullity basis). This is the "right bases make any map trivial" message, contrasted with similarity (same basis on both sides, a much harder classification: Ch 9).
- **Rank inequalities** for maps: rank(ST) ≤ min; Sylvester's inequality (if not done as an exercise in 03); Frobenius's inequality in the exercises.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** CR factorization of a 3×4 matrix; find Q, P with QAP in normal form for a 2×3; show rank-1 matrices are xyᵀ (recall Ch 2 exercise).
  - **C:** Frobenius inequality rank(ABC) + rank B ≥ rank(AB) + rank(BC), with scaffolding.

## 09-products-and-quotients.md — Products and quotient spaces

- **Products / external direct sums** V₁ × ⋯ × V_k — `def-product-of-spaces`; dim is the sum; relation to internal direct sums (`thm-internal-external-direct-sum`).
- **Quotient spaces:**
  - **Hook:** "forget U". Parallel planes; cosets v + U. Uses Ch 0 quotient sets and `thm-well-defined-on-quotient`.
  - **Definition** `def-quotient-space` with **well-definedness proofs** of the operations — `thm-quotient-space-operations-well-defined`.
  - **Quotient map** π: V → V/U, with kernel U.
  - `thm-dimension-quotient`: dim V/U = dim V − dim U (via Rank–Nullity on π, or via a basis extension).
  - **Warning:** a coset has many representatives; v + U = w + U ⇔ v − w ∈ U.
- **First Isomorphism Theorem** — `thm-first-isomorphism`: V/ker T ≅ im T, with a well-definedness check. Worked: ℝ³/line ≅ ℝ², and ℝ[x]≤n/{p : p(0) = 0} ≅ ℝ.
- **Second and Third Isomorphism Theorems** — `thm-second-isomorphism` ((U + W)/W ≅ U/(U ∩ W)), which gives another proof of the dimension formula. `thm-third-isomorphism`.
- **Universal property of the quotient** (stated as the factor theorem) — `thm-quotient-universal-property`.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** a basis of V/U for concrete U ⊆ ℝ⁴; show that a given T factors through a quotient; dim of M_n/{scalar matrices}.
  - **C:** give a quotient-based proof of the dimension formula; the induced operator on V/U when T(U) ⊆ U, and [T] in a basis adapted to U (block upper triangular), previewing Ch 7 and 8.

## 10-projections-and-trace.md — Projections and the trace of an operator

- **Projections** — `def-projection-operator`: P² = P.
  - `thm-projection-direct-sum`: V = im P ⊕ ker P, and conversely each decomposition V = U ⊕ W gives a unique projection onto U along W.
  - [P] in an adapted basis is diag(I, 0); hence rank P = tr P (over a field of characteristic 0; state it carefully).
  - **Warning:** "along W" matters; different complements give different projections (TikZ picture of two oblique projections onto the same line).
- **Trace of an operator** — `def-trace-operator`: tr T = tr [T]_𝓑, well defined by similarity invariance (`thm-trace-similarity-invariant`). tr(ST) = tr(TS).
- **Reflections and involutions:** T² = I ⇒ V = E₊ ⊕ E₋ in characteristic ≠ 2 — `thm-involution-decomposition` (the even/odd split again), without eigen-language beyond {v : Tv = v}.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find the projection onto a plane along a line in ℝ³ as a matrix; show P projection ⇒ I − P projection, with im and ker swapped; trace of the transpose map on M_n.
  - **C:** a sum of projections P + Q is a projection ⇔ PQ = QP = 0 (char ≠ 2); the only linear functionals φ on M_n with φ(AB) = φ(BA) are multiples of the trace.
