# Blueprint: Chapter 17 — Convexity

**Directory:** `src/ch17-convexity/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Convexity". **Part:** continues "Part V · Matrix Analysis" — add to `config/config.json` as a bare string after `src/ch16-variational`, **with a minimal edit** (do not round-trip the JSON; an earlier round-trip reformatted all 123 lines).

**Purpose.** Chapters 12, 15 and 16 kept meeting convexity without studying it: the positive semidefinite matrices form a convex cone (Ch 12 §01), every unit ball is convex because of the triangle inequality (Ch 15 §01), the sum of the top k eigenvalues is a convex function of the matrix (Ch 16 §06), and the diagonals of the Hermitian matrices with a given spectrum form a convex set (Ch 16 §08, exercise C3). This chapter makes convexity a subject. It builds

- convex sets, hulls and Carathéodory's theorem;
- separation by hyperplanes, the finite-dimensional heart of the Hahn–Banach theorem;
- gauges, support functions, and **dual norms** (see the note below);
- cones, Farkas's lemma, and linear-programming duality;
- the minimax theorem for matrix games;
- extreme points and Minkowski's theorem, and polytopes (Minkowski–Weyl);
- convex functions.

**Corrections found while drafting** (each verified by the drafter; the referees are checking them):

3. **§03, uniqueness of the nearest point** comes from the Pythagorean inequality that the variational inequality gives, not the parallelogram law.
4. **§08, Minkowski's theorem: the proof route in this blueprint stalls.** A supporting hyperplane taken in all of V can contain a lower-dimensional K entirely, and the induction on dimension makes no progress. The induction must run inside the affine hull (`def-dimension-convex-set`).
5. **§08, doubly stochastic matrices had never been defined** in the book (Ch 16 §08 uses the idea without a definition). §08 defines `def-doubly-stochastic` (Ω_n); Chapter 18 inherits it.
6. **§10, the book had no logarithm and no real powers**, and ‖·‖_p had never been proved to be a norm (Ch 15 §01 said so). §10 builds log from Ch 9's exp and proves Minkowski's inequality from Hölder's.
7. **§05, this blueprint's suggested negative example was a trap.** "The Lorentz cone projected" fails under a coordinate projection: dropping x₁ from {x₃ ≥ ‖(x₁,x₂)‖} gives {x₃ ≥ |x₂|}, which is closed. The projection must run along a boundary ray; §05 uses the 2×2 PSD cone (a rotated Lorentz cone), whose projection to (x, y) is {x > 0} ∪ {(0,0)}. Also: the inequality form of Farkas must be a *corollary*, not an exercise, since strong duality rests on it.
8. **§10 was split** after review (about 8,000 words): §10 "Convex Functions" keeps the theory; a new **§11 "Three Classical Inequalities"** holds the logarithm and real powers, weighted AM–GM, Young, Hölder, Minkowski and the dual p-norms, and the chapter summary. **Signature moves:**
- **Nearest point, then the normal direction.** The separation theorem is the geometry of the projection onto a closed convex set.
- **Count, then drop a point.** Carathéodory and the conic version: an affine or linear dependence lets you remove a point without leaving the hull.
- **Dualize.** Every statement about a cone has a statement about its dual; Farkas is the move made into a theorem, and LP duality is Farkas applied to one combined system.
- **Sup of linear is convex.** Support functions, dual norms, λ₁ and Ky Fan sums are all this.

**Negative results the chapter must contain:**
- A union of convex sets need not be convex (§01).
- Two disjoint closed convex sets need not be *strictly* separable — the region above a hyperbola and its asymptote (§03).
- The linear image of a closed convex cone need not be closed — this is why Farkas needs *finitely generated* cones (§05). Give an explicit example and verify it.
- The set of extreme points of a compact convex set need not be closed (§08) — an explicit example in ℝ³.
- A function convex on each line through a point need not be convex, or: a convex function need not be differentiable (§10).

## 01-convex-sets.md — Convex sets and hulls

## 02-caratheodory.md — Carathéodory's theorem
- `thm-caratheodory` (**major**): in ℝⁿ (or an n-dimensional space) every point of conv S is a convex combination of at most n+1 points of S. Proof: an affine dependence among too many points lets one be dropped. Sharp: the simplex.
- `cor-hull-of-compact-is-compact`: uses Carathéodory and (A3)/(A4). Name them.
- Radon's theorem and Helly's theorem (encyclopedic depth): state and prove Radon; prove Helly from Radon. Worked example in ℝ².
- **Warning:** the hull of a closed set need not be closed (the hull of a point and a line). Give it.

## 03-separation.md — Separating hyperplanes
- `thm-nearest-point` (**major**): a non-empty closed convex set C in a real inner product space of finite dimension has a unique nearest point P_C(x) to each x, characterized by ⟨x − P_C x, y − P_C x⟩ ≤ 0 for all y ∈ C. Existence by compactness (A3)/(A4) on C ∩ a ball; uniqueness by the parallelogram law. **Say explicitly that this generalizes `thm-best-approximation`**, where C is a subspace and the inequality becomes an equality.
- `thm-separation-point`: a point outside a closed convex set is strictly separated from it.
- `thm-separating-hyperplane`: two disjoint convex sets, one compact and one closed, are strictly separated; two disjoint convex sets are (weakly) separated. `thm-supporting-hyperplane` at boundary points.
- `thm-hahn-banach-finite`: the finite-dimensional Hahn–Banach extension theorem for a sublinear functional. Prove it (one dimension at a time is the classical route); say the infinite-dimensional version needs Zorn's lemma and is outside the book's scope, and point to Ch 1 §08, where Zorn's lemma is stated (`thm-zorn`) and used for `thm-every-space-has-basis`.
- **Negative result:** hyperbola region and asymptote — disjoint, closed, convex, not strictly separable.

## 04-gauges-support-functions-dual-norms.md — Gauges, support functions, dual norms
- `def-gauge` (Minkowski functional) of a convex set containing 0 in its interior; `thm-gauge-is-norm`: for a bounded, symmetric, closed convex body the gauge is a norm whose unit ball is the body. **This is the converse of Ch 15's "every unit ball is convex".**
- `def-support-function` h_K(y) = sup_{x∈K} ⟨x, y⟩; basic properties (sublinear, determines a closed convex K via separation: `thm-support-function-determines`).
- `def-polar-set`, and `thm-bipolar` for closed convex sets containing 0.
- `def-dual-norm` (**major**) ‖y‖_* = max_{‖x‖≤1} ⟨x,y⟩ = h of the unit ball; attained by (A4). `thm-dual-dual-norm`: ‖·‖_** = ‖·‖, via separation. Compute: dual of ‖·‖₁ is ‖·‖_∞ and vice versa, ‖·‖₂ is self-dual. General p (Hölder) is proved in §10.
- `cor-norm-as-max`: ‖x‖ = max_{‖y‖_*≤1} ⟨x,y⟩. Connect to the operator norm: ‖A‖ between two norms is a max of bilinear values.
- Record in the payoff that this fills Chapter 15's missing dual norms.

## 05-cones-and-farkas.md — Cones and Farkas's lemma
- `def-convex-cone`, `def-dual-cone`; finitely generated cones.
- `thm-conic-caratheodory`: points of a cone generated by m vectors in ℝⁿ are combinations of linearly independent generators.
- `thm-finitely-generated-cone-closed` (**the key lemma**): prove it via conic Carathéodory + the image of an orthant under an injective linear map is closed. This is the subtle step; do not wave at it.
- `thm-farkas` (**major**): exactly one of Ax = b, x ≥ 0 and Aᵀy ≥ 0, bᵀy < 0 has a solution. Proof by separation from the closed cone. Give the geometric reading. Variants (Gordan, the inequality form) as corollaries or exercises.
- `thm-dual-cone-of-dual-cone`: K** = K for closed convex cones.
- **Negative result:** a closed convex cone whose linear image is not closed (e.g. the Lorentz cone projected); show Farkas's alternative fails for it.

## 06-linear-programming-duality.md — Linear programming
- Standard and inequality forms; `thm-weak-duality`; `thm-strong-duality` (**major**) from Farkas, including the case analysis (both feasible ⇒ both optimal with equal values; one infeasible ⇒ the other infeasible or unbounded). `thm-complementary-slackness`.
- Existence of an optimum for a feasible bounded LP follows from strong duality — say so, since it is not obvious.
- A worked 2-variable LP drawn (TikZ) and solved, with its dual.
- Answer Ch 2 §07's traffic remark.
- **Warning:** duality gap cannot occur for LPs but does for general convex programs; give a pointer only.

## 07-minimax.md — The minimax theorem
- Matrix games, mixed strategies over the simplex Δ.
- `thm-von-neumann-minimax` (**major**): max_x min_y xᵀAy = min_y max_x xᵀAy over simplices. Proof from LP duality (§06), or from separation — pick one, mention the other.
- Contrast with Chapter 16's min–max (Courant–Fischer): there the inner and outer sets are subspaces and the equality is a dimension count; here they are simplices and the equality is convexity. **Say so explicitly** — a reader will conflate them.
- Worked example: rock–paper–scissors or a 2×2 game with a saddle-free matrix; value and optimal strategies verified.

## 08-extreme-points.md — Extreme points
- `def-extreme-point` (**major**), `def-face` (brief).
- `thm-minkowski-extreme` (**major**, finite-dimensional Krein–Milman): a compact convex set is the convex hull of its extreme points. Proof by induction on dimension through a supporting hyperplane (§03). Name (A3)/(A4) where used.
- Examples, each verified: unit balls of ‖·‖₁ (±eᵢ), ‖·‖_∞ (sign vectors), ‖·‖₂ (the sphere); density matrices {A ⪰ 0, tr A = 1} have extreme points exactly the rank-one xx* with ‖x‖ = 1 (prove it — spectral theorem); doubly stochastic matrices: the permutation matrices are extreme — prove that; that they are *all* the extreme points is Birkhoff's theorem, **Chapter 18**.
- `cor-linear-max-at-extreme`: a linear functional attains its max over a compact convex set at an extreme point. Tie to LP (§06) and to Ky Fan (the max of tr Q*AQ).
- **Negative result:** a compact convex set in ℝ³ whose set of extreme points is not closed.

## 09-polytopes.md — Polytopes
- `def-polytope` (hull of finitely many points), `def-polyhedron` (finite intersection of half-spaces).
- `thm-minkowski-weyl` (**major**): a bounded polyhedron is a polytope and a polytope is a bounded polyhedron; the conic version (finitely generated ⇔ polyhedral cone). Proof via Fourier–Motzkin elimination (elementary, give it as an algorithm with the `.algorithm` environment) or via polarity and §05. Pick one; the Fourier–Motzkin route should be shown on an example.
- Vertices of {x : Ax ≤ b} are exactly the basic feasible points (n linearly independent active constraints) — the link to §06. **Do not promise the simplex method to a later chapter: the plan's Chapter 23 does not list it** (verified). Describe it in a sentence if useful, without a forward pointer.
- Worked examples: the cube, the cross-polytope (dual), a triangle given both ways.

## 10-convex-functions.md — Convex functions
- `def-convex-function` (**major**) with the epigraph; strict convexity; Jensen's inequality (finite form) `thm-jensen`.
- `prp-sup-of-affine-convex`: a pointwise supremum of affine functions is convex — **pays Ch 16 §06's promise**, and cites `cor-ky-fan-subadditive` and λ₁ as instances.
- `thm-convex-second-derivative`: a C² function on an open convex set is convex iff its Hessian is PSD everywhere. Cite `def-hessian` and Ch 13 §06's Taylor assumption; one-variable case via (A6).
- `thm-convex-continuous`: a convex function on an open convex subset of ℝⁿ is continuous — the finite-dimensional proof (bounded on a simplex, then Lipschitz on smaller balls).
- Applications: AM–GM from concavity of log (a **second proof** of Ch 16's `lem-am-gm` — cite it, and note that §08 there proved it by pure algebra); Young's and **Hölder's inequality**, and with them the dual of ‖·‖_p is ‖·‖_q for all 1 < p < ∞ (completes §04).
- Convex functions of a Hermitian matrix: A ↦ λ₁(A) and A ↦ Σ_{i≤k}λ_i(A) are convex; A ↦ tr f(A) for convex f is a Chapter 20 statement — pointer only, and check STATUS for how Chapter 20 is described.
- **Negative results:** a convex function need not be differentiable (|x|); a function convex along every line through the origin need not be convex.
