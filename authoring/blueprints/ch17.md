# Blueprint: Chapter 17 — Variational Principles and Interlacing

**Directory:** `src/ch17-variational/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Variational Principles and Interlacing". **Part:** continues "Part V · Matrix Analysis" — add the chapter to `config/config.json` as a bare string (no `part` key; Chapter 16 opens the part).

**Purpose.** Hermitian matrices have real eigenvalues, and the book has ordered them \( \lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n \) since Chapter 13 without ever saying what the middle ones *are*. Chapter 12 got \( \lambda_1 \) and \( \lambda_n \) as the extreme values of \( \langle A x, x\rangle \) on the unit sphere and stopped, because the intermediate eigenvalues are not maxima of anything over the whole sphere. This chapter supplies the description that works — a max over a subspace, then a min over subspaces — and then spends the rest of its length collecting what that description buys:

- each eigenvalue as an optimization (Rayleigh, Courant–Fischer);
- how far eigenvalues move when the matrix moves (Weyl);
- how they move when a row and column are deleted, or a rank-one matrix is added (Cauchy interlacing);
- sums of eigenvalues as optimizations over subspaces (Poincaré, Ky Fan);
- the sharpening that handles arbitrary index sets (Wielandt, Lidskii);
- what the diagonal of a Hermitian matrix can be, given its spectrum (Schur);
- the same theory for singular values, which finishes a theorem Chapter 13 could not state;
- inertia, re-proved and then made additive;
- and a first look at which scalar functions respect the Loewner order.

**Corrections found while drafting** (the drafters checked the mathematics instead of complying; each was verified):

1. **§04, bordered-matrix strictness.** "Strict interlacing iff b has a nonzero component in every eigenspace of B" is **false** when B has a repeated eigenvalue: A = [[2,1,0],[1,2,1],[0,1,2]] with index 2 deleted gives B = 2I, b = (1,1) ≠ 0, and the interlacing reads 2 = 2 = 2. `thm-bordered-matrix` proves the correct statement: strict everywhere iff B has n−1 **distinct** eigenvalues and the border meets every eigenspace.
2. **§10, `cor-inertia-subspace-characterization`.** "And similarly for n₀" is **false**: diag(1,−1) has n₀ = 0, yet the form vanishes on the line through (1,1). The section states the correct companions (n₊ + n₀ as the largest dimension where the form is ≥ 0; n₀ = dim nul A).
3. **§10, Haynsworth over ℂ.** `thm-sylvester-inertia` (Ch 14 §05) is stated only for real symmetric forms, so citing it alone left the complex Hermitian case unproved. §10 proves congruence invariance for Hermitian matrices over ℝ or ℂ first.
4. **A promise this blueprint missed.** Ch 11 §10 (~line 476) sends the general **strict** interlacing of orthogonal-polynomial zeros to Chapter 17. Plain Cauchy interlacing does not give strictness; it needs correction 1's criterion. Being added to §04.
5. **The Purpose paragraph misdescribes Chapter 12.** Chapter 12 §02 *declined* the extreme-value route rather than using it; the extreme-value lemma is Ch 13 §05's `lem-extreme-eigenvalues-quadratic-form`.
6. **No analytic fact is *invoked* in §§01–02** (a referee then corrected the drafter's stronger "no analysis at all": the spectral theorem rests on the fundamental theorem of algebra, which Ch 6 §05 proves with the extreme value theorem). This blueprint predicted compactness and the extreme value theorem would be used "repeatedly". Courant–Fischer needs neither: the max of R_A over a subspace W is λ₁ of the compression Q*AQ, which the spectral theorem names. The imported-analysis list is (A1)–(A6), not (A1)–(A4).
7. **§08, "majorization is a preorder, not a total order (see correction 7)"** is wrong: it is only a **preorder** on ℝⁿ, since (1,0) ≺ (0,1) ≺ (1,0). Antisymmetry holds up to rearrangement.
8. **§08, deferring Horn's converse because it "needs Birkhoff"** was wrong twice over. Birkhoff gives doubly stochastic matrices, but Horn's theorem needs an orthostochastic one; and there is an elementary proof by induction from the 2×2 rotation. The approved outline lists Schur–Horn under Chapter 17, so it is proved there.
9. **§05, rank-one interlacing does not follow from `cor-weyl-rank-bound` alone.** That gives only the outer bounds; the half λᵢ(A+τvv*) ≥ λᵢ(A) needs τ ≥ 0 and was proved from the max–min form.
10. **§07, "Weyl's inequalities and Ky Fan's are both special cases" of Lidskii** is only partly right. Lidskii gives the Weyl inequalities with one index at an end, and — applied to negated pairs — those with i + j − 1 = n; not the rest. **The first witness offered for this, the n = 3 triple (1,0,0), (1,0,0), (1,½,½), was wrong** (it was also copied into this list unchecked): Lidskii applied to −X and Y rules it out. A correct witness needs n = 4: (2,0,0,−1), (2,0,0,0), (2,1,1,−1) satisfies every Lidskii inequality over all twelve signed pairs and breaks λ₃(X+Y) ≤ λ₂(X)+λ₂(Y). Verified independently.

## 01-the-rayleigh-quotient.md — The Rayleigh quotient

- **Hook:** \( \langle A x, x\rangle \) has been a *test* for definiteness since Chapter 13 §12. Make it the object.
- **Definition** `def-rayleigh-quotient` (**major**): \( R_A(x) = \langle Ax, x\rangle / \langle x, x\rangle \) for \( x \ne 0 \), \( A \) Hermitian. Bold **Hermitian** and **\( x \ne 0 \)**. Clause paraphrase: it is real (say why, by `thm-self-adjoint-real-eigenvalues` or the direct computation), it is scale-invariant, so it is a function on lines, hence on the unit sphere.
- Examples: a diagonal matrix, where \( R_A \) is a weighted average of the diagonal entries with weights \( |x_i|^2 \) — this is the whole chapter in miniature and should be stated as such; a \( 2 \times 2 \) rotation-free symmetric example with a picture; \( A = I \) (degenerate: \( R_A \equiv 1 \)); a projection, where \( R_A(x) = \norm{Px}^2/\norm{x}^2 \in [0,1]\).
- **Proposition** `prp-rayleigh-basic`: the range of \( R_A \) is exactly \( [\lambda_n, \lambda_1] \) — restate `lem-extreme-eigenvalues-quadratic-form` in quotient form and **cite it, do not re-prove it**; add that the range is the whole closed interval (connectedness, or explicitly along a path of unit vectors — prefer the explicit path, so no new analysis is imported).
- **Proposition** `prp-rayleigh-critical-points`: the critical points of \( R_A \) on the unit sphere are exactly the unit eigenvectors, and the critical values are the eigenvalues. Keep this short and honest about the calculus it uses; if the derivative argument would need a fact outside Chapter 16's list, give the finite-dimensional computation \( R_A(x + tv) \) expanded in \( t \) instead.
- **Warning** (the chapter's central misconception): \( \lambda_2 \) is **not** \( \max\{R_A(x) : x \perp \text{nothing}\} \) and not the second-largest value of \( R_A \) in any sense — the range is an interval, so there is no "second largest value". Give a concrete \( 3\times 3 \) diagonal matrix and show the reader what goes wrong.
- **Proposition** `prp-rayleigh-deflation`: \( \lambda_2 = \max\{R_A(x) : x \perp u_1\} \) where \( u_1 \) is a top unit eigenvector, and inductively \( \lambda_k = \max\{R_A(x): x \perp u_1,\dots,u_{k-1}\} \). Prove it; then say clearly what is unsatisfying about it — **it names an eigenbasis, so it describes the eigenvalues in terms of themselves**, and is useless for comparing two different matrices. That dissatisfaction is the hook for §02.
- Pay off Chapter 13 §12's sentence here, by label.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C. (B: compute \( R_A \) at given vectors and locate it in \( [\lambda_n,\lambda_1] \); find a vector with a prescribed quotient. C: show \( R_A(x) \) is within \( O(\varepsilon^2) \) of \( \lambda_1 \) when \( x \) is within \( \varepsilon \) of \( u_1 \) — the reason the Rayleigh quotient is used to *estimate* eigenvalues, and a forward pointer to Chapter 24's Rayleigh quotient iteration.)

## 02-courant-fischer.md — The min–max theorem

- **Lemma** `lem-subspace-intersection` (**the workhorse of the chapter**): if \( U, W \le F^n \) and \( \dim U + \dim W > n \) then \( U \cap W \ne \{0\} \). One line from the dimension formula; give it a label because five later proofs cite it.
- **Theorem** `thm-courant-fischer` (**major**): for Hermitian \( A \in M_n \) and \( 1 \le k \le n \),
  \[ \lambda_k(A) = \min_{\dim W = n-k+1} \ \max_{0 \ne x \in W} R_A(x) = \max_{\dim W = k}\ \min_{0 \ne x \in W} R_A(x). \]
  **Idea block:** the max–min form says "\( \lambda_k \) is the best guaranteed value you can obtain on a \( k \)-dimensional subspace"; the witness is the span of the top \( k \) eigenvectors, and no other \( k \)-dimensional subspace does better because it must meet the span of the bottom \( n-k+1 \) eigenvectors, by `lem-subspace-intersection`.
  **Proof:** both halves, both directions, with the inner max/min attained by compactness (name the imported fact). Do **not** prove only one half and say the other is similar — write both, or prove one and derive the other by applying it to \( -A \), which is a genuine argument and should be presented as one.
- **Remark:** the increasing-index form, once, for readers who meet it elsewhere.
- **Corollary** `cor-loewner-eigenvalue-monotone`: \( A \succeq B \) implies \( \lambda_i(A) \ge \lambda_i(B) \) for every \( i \). **This is exactly what Chapter 13 §05 deferred** — discharge it by label and in its words.
- **Corollary** `cor-courant-fischer-operator`: the basis-free statement for a self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional inner product space, which is what Chapter 12 §02 promised.
- **Warning:** the order of \( \min \) and \( \max \) matters, and the subspace dimension in each form is different; swapping either gives a false statement. Give the false statement and the \( 2\times 2 \) counterexample.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C. (C: the generalized eigenvalue problem — with `def-generalized-eigenvalue` and `thm-simultaneous-congruence` from Ch 13 §04, state and prove the min–max for \( \lambda(A, B) \) with \( B \succ 0 \).)

## 03-weyl-inequalities.md — How far eigenvalues move

- **Theorem** `thm-weyl-inequalities` (**major**): for Hermitian \( A, B \),
  \( \lambda_{i+j-1}(A+B) \le \lambda_i(A) + \lambda_j(B) \) whenever \( i + j - 1 \le n \), and the dual family with \( \ge \) and \( \lambda_{i+j-n} \). Prove from Courant–Fischer with `lem-subspace-intersection`. State the dual family properly rather than waving at it; deriving it by applying the first to \( -A, -B \) is the clean route and should be written out.
- **Corollary** `cor-weyl-monotone`: \( \lambda_i(A) + \lambda_n(B) \le \lambda_i(A+B) \le \lambda_i(A) + \lambda_1(B) \).
- **Corollary** `cor-weyl-perturbation` (**the promise from Ch 16 §07**): \( |\lambda_i(A+E) - \lambda_i(A)| \le \norm{E}_2 \) for Hermitian \( E \), every \( i \). Discharge Chapter 16 §07's sentence by label and in its words, and say plainly what it adds to Chapter 16's continuity result: continuity becomes a **Lipschitz constant of 1**, with no dependence on \( n \), on the conditioning, or on the gaps between eigenvalues.
- **Corollary** `cor-hermitian-eigenvalues-well-conditioned`: the Hermitian eigenvalue problem is perfectly conditioned in the sense of Chapter 16 §08 — connect to `def-condition-number` explicitly, and contrast with the Jordan-block example of Chapter 16 §07 by label. **This is a negative result about the non-Hermitian case and should be flagged as such.**
- **Corollary** `cor-weyl-rank-bound`: if \( \rank B \le r \) then \( \lambda_{i+r}(A+B) \le \lambda_i(A) \le \lambda_{i-r}(A+B) \). This is the bridge to §05 and to §09's Eckart–Young.
- **Warning:** Weyl needs \( B \) Hermitian, not merely small; and the inequalities relate the eigenvalues **in order**, not "each eigenvalue to its nearest neighbour".
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 04-cauchy-interlacing.md — Deleting a row and a column

- **Hook:** a Hermitian matrix and its \( (n-1)\times(n-1) \) corner. The spectra cannot be independent; how are they tied?
- **Theorem** `thm-cauchy-interlacing` (**major**): if \( B \) is the principal submatrix of Hermitian \( A \) on an index set of size \( n-1 \), then \( \lambda_1(A) \ge \lambda_1(B) \ge \lambda_2(A) \ge \dots \ge \lambda_{n-1}(B) \ge \lambda_n(A) \). Prove from Courant–Fischer by restricting to subspaces of the coordinate hyperplane; the dimension count is again `lem-subspace-intersection`.
- **Corollary** `cor-interlacing-general-submatrix`: deleting \( m \) rows and the same \( m \) columns gives \( \lambda_i(A) \ge \lambda_i(B) \ge \lambda_{i+m}(A) \). Induction on \( m \).
- **Theorem** `thm-bordered-matrix`: the bordered form \( \begin{psmallmatrix} B & b \\ b^{*} & c\end{psmallmatrix} \), with the interlacing read in the other direction, and the strictness criterion (strict interlacing iff \( b \) has a nonzero component in every eigenspace of \( B \) — state it carefully, it is the part most books get vague about, and prove it or scope it explicitly).
- **Example:** a \( 3\times 3 \) integer symmetric matrix and all three of its \( 2\times 2 \) principal submatrices, with every eigenvalue computed exactly and the interlacing exhibited. **Every number checked.**
- **Application** `cor-interlacing-inertia`: a principal submatrix of a PSD matrix is PSD — already known from Ch 13 §01(e), so present it as a second proof and say so — and the sharper statement that the number of positive eigenvalues can drop by at most \( m \).
- **Negative result:** interlacing fails for non-Hermitian \( A \). Give a \( 2 \times 2 \) or \( 3\times 3 \) witness where the submatrix eigenvalue lies outside the spectrum's convex hull.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 05-rank-one-updates.md — Adding a rank-one matrix

- **Theorem** `thm-rank-one-interlacing`: for Hermitian \( A \) and \( \tau > 0 \), the eigenvalues of \( A + \tau vv^{*} \) interlace those of \( A \) from above: \( \lambda_i(A + \tau v v^{*}) \ge \lambda_i(A) \ge \lambda_{i+1}(A + \tau vv^{*}) \). Two proofs are available — from `cor-weyl-rank-bound`, and from Cauchy interlacing applied to a bordered matrix. **Give the short one and mention the other.**
- **Theorem** `thm-secular-equation`: with \( A = \diag(d_1 > \dots > d_n) \) and \( v \) having all entries nonzero, the eigenvalues of \( A + \tau vv^{*} \) are the roots of \( 1 + \tau \sum_i |v_i|^2/(d_i - \lambda) = 0 \), one strictly between each consecutive pair. Derive it from the determinant of a rank-one update, \( \det(A + \tau vv^{*}) = \det(A)\,(1 + \tau v^{*}A^{-1}v) \), which is `thm-schur-determinant` (Ch 8 §03) applied to a \( 1\times 1 \) block; `cor-sherman-morrison` in the same section is its inverse-formula companion and is worth citing alongside. **Draw the picture:** the graph of the secular function with its vertical asymptotes, one TikZ figure. This is the most illuminating figure in the chapter.
- **Worked example:** a \( 3 \times 3 \) diagonal matrix plus an explicit rank-one term, all eigenvalues found from the secular equation and confirmed independently.
- **Corollary** `cor-eigenvalue-shift-total`: \( \sum_i (\lambda_i(A + \tau vv^{*}) - \lambda_i(A)) = \tau\norm{v}^2 \) by the trace. A satisfying "the shifts add up" statement.
- **Forward pointer:** the divide-and-conquer eigenvalue algorithm of Chapter 24 is built on the secular equation; the power method's deflation is the same idea.
- **Warning:** for \( \tau < 0 \) the interlacing reverses; and the "all entries nonzero, distinct \( d_i \)" hypothesis in `thm-secular-equation` is not decoration — show what a zero entry does.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 06-poincare-and-ky-fan.md — Subspaces and sums

- **Theorem** `thm-poincare-separation`: if \( \{u_1,\dots,u_k\} \) is orthonormal and \( B = [\langle A u_j, u_i\rangle] \) is the compression of \( A \) to \( W = \Span\{u_i\} \), then \( \lambda_i(A) \ge \lambda_i(B) \ge \lambda_{i + n - k}(A) \). This is Cauchy interlacing freed from coordinates; prove it from Courant–Fischer directly, and note that `thm-cauchy-interlacing` is the case where \( W \) is a coordinate subspace.
- **Theorem** `thm-ky-fan` (**major**): \( \sum_{i=1}^k \lambda_i(A) = \max\{ \sum_{i=1}^k \langle A q_i, q_i\rangle : q_1,\dots,q_k \text{ orthonormal}\} = \max\{\tr(Q^{*}AQ) : Q^{*}Q = I_k\} \).
  **This is the family that Chapter 13 §10's `lem-orthonormal-capture-bound` was advertised as the extreme case of** — read that lemma's surrounding paragraph, discharge the promise by label, and show explicitly that \( k = 1 \) with \( A = A^{*}A \) recovers it.
- **Corollary** `cor-ky-fan-subadditive`: \( \sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i(A+B) \le \sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i(A) + \sum_{i \le k}\lambda_i(B) \) — the sum of the top \( k \) eigenvalues is a **convex** function of the matrix, and in particular sublinear. Say the word convex and point at Chapter 18.
- **Corollary** `cor-trace-min`: the bottom-\( k \) version, by \( A \mapsto -A \).
- **Warning:** \( \lambda_k \) itself is neither convex nor concave in \( A \); only the *sums* are. Give the witness.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C. (C: deduce `cor-fischer-inequality`-adjacent statements, or the trace form of the best-fitting-subspace corollary `cor-best-fitting-subspace`.)

## 07-wielandt-and-lidskii.md — Arbitrary index sets

- **Theorem** `thm-wielandt-minimax`: for any \( 1 \le i_1 < \dots < i_k \le n \),
  \( \sum_{j} \lambda_{i_j}(A) = \max \min \{ \sum_j R_A(x_j) \} \) over the appropriate flag of subspaces — **state it precisely with the flag \( W_1 \subset \dots \subset W_k \), \( \dim W_j = i_j \)**, and prove it. This is the hardest proof in the chapter; give it a Step roadmap and Claims, in the manner of the Jordan form proof.
- **Theorem** `thm-lidskii-inequality`: \( \sum_j \lambda_{i_j}(A) - \sum_j \lambda_{i_j}(B) \le \sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j(A - B) \), for Hermitian \( A, B \) and any index set. Deduce from Wielandt.
- **Remark:** the majorization form, \( \lambda(A) - \lambda(B) \prec \lambda(A-B) \), is what this becomes once majorization is in hand; §08 introduces majorization and Chapter 21 proves the strongest form. **Be precise about which form is proved here and which is deferred** — this is exactly the kind of place where a vague pointer becomes a debt.
- **Corollary:** Weyl's inequalities and Ky Fan's are both special cases; say which index sets give which, so the reader sees the hierarchy.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.
- **If the Wielandt proof does not come out clean, say so in your report rather than papering over a gap.** A correct, fully proved weaker statement plus an honest note is worth more than a hand-waved strong one.

## 08-majorization-and-schur.md — The diagonal and the spectrum

- **Hook:** a Hermitian matrix's diagonal entries and its eigenvalues have the same sum. What else is forced?
- **Definition** `def-majorization` (**major**): \( x \prec y \) for real vectors, via the decreasing rearrangements, partial sums, and equal totals. Bold every clause. `x \prec_w y` for weak majorization. Use the registered NOTATION rows.
- Examples: \( (1,1,1) \prec (2,1,0) \prec (3,0,0) \); the extremes \( (\bar a, \dots, \bar a) \prec x \prec (\sum x_i, 0, \dots, 0) \) for nonnegative \( x \); a non-example, a pair that is incomparable — **majorization is a preorder, not a total order** (see correction 7), and the reader must see an incomparable pair.
- **Proposition** `prp-majorization-basic`: \( \prec \) is reflexive and transitive, and \( x \prec y \prec x \) implies \( y \) is a rearrangement of \( x \).
- **Theorem** `thm-schur-majorization` (**major**): \( \diag(A) \prec \lambda(A) \) for Hermitian \( A \). Prove it — the partial-sum inequality is Ky Fan applied to the coordinate vectors, and the equality of totals is the trace.
- **The converse (Horn).** *Superseded by correction 8:* Horn's theorem is proved in §08 (`lem-horn-two-by-two`, `thm-horn`, `thm-schur-horn`), by induction from a 2×2 rotation, as the approved outline requires.
- **Corollary** `cor-hadamard-from-majorization`: a second proof of `thm-hadamard-inequality` (Ch 13 §06) for PSD matrices, via majorization and the AM–GM inequality. Present it as a second proof and cite the first.
- **Warning:** \( x \prec y \) does **not** mean \( x_i \le y_i \). Give the witness.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 09-singular-values-variationally.md — The same theory for singular values

- **Theorem** `thm-singular-value-minmax`: \( \sigma_k(A) = \min_{\dim W = n-k+1}\max_{0\ne x\in W} \norm{Ax}/\norm{x} = \max_{\dim W = k}\min\ \dots \). Two routes: apply Courant–Fischer to \( A^{*}A \), or to the Hermitian dilation. **Use \( A^{*}A \) for the main proof** (shorter, and \( \sigma_i(A)^2 = \lambda_i(A^{*}A) \) is already in Chapter 13), and give the dilation as a remark, since §10 and Chapter 20 will want it.
- **Theorem** `thm-weyl-singular-values`: \( \sigma_{i+j-1}(A+B) \le \sigma_i(A) + \sigma_j(B) \); in particular \( |\sigma_i(A+E) - \sigma_i(A)| \le \norm{E}_2 \). Singular values are as well conditioned as Hermitian eigenvalues, **and this holds with no hypothesis on \( E \) at all** — a genuinely striking fact next to Chapter 16 §07's Jordan block. Make the contrast explicit.
- **Theorem** `thm-eckart-young-spectral`: \( \min\{\norm{A - B}_2 : \rank B \le k\} = \sigma_{k+1}(A) \), attained at the truncated SVD. **This finishes the theorem Chapter 13 §10 could only state in the Frobenius norm.** Chapter 16 §08's closing ledger currently sends this to Chapter 21; **that pointer must be corrected to Chapter 17 when this chapter ships** (Chapter 21 keeps the general unitarily invariant version). Flag the correction in your report.
- **Corollary** `cor-distance-to-singular`: the distance from an invertible \( A \) to the nearest singular matrix is \( \sigma_n(A) = 1/\norm{A^{-1}}_2 \), so \( \kappa_2(A) = \norm{A}_2/\sigma_n(A) \) is the reciprocal of the *relative* distance to singularity. **This is the geometric meaning of Chapter 16's condition number** and is the single best payoff in the chapter; give it room, and cite `def-condition-number`.
- **Theorem** `thm-singular-value-interlacing`: deleting a row (or a column) from \( A \) interlaces the singular values.

## 10-inertia-revisited.md — Inertia, again and additively

- **Theorem** `thm-inertia-by-interlacing`: a second proof of Sylvester's law of inertia (`thm-sylvester-inertia`) from Courant–Fischer — the number of positive eigenvalues is the largest dimension of a subspace on which the form is positive definite, a description that mentions no basis. **Present it as a second proof, cite the first, and say what each proof gives that the other does not.**
- **Corollary** `cor-inertia-subspace-characterization`: \( n_+(A) = \max\{\dim W : \langle Ax,x\rangle > 0 \text{ for all } 0 \ne x \in W\} \), and similarly for \( n_- \) and \( n_0 \).
- **Theorem** `thm-inertia-congruence-interlacing`: under a congruence by an arbitrary invertible matrix, eigenvalues need not interlace, but the inertia is fixed; under a *rank-\( r \)* Hermitian perturbation, each of \( n_+ \) and \( n_- \) changes by at most \( r \). Prove from `cor-weyl-rank-bound`.
- **Theorem** `thm-haynsworth`: inertia additivity for a block Hermitian matrix — the triple \( (n_+, n_-, n_0) \) of \( A \) is the sum, entry by entry, of the triples of \( A_{11} \) and of the Schur complement \( A/A_{11} \), for \( A_{11} \) invertible. **Chapter 14 §05 writes the inertia as the three counts \( n_+, n_-, n_0 \) and has no symbol for the triple**; if you want one, define it here and register it in NOTATION.md. Prove it by exhibiting the congruence (the block elimination Ch 13 §01 already uses) and applying `thm-sylvester-inertia`. **This is the cleanest illustration in the book that a Schur complement is a congruence in disguise.**
- **Corollary:** a second proof of `thm-block-psd-schur` (Ch 13 §06), by inertia.

## 11-monotone-matrix-functions.md — Which functions respect the order

- **Hook:** Chapter 13 §05 proved \( A \succeq B \succeq 0 \Rightarrow A^{1/2}\succeq B^{1/2} \) (`prp-square-root-monotone`) and showed that squaring fails. Why should the square root behave and the square not?
- **Definition** `def-operator-monotone` (**major**): \( f \) defined on an interval \( I \) is **operator monotone** if \( A \succeq B \), with spectra in \( I \), implies \( f(A) \succeq f(B) \), **for every size \( n \)** — bold the "every \( n \)", it is the clause that makes the notion hard and interesting. Define **operator convex** alongside.
- The functional calculus for Hermitian matrices is already available: `def-function-of-normal-operator`, `thm-functional-calculus-properties` (Ch 12 §08). Use it; do not rebuild it.
- **Examples proved here:** \( t \mapsto \alpha + \beta t \) with \( \beta \ge 0 \); \( t \mapsto -1/t \) on \( (0,\infty) \) (prove it — the clean proof is a congruence, and it is `thm-loewner-basic` (d) restated); \( t\mapsto t^{1/2} \) (cite Ch 13, do not re-prove); and the consequence \( \lambda_i(A)\ge\lambda_i(B) \) from `cor-loewner-eigenvalue-monotone`, which is "operator monotonicity at the level of eigenvalues" and holds for every increasing \( f \).
- **Non-example:** \( t\mapsto t^2 \). **Chapter 13 §05 already has the witness under "What fails: squaring" — read it and cite it.** Add the observation that \( t \mapsto t^2 \) *is* operator convex, so the two notions genuinely differ.
- **What is not proved here.** Loewner's theorem — that the operator monotone functions on \( (0,\infty) \) are exactly the ones with an integral representation, equivalently those extending analytically to the upper half-plane — is Chapter 21's, and so is \( t^p \) for \( 0 < p < 1 \). State the classification, say it is not proved here, and record the promise.
- **Closing "Summary and transfer"** for the whole chapter: the named moves (the dimension count; min–max; compress and interlace; perturb in order; majorize), and a table of which theorem answers which earlier promise.
