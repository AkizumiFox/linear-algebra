# Blueprint: Chapter 9 — Eigenvalues and Diagonalization

**Directory:** `src/ch09-eigenvalues/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Eigenvalues and Diagonalization".

**Purpose.** Answer the question Chapter 4 raised: how simple can the matrix of an operator be made by choosing a basis? The chapter covers:
- invariant subspaces;
- eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and multiplicities;
- the diagonalization theorem;
- the minimal polynomial and Cayley–Hamilton;
- triangularization;
- primary decomposition, giving diagonalizable ⇔ minimal polynomial with distinct linear factors;
- commuting families;
- the spectral mapping theorem, AB vs BA, left eigenvectors;
- first applications to powers, recurrences and Markov chains.

**Signature moves:**
- **When is [T] diagonal?** It forces Tv = λv.
- **λ is an eigenvalue ⇔ T − λI is not injective:** "big fan of kernel".
- **Distinct eigenvalues ⇒ independent:** induction plus applying T − λ_k I.
- **Polynomial relation ⇒ annihilating polynomial ⇒ m_T divides it.**
- **Take one eigenvector, extend to a basis, recurse** (triangularization).
- **Search for a common eigenvector inside an eigenspace** (commuting operators).
- **Coprime factors split the space** (primary decomposition).

## 01-invariant-subspaces.md — Invariant subspaces

- **Hook:** Ch 8 showed that T mapping U into U makes the matrix block triangular (recall `thm-invariant-subspace-block-triangular`). Name that property.
- **Definition** `def-invariant-subspace` (**major notion**).
  - **Examples:** {0}, V, ker T, im T; ker p(T) and im p(T) (recall Ch 6 `thm-kernel-image-of-polynomial-invariant`); span of a vector v with Tv ∈ span(v); for D on polynomials, the degree-≤k polynomials; for rotation of ℝ², only {0} and ℝ² (proof).
  - **Non-example:** a line not mapped to itself.
- **Restriction** `def-restriction-operator` T|_U; **induced operator** on V/U (recall Ch 4 §04 exercise and Ch 8).
  - `thm-invariant-subspace-matrix` restates the block triangular form in this language.
  - `thm-direct-sum-invariant-block-diagonal`.
- **Sums and intersections** of invariant subspaces are invariant.
- **Warning:** the complement of an invariant subspace need not be invariant ([[0,1],[0,0]], span(e₁)).

## 02-eigenvalues-and-eigenvectors.md — Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

**Keep label** `def-eigenvalue` (operator-first; matrices via T_A).

- **Hook:** "When is [T]_𝓑 diagonal?" Unwind the columns: it forces Tv_j = λ_j v_j.
- **Definition** (**major**): eigenvalue, eigenvector (**non-zero**), eigenspace E_λ(T) — `def-eigenspace`.
  - **Bold:** v ≠ 0 but λ may be 0.
- **Characterizations** — `thm-eigenvalue-characterizations`: for finite-dimensional V, λ is an eigenvalue ⇔ T − λI is not injective ⇔ not invertible ⇔ det(λI − T) = 0 ⇔ p_T(λ) = 0. Cite Ch 7 `thm-charpoly-root-iff-singular` and Ch 2 kernel test and TFAE.
- **Spectrum** `def-spectrum`: spec(T).
- **Examples:**
  - diagonal and triangular matrices;
  - a 2×2 with two eigenvalues, worked in full;
  - rotation by 90°: no real eigenvalues, but ±i over ℂ;
  - D on ℝ[x]≤n: only 0;
  - D on smooth functions: every λ, with e^{λx} (infinite dimension, no characteristic polynomial);
  - the shift R on F^ℕ: no eigenvalues;
  - projections (0 and 1); involutions (±1).
- **Distinct eigenvalues ⇒ independent eigenvectors** — `thm-distinct-eigenvalues-independent`, with the Idea and a proof by contradiction via a minimal dependence. Consequence: at most dim V eigenvalues; eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues form a direct sum (`cor-eigenspaces-direct-sum`, using Ch 1 `thm-direct-sum-k-criteria`).
- **Existence over ℂ** — `thm-complex-operator-has-eigenvalue`: every operator on a non-zero finite-dimensional complex space has an eigenvalue.
  - Proof 1: FTA applied to p_T.
  - "Alternatively": a determinant-free proof via an annihilating polynomial (Ch 6 `thm-annihilating-polynomial-exists`), factored into linear factors, one of which is not injective.
  - Pays off Ch 6's promise.
- **Real matrices** — `thm-real-matrix-complex-eigenvalues`: complex eigenvalues of real matrices come in conjugate pairs, with conjugate eigenvectors. Pays off Ch 1 §09 (complexification: say A acts on ℂⁿ = (ℝⁿ)_ℂ).
- **The TFAE grows** — `thm-invertible-tfae-eigen`: A is invertible ⇔ 0 ∉ spec(A).
- **Warnings:**
  - eigenvalues of A + B are not sums; of AB are not products;
  - row operations change eigenvalues;
  - "an eigenvector", not "the eigenvector".
- **Interactive:** a widget (existing `widgets/linear-map.js`) showing vectors whose images stay on their line, with a TikZ print fallback. Check the widget's options in its source.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** eigenvalues and eigenspaces of 2×2 and 3×3 matrices with integer data; eigenvalues of the transpose map on M₂ (±1, with eigenspaces Sym and Skew); show λ² is an eigenvalue of T².
  - **C:** if every non-zero vector is an eigenvector, T is scalar; eigenvalues of a nilpotent operator; a real 3×3 matrix has a real eigenvalue (odd degree, Ch 6).

## 03-multiplicities.md — Algebraic and geometric multiplicity

- **Definitions** `def-algebraic-multiplicity` (the multiplicity of λ as a root of p_T, recalling Ch 6 `def-root-multiplicity`) and `def-geometric-multiplicity` (dim E_λ).
- **Theorem** `thm-geometric-le-algebraic`: 1 ≤ g(λ) ≤ a(λ). Proof: take a basis of E_λ, extend it, compute p_T by block triangular determinant (Ch 7 `thm-det-block-triangular`); (x − λ)^g divides p_T.
- **Examples:**
  - [[λ, 1], [0, λ]] with g = 1, a = 2;
  - λI with g = a = 2;
  - a 3×3 with a mixed pattern.
- **Sum of algebraic multiplicities** = n when p_T splits; tr and det as sums and products of eigenvalues with multiplicity when p splits (via the coefficients, Ch 7 `thm-charpoly-coefficients`) — `thm-trace-det-eigenvalues`.
- **Warning:** a repeated eigenvalue does not imply non-diagonalizable (I), and a matrix with the same eigenvalues need not be similar.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute a and g for listed matrices; find t such that a matrix has g < a; tr and det from eigenvalues.
  - **C:** for a nilpotent n×n A, g(0) = n − rank A; build a 4×4 matrix with prescribed (a, g) patterns.

## 04-diagonalization.md — Diagonalization

- **Definition** `def-diagonalizable` (operator and matrix).
- **Diagonalization Theorem** — `thm-diagonalization`. For T on finite-dimensional V, TFAE:
  - (a) T is diagonalizable;
  - (b) V has a basis of eigenvectors;
  - (c) V = ⊕ E_λ;
  - (d) Σ dim E_λ = dim V;
  - (e) p_T splits and g(λ) = a(λ) for every λ.

  Full proof cycle. Pays off Ch 1 §07's promise.
- **Corollary** `cor-distinct-eigenvalues-diagonalizable`: n distinct eigenvalues ⇒ diagonalizable. **Warning:** the converse fails.
- **Computing P and D** — `exm-diagonalize-3x3`, fully worked (verify with sympy); A = PDP⁻¹, with the column order explained.
- **Powers and polynomials:** A^k = PD^kP⁻¹ and p(A) = P p(D) P⁻¹ — `thm-powers-diagonalizable`. **Worked:** Fibonacci closed form (Binet) via a 2×2 matrix.
- **Non-diagonalizable examples:** [[1,1],[0,1]] over any field; rotation over ℝ (diagonalizable over ℂ).
- **Warning:** diagonalizability depends on the field.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** diagonalize listed matrices or show impossible; compute A¹⁰; decide diagonalizability of a matrix with a parameter.
  - **C:** a diagonalizable A with A^k = I over ℂ, and the eigenvalues are roots of unity; if A is diagonalizable, so is p(A); the transpose map on M_n is diagonalizable (char ≠ 2).

## 05-minimal-polynomial.md — The minimal polynomial

- **Hook:** among all polynomials killing T, one divides all others. Recall Ch 6 `thm-annihilator-ideal`.
- **Definition** `def-minimal-polynomial` (**major**): the monic generator m_T of I_T. Pays off the Ch 6 promise. **Worked:** m for diag(1, 1, 2), for [[1,1],[0,1]], for a projection, for D on ℝ[x]≤n.
- **Theorem** `thm-minimal-polynomial-divides`: p(T) = 0 ⇔ m_T | p.
- **Roots** — `thm-minimal-polynomial-roots`: the roots of m_T in F are exactly the eigenvalues of T. Proof: (⇒) apply m_T(T) to an eigenvector; (⇐) write m_T = (x − λ)q with q(T) ≠ 0.
- **Computing m_T:** by finding the first linear dependence among I, A, A², … (Ch 3 toolkit, `exm-minimal-polynomial-by-dependence`), and via the eigenvalues plus trial exponents.
- **Similar matrices** have the same minimal polynomial — `thm-minimal-polynomial-similarity`; the minimal polynomial of a block diagonal matrix is the lcm (Ch 8).
- **Warning:** m_T and p_T can differ ([I₂: x − 1 vs (x − 1)²]); the "fake proof" of Cayley–Hamilton was flagged in Ch 7 — do not use it.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** minimal polynomials of listed matrices; show T² = T ⇒ m_T ∈ {x, x − 1, x(x − 1)}; the minimal polynomial of a companion matrix is p (Ch 7 exercise).
  - **C:** deg m_T = dim V ⇔ there is a vector v with (v, Tv, …) a basis (cyclic vector, the ⇐ direction only; ⇒ is in Ch 10); m_{T|_U} divides m_T for invariant U.

## 06-cayley-hamilton.md — The Cayley–Hamilton Theorem

- **Goal:** p_T(T) = 0.
- **Proof:** via the adjugate identity over F[x].
  - (xI − A)·adj(xI − A) = p_A(x)·I, cite Ch 7 `thm-adjugate-identity` and its commutative-ring remark.
  - Write adj(xI − A) = Σ B_k x^k with matrix coefficients.
  - Compare coefficients, then telescope after multiplying by A^k.
  - Idea block with the 2×2 case. Pays off Ch 7's promise.
- **Warning (explicit):** why "substitute x = A into det(xI − A)" is not a proof. Recall the Ch 7 warning.
- **Consequences:**
  - `cor-minimal-divides-characteristic`: m_T | p_T, deg m_T ≤ n;
  - same roots with a(λ) ≥ multiplicity in m_T ≥ 1;
  - A⁻¹ is a polynomial in A — `cor-inverse-polynomial`;
  - Aⁿ is a combination of I, …, A^{n−1}.
- **Alternatively:** a second proof via triangularization over ℂ, given in §07 as an exercise with a pointer.
- **Worked:** verify for a 3×3; compute A⁻¹ and A⁵ using p_A. Verify with sympy.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** use C–H to compute A⁻¹ and A¹⁰⁰ for a 2×2 A; show a 2×2 A with tr A = 0 has A² scalar; find m_A given p_A and one extra check.
  - **C:** Cayley–Hamilton over any commutative ring (the same proof); if A is nilpotent n×n then Aⁿ = 0.

## 07-triangularization.md — Triangularization

- **Upper triangular ⇔ flag of invariant subspaces** — `thm-triangular-iff-flag` (recall Ch 4 §01 exercise and pay it off).
- **Triangularization Theorem** — `thm-triangularization`: T is triangularizable over F ⇔ p_T splits over F. Proof by induction via an eigenvector and the induced operator on V/span(v) (Ch 4 quotient, Ch 8 block form) — "take one eigenvector, extend, recurse".
- `cor-complex-triangularizable`: every complex matrix is similar to an upper triangular matrix.
- **Consequences:**
  - the diagonal entries of any triangular form are the eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity — `thm-diagonal-of-triangular-form`;
  - tr = sum, det = product (again, now over any splitting field).
  - Second proof of Cayley–Hamilton for split p_T: `exr-triangularization-c1` scaffolds (T − λ₁I)⋯(T − λ_nI) = 0 using the flag.
- **Worked:** triangularize a 3×3 with a repeated eigenvalue and a single eigenvector — `exm-triangularize-3x3`, verified.
- **Warning:** a triangular form is far from unique; the flag depends on choices. Real matrices may need ℂ.
- **Forward:** Schur's unitary triangularization in Ch 12.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** triangularize listed matrices; show a nilpotent matrix is similar to a strictly upper triangular one; eigenvalues of A² from a triangular form.
  - **C:** the second Cayley–Hamilton proof (scaffolded); over ℝ, every operator has an invariant subspace of dimension 1 or 2.

## 08-primary-decomposition.md — Primary decomposition

- **Theorem** `thm-primary-decomposition`: if m_T = p₁^{e₁}⋯p_k^{e_k} (distinct monic irreducibles), then V = ⊕ ker p_i(T)^{e_i}. Each summand is T-invariant, the restriction has minimal polynomial p_i^{e_i}, and the projections are polynomials in T.
  - Proof from Ch 6 `thm-kernel-splitting` and `exr-polynomials-of-operators-c2` (read both).
- **Diagonalizability criterion** — `thm-diagonalizable-iff-minimal-distinct-linear`: T is diagonalizable ⇔ m_T splits with distinct roots. Pays off the Ch 6 promise. Worked uses:
  - projections;
  - involutions (char ≠ 2);
  - A^k = I over ℂ ⇒ diagonalizable;
  - [[1,1],[0,1]] is not.
- **Generalized eigenspaces** when m_T splits: ker(T − λI)^{e}, **named only as a forward pointer** to Ch 10 (the definition lives there).
- **Consequences:**
  - the restriction of a diagonalizable T to an invariant subspace is diagonalizable — `cor-restriction-diagonalizable`;
  - diagonalizability is preserved by polynomials.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** decide diagonalizability from m_A; decompose ℝ³ for a given matrix; show A³ = A ⇒ diagonalizable (char ≠ 2).
  - **C:** over 𝔽_p, A^p = A ⇒ diagonalizable over 𝔽_p; if m_T is irreducible then every non-zero v generates an invariant subspace of dimension deg m_T.

## 09-commuting-operators.md — Commuting operators

- **Invariance** — `thm-commuting-preserves-eigenspaces`: ST = TS ⇒ each E_λ(T) is S-invariant.
- **Common eigenvector** — `thm-commuting-common-eigenvector` (over ℂ, or when p splits): search inside an eigenspace.
- **Simultaneous triangularization** — `thm-simultaneous-triangularization` (commuting family, split polynomials).
- **Simultaneous diagonalization** — `thm-simultaneous-diagonalization`: commuting diagonalizable operators are simultaneously diagonalizable. Proof via restriction to eigenspaces and `cor-restriction-diagonalizable`. **Converse:** simultaneously diagonalizable ⇒ commute.
  - Extension to any (possibly infinite) commuting family of diagonalizable operators — `thm-simultaneous-diagonalization-family` (reduce to a finite spanning subfamily in 𝓛(V)).
- **Warning:** commuting does not imply both are diagonalizable; AB = BA with A diagonalizable and B not ([[1,1],[0,1]] and I).
- **Worked:** simultaneously diagonalize two 3×3 commuting matrices — `exm-simultaneous-diagonalization`, verified.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find a common eigenvector; decide simultaneous diagonalizability; show that matrices commuting with diag(1, 2, 3) are diagonal.
  - **C:** if A has n distinct eigenvalues and AB = BA, then B is a polynomial in A; commuting nilpotents have a nilpotent sum and product.

## 10-spectral-mapping.md — Spectral mapping, AB vs BA, left eigenvectors

- **Spectral mapping** — `thm-spectral-mapping`: over ℂ (or when p_T splits), spec p(T) = p(spec T), and the characteristic polynomial of p(T) has roots p(λ_i) with multiplicity (via triangularization).
  - **Warning:** over ℝ it fails (the rotation squared).
- **AB vs BA** — `thm-ab-ba-eigenvalues`: AB and BA have the same non-zero eigenvalues with the same algebraic multiplicities. For A m×n and B n×m, x^n p_{AB}(x) = x^m p_{BA}(x). Proof via block matrices (Ch 8 Schur/Sylvester determinant identity) — pays off Ch 7's "general case" promise.
  - **Warning:** the geometric multiplicities of 0 can differ.
- **Left eigenvectors** — `def-left-eigenvector`: yᵀA = λyᵀ ⇔ eigenvectors of Aᵀ; same eigenvalues (Ch 7 `exr-characteristic-polynomial-b2`); biorthogonality of left and right eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues — `thm-left-right-biorthogonal` (yᵀv = 0).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** eigenvalues of A² + A + I from those of A; verify x^n p_{AB} = x^m p_{BA} for a 2×3 example; left eigenvectors of a 2×2.
  - **C:** tr(AB)^k = tr(BA)^k; for nilpotent N commuting with A, spec(A + N) = spec(A).
