# Blueprint: Chapter 18 — Non-negative Matrices

**Directory:** `src/ch18-nonnegative/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Non-negative Matrices" (hyphenated, as the book writes it everywhere; the directory keeps `nonnegative`). **Part:** continues "Part V · Matrix Analysis" — add to `config/config.json` as a bare string after `src/ch17-convexity`, **with a minimal two-line edit** (never round-trip the JSON).

**Purpose.** Chapter 8 §11 met Markov chains, proved their long-run behaviour only for diagonalizable matrices, and promised that "Chapter 18 proves the Perron–Frobenius theorem, which gives the conclusions of `thm-markov-limit-diagonalizable` for every stochastic matrix with some power having all entries positive, without assuming diagonalizability and without computing eigenvalues." Chapter 17 §08 stated **Birkhoff's theorem** in two equivalent forms and left its proof here. This chapter is the theory of matrices whose entries are all ≥ 0: the spectral radius is an eigenvalue with a nonnegative eigenvector, and when the matrix is positive or irreducible that eigenvalue is simple and its eigenvector is positive. Everything else — Markov chains, PageRank, doubly stochastic matrices, M-matrices — follows.

**Corrections found while drafting:**

1. **§01, the Collatz–Wielandt discontinuity** was misstated here. For A > 0 the repaired quotient (minimum over indices with x_i > 0) is continuous on Δ_n, since a vanishing coordinate's ratio tends to +∞; the discontinuity is real only for A ≥ 0 with zero entries (diag(2,1) shows it). §01 proves Perron by maximizing t over the compact set {(x,t) : x ∈ Δ_n, t ≥ 0, Ax ≥ tx}, which avoids the quotient.
2. **§02, the n = 1 convention is forced.** `thm-irreducible-power-positive` needs every 1×1 matrix, including (0), to be irreducible (since (I+A)^0 = I > 0), unlike some sources. So "ρ(A) > 0" in Perron–Frobenius carries n ≥ 2.
3. **Edge direction** of G(A): j → i when a_ij ≠ 0 (column index to row index), to match x_{k+1} = Ax_k and column-stochastic matrices. Not specified here originally.
4. **§07, the suggested Birkhoff route had a gap.** "At most 2n−1 non-zero entries, so some row has one non-zero, delete and induct" does not close on the count alone: 1 ⊕ ½J₂ is in Ω₃ with 5 = 2·3−1 non-zeros and is not a permutation matrix. The induction must carry **extremality**: the reduced matrix is shown extreme in Ω_{n−1}, then the count is reapplied. §07 does this and shows the counterexample in a warning.
5. **Normalization of the left Perron vector: wᵀv = 1, chapter-wide** (coordinator's decision). §01 first scaled it by 𝟏ᵀw = 1 and §03 by wᵀv = 1. The right vector keeps 𝟏ᵀv = 1. The limit (A/ρ)^k → vwᵀ/(wᵀv) is scale-invariant, so nothing depends on the choice beyond readability.
6. **§04, overstated hypotheses.** This blueprint said the row-sum bounds and the max form of Collatz–Wielandt need irreducibility. They hold for every A ≥ 0; only the equality clause and the attainment of the min form need it.

**The field is ℝ**, and the entries are real. The spectrum lives in ℂ as usual.

**Conventions to state once and obey.**
- **Stochastic means column-stochastic** in this book (`def-stochastic-matrix`, Ch 8 §11: every *column* is a probability vector), and a Markov chain is `x_{k+1} = A x_k`. Many sources use rows; say so once, and never switch.
- **Entrywise order**: `A ≥ 0`, `A > 0` (every entry positive), `x ≥ 0`, `x > 0`, as in Ch 17 §05's NOTATION row. **Never write `A ⪰ 0` for this**; `⪰` is the Loewner order. Warn the reader once: a positive matrix need not be positive definite, and vice versa, with a 2×2 example each way.
- `ρ(A)` is the spectral radius (`def-spectral-radius`, Ch 15 §04).

## 01-positive-matrices.md — Positive matrices and Perron's theorem
- Entrywise order: definitions and the monotonicity facts `A ≥ B ≥ 0 ⇒ ρ(A) ≥ ρ(B)` (via Gelfand or via powers and a monotone norm — prove it) and `|Ax| ≤ |A||x|`.
- `thm-perron` (**major**): if A > 0 then ρ(A) > 0 is an eigenvalue, it is algebraically simple, it has a positive eigenvector, every other eigenvalue has modulus < ρ(A), and no other eigenvector is nonnegative. Pick one proof route and say which: the Collatz–Wielandt route through the simplex (compactness (A3)/(A4) — mind that the ratio min_i (Ax)_i/x_i is not continuous on the boundary of Δ_n; work on A(Δ_n) or with positive vectors), or another. §04 develops Collatz–Wielandt fully; §01 may use the minimum of it it needs.
- Warning: entrywise positive vs positive definite, each way.
- Worked 2×2 and 3×3 examples, eigenvalues exact.

## 02-irreducibility-and-graphs.md — Irreducibility and graphs
- `def-directed-graph-of-matrix`, paths, `def-strongly-connected`; `def-irreducible` (**major**) by permutation similarity to a block upper triangular form, and `thm-irreducible-iff-strongly-connected`.
- `thm-irreducible-power-positive`: A ≥ 0 is irreducible iff (I + A)^{n−1} > 0. Prove it.
- The reducible normal form (Frobenius normal form) — state and prove at the level of block triangular with irreducible diagonal blocks.
- Examples: path, cycle, a reducible matrix; a warning that irreducibility is a property of the zero pattern only.

## 03-perron-frobenius.md — The Perron–Frobenius theorem
- `thm-perron-frobenius` (**major**): A ≥ 0 irreducible ⇒ ρ(A) > 0 (for n ≥ 2), algebraically simple eigenvalue with a positive eigenvector, unique nonnegative eigenvector up to scale. Route: apply Perron to (I+A)^{n−1} or to A + εJ and pass to the limit; say which.
- `thm-nonnegative-rho-eigenvalue`: every A ≥ 0 has ρ(A) as an eigenvalue with a nonnegative eigenvector — by the limit A + εJ → A, using `cor-eigenvalues-continuous` and compactness of the unit sphere/simplex. **Name the imported facts.**
- Negative results: nilpotent (ρ = 0), a reducible matrix with ρ repeated, a nonnegative eigenvector for a smaller eigenvalue.
- The left Perron vector (via Aᵀ, `prp-left-eigenvectors-transpose`) and the normalization vᵀw.

## 04-collatz-wielandt.md — The Collatz–Wielandt formula
- `thm-collatz-wielandt`: for A ≥ 0 irreducible, ρ(A) = max_{x≥0, x≠0} min_{x_i>0} (Ax)_i/x_i = min_{x>0} max_i (Ax)_i/x_i. Prove both.
- Corollaries: row/column-sum bounds min ≤ ρ ≤ max, with equality iff all sums are equal (irreducible case) — compare with Ch 15's `cor-spectral-radius-row-column-bound`, which gives only the upper bound for all matrices; subinvariance `Ax ≤ sx, x > 0 ⇒ ρ ≤ s`.
- Worked numerical estimates of ρ from test vectors, verified.

## 05-primitive-matrices.md — Primitive matrices and the limit theorem
- `def-primitive` (A ≥ 0 with some A^k > 0); `thm-primitive-iff-single-peripheral`: irreducible A is primitive iff ρ is the only eigenvalue of modulus ρ.
- The period h and the peripheral spectrum: the eigenvalues of modulus ρ are ρ·e^{2πij/h} (state; prove if the route is clean, else scope it explicitly).
- `thm-primitive-limit` (**major**): A primitive ⇒ (A/ρ)^k → v wᵀ / (wᵀ v), with v, w the right and left Perron vectors. Proof via Ch 9 §10's convergence theorems — no diagonalizability.
- Wielandt's bound k ≤ (n−1)² + 1 for primitivity: state, credit, give the extremal example; prove only if short.
- Negative result: the cyclic permutation — irreducible, not primitive, powers do not converge.

## 06-markov-chains.md — Stochastic matrices and Markov chains
- **Pay Ch 8 §11's promise in its words**: for a stochastic A with some A^k > 0, there is exactly one steady state, A^k → v 1ᵀ, and every chain converges to v — no diagonalizability, no eigenvalue computation.
- Irreducible (not primitive) chains: unique stationary distribution, Cesàro convergence of the averages. Reducible chains: several stationary distributions (negative result).
- Absorbing chains: the fundamental matrix N = (I − Q)^{−1} via the Neumann series (`thm-neumann-series-spectral`), expected absorption times and probabilities. A worked example (gambler's ruin, small), exact.

## 07-birkhoff.md — Doubly stochastic matrices and Birkhoff's theorem
- **Pay Ch 17 §08's promise.** `thm-birkhoff` (**major**): every extreme point of Ω_n is a permutation matrix. Suggested route (verify it before writing): Ω_n is the polyhedron {X ≥ 0, row sums 1, column sums 1} in ℝ^{n²}; by `thm-vertices-basic-feasible` a vertex has n² linearly independent active constraints; the 2n equality constraints have rank 2n−1, so at least n² − (2n−1) entries vanish, leaving at most 2n−1 non-zero entries; some row therefore has exactly one non-zero entry, which must be 1; delete its row and column and induct. **Check the rank claim 2n−1 and the induction step (that the reduced matrix is again a vertex, or argue extremality directly).** A second route via the Frobenius–König / Hall theorem may be mentioned.
- With Ch 17 §08's equivalence, the convex-combination form follows; state it as a corollary with the number of permutations needed (Carathéodory bound).
- Complete Ch 17 §08's aside: a second proof of `thm-schur-majorization` via Birkhoff (d(A) = Sλ(A) with S doubly stochastic, S = (q_ij²)); cite the first proof.
- Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya (x ≺ y ⇔ x = Dy for a doubly stochastic D): **Chapter 20's** ("Majorization and doubly stochastic maps" in the plan) — pointer only; record it in the report.

## 08-pagerank.md — PageRank
- The web as a directed graph; the column-stochastic link matrix P; dangling pages; the Google matrix G = αP + (1−α)(1/n)𝟏𝟏ᵀ, which is positive, hence Perron applies: a unique positive ranking vector.
- The rate: the second eigenvalue of G has modulus ≤ α — prove it (the classical argument: eigenvalues of G other than 1 are α times eigenvalues of P restricted to the complement). **Verify the statement precisely before writing it.**
- A small worked example (4–5 pages), ranking computed exactly.
- The power method is Chapter 23's (the plan lists it); a pointer is allowed.

## 09-m-matrices.md — M-matrices
- `def-z-matrix`, `def-m-matrix` (A = sI − B, B ≥ 0, s ≥ ρ(B); nonsingular when s > ρ(B)).
- `thm-m-matrix-characterizations` (**major**): for a Z-matrix, TFAE: nonsingular M-matrix; A⁻¹ ≥ 0; Ax > 0 for some x > 0; all principal minors positive; all real eigenvalues positive (or: every real eigenvalue positive). Prove a well-chosen cycle; the Neumann series (`thm-neumann-series-spectral`) is the engine. **Pick a set you can fully prove; list any stated-only equivalences honestly.**

- Warning: A⁻¹ ≥ 0 alone does not make A a Z-matrix.
