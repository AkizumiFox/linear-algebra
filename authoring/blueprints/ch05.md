# Blueprint: Chapter 5 — Duality

**Directory:** `src/ch05-duality/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Duality".

**Purpose.** Linear functionals are the measurements of a vector space. Every question about a subspace has a mirror question about the functionals that vanish on it. This chapter builds:
- the dual space and dual bases;
- annihilators;
- the double dual and its natural isomorphism;
- the dual map, whose matrix is the transpose;
- duality for subspaces and quotients.

It ends by revisiting linear systems: every subspace is an intersection of hyperplanes, and row rank = column rank is proved a third way.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–4, including linear maps, matrices of maps, change of basis, quotients (Ch 4 §04) and the First Isomorphism Theorem. No determinants, no inner products. Everything is over a general field F. Finite dimension is assumed only where stated, and every result says so.

**Notation** (NOTATION.md):
- \( V^{*} \) for the dual space;
- Greek letters \( \varphi, \psi \) for functionals;
- dual basis \( (\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n) \) or \( (\v_1^{*}, \dots, \v_n^{*}) \) (pick one and use it throughout: prefer \( \varphi_i \) with a remark on the \( \v_i^* \) notation);
- annihilator \( U^{0} \);
- dual map \( T' \colon W^{*} \to V^{*} \);
- evaluation map \( \operatorname{ev} \colon V \to V^{**} \) (add a macro `\ev` if needed).

**Signature moves.**
- **To show a vector is zero, test it against every functional** (a dual basis suffices).
- **Pass to the dual and count:** dim U + dim U⁰ = dim V.
- **Natural vs chosen:** isomorphisms that need a basis vs those that don't.

## 01-linear-functionals.md — Linear functionals and the dual space

- **Hook:** the coordinate functions \( \v \mapsto \) (the i-th coordinate in a basis), evaluation \( p \mapsto p(3) \), \( f \mapsto \int_0^1 f \), \( A \mapsto \tr A \). All are linear maps to F. Name them and study them as a family.
- **Definition** `def-linear-functional` and `def-dual-space`: \( V^{*} = \cL(V, F) \), a vector space by Ch 2 `thm-linear-maps-vector-space`. **Major notion:** full template.
- **Examples:**
  - row vectors act on \( F^n \): every functional on \( F^n \) is \( \x \mapsto \a\tp\x \) (`thm-functionals-on-fn`);
  - evaluation;
  - integration;
  - trace.
  - **Degenerate:** the zero functional; the dual of {0}.
- **Non-examples:** \( \x \mapsto \lVert \x \rVert \) (not linear); \( p \mapsto p(0)^2 \); \( A \mapsto \det \)-like 2×2 \( ad - bc \) (not linear).
- **Dual basis** `thm-dual-basis`: given a basis \( (\v_1, \dots, \v_n) \), there are unique \( \varphi_i \) with \( \varphi_i(\v_j) = \delta_{ij} \), and they form a basis of \( V^{*} \). Proof via `thm-linear-transform-basis` (existence and uniqueness), then independence by evaluating at \( \v_j \) and spanning by \( \varphi = \sum \varphi(\v_i)\varphi_i \). Corollary: dim V* = dim V.
  - **Worked:** the dual basis of \( ((1, 1), (1, -1)) \) in ℝ², found by inverting the matrix. Explain that the dual basis vectors are the rows of the inverse matrix (`exm-dual-basis-inverse-rows`).
  - **Worked:** the dual basis of \( (1, x, x^2) \) in \( \nR[x]_{\le 2} \) is \( p \mapsto p(0), p'(0), p''(0)/2 \) (`exm-dual-basis-polynomials`); and Lagrange-type evaluation functionals at three points as a dual basis of the Lagrange basis (a forward pointer to Ch 6 interpolation).
- **Separating vectors** — `thm-functionals-separate-points`: if \( \v \neq \0 \) (V finite-dimensional), some φ has \( \varphi(\v) \neq 0 \). Proof: extend (v) to a basis and take the first dual basis vector. This is the signature move "test against functionals". State the infinite-dimensional version as a remark using `thm-basis-extension-general` (Ch 1 §08).
- **Warning:** the dual basis vector \( \varphi_1 \) depends on the **whole** basis, not only on \( \v_1 \). Example: \( \varphi_1 \) for \( ((1, 0), (0, 1)) \) vs for \( ((1, 0), (1, 1)) \).
- **Warning:** "V ≅ V*" in finite dimension is true, but the isomorphism \( \v_i \mapsto \varphi_i \) depends on the basis. There is no natural choice (made precise in 03).
- **Infinite dimension** (remark with proof sketch as a C exercise): \( F[x]^{*} \) is not isomorphic to F[x] in any "size" sense; state that dim V* > dim V for infinite-dimensional V without proof, and prove only that the dual "basis" \( (\varphi_i) \) of \( (1, x, x^2, \dots) \) does not span \( F[x]^{*} \). The functional \( p \mapsto p(1) \) is not a finite combination.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find dual bases (ℝ³ with a non-standard basis; \( \nR[x]_{\le 2} \) with the basis \( (1, x - 1, (x - 1)^2) \)); decide which maps are functionals; express a functional in a dual basis.
  - **C:** show \( \varphi, \psi \in V^{*} \) with \( \ker\varphi = \ker\psi \) are scalar multiples; show that if \( \varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k \in V^{*} \) and \( \bigcap \ker\varphi_i \subseteq \ker\psi \), then ψ is a combination of the \( \varphi_i \) (hint: consider the map \( V \to F^k \)); the evaluation functionals at n + 1 distinct points form a basis of \( F[x]_{\le n}^{*} \) (use Ch 3 `thm-interpolation-unique`).

## 02-annihilators.md — Annihilators

- **Hook:** describe a plane in ℝ³ by an equation. The equations that vanish on a subspace form a subspace of the dual.
- **Definition** `def-annihilator`: \( U^{0} = \{ \varphi \in V^{*} : \varphi(\u) = 0 \text{ for all } \u \in U \} \), for any **subset** U. It is a subspace.
- **Dimension** — `thm-dimension-annihilator`: dim U + dim U⁰ = dim V (V finite-dimensional). Proof by extending a basis of U and identifying U⁰ with the span of the last dual basis vectors. Alternatively, via the restriction map \( V^{*} \to U^{*} \) and Rank–Nullity; give this as an "Alternatively" paragraph.
- **Properties** — `thm-annihilator-properties`:
  - inclusion-reversing;
  - \( (U + W)^{0} = U^{0} \cap W^{0} \);
  - \( (U \cap W)^{0} = U^{0} + W^{0} \) (finite dimension; this one needs the dimension count and is the harder half);
  - \( U^{0} = (\Span U)^{0} \).
- **Worked:** the annihilator of span((1, 2, 0), (0, 1, 1)) in ℝ³, via the null space of a matrix whose rows are the spanning vectors (Ch 3 toolkit).
- **Warning:** U⁰ lives in V*, not in V. It is not a complement of U (contrast with Ch 11's orthogonal complement: forward pointer). \( \{0\}^{0} = V^{*} \) and \( V^{0} = \{0\} \).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute annihilators (ℝ⁴, polynomials vanishing conditions); verify the dimension formula; show \( U \subseteq W \Rightarrow W^{0} \subseteq U^{0} \).
  - **C:** show \( (U \cap W)^{0} = U^{0} + W^{0} \) using the dimension formula; compute \( U^{0} \) for \( U = \{ p \in \nR[x]_{\le 3} : p(1) = p'(1) = 0 \} \) as a span of explicit functionals.

## 03-double-dual.md — The double dual

- **Hook:** V ≅ V* needed a basis, but V ≅ V** needs nothing. What does "natural" mean?
- **Evaluation map** `def-evaluation-map`: \( \operatorname{ev}_{\v}(\varphi) = \varphi(\v) \).
  - `thm-evaluation-map-linear-injective` (any dimension; injectivity uses `thm-functionals-separate-points`, and the infinite-dimensional version uses Ch 1 §08).
  - `thm-double-dual-isomorphism`: an isomorphism if V is finite-dimensional (count).
- **Naturality** — `thm-evaluation-natural`: for T: V → W, \( T''\operatorname{ev}_V = \operatorname{ev}_W T \). State it after the dual map is defined, so this sits in 04, or define the double dual map here via composition directly. **Drafter:** place this theorem in 04 if the dual map is needed; keep 03's discussion of "natural" informal with a forward pointer.
- **Consequences** — `cor-every-basis-of-dual-is-dual`: every basis of V* is the dual basis of some basis of V (finite dimension).
  - `cor-annihilator-of-annihilator`: under the identification V ≅ V**, \( U^{00} = U \).
  - `cor-subspaces-determined-by-annihilator`: U = W ⇔ U⁰ = W⁰.
- **Warning:** V ≅ V** fails for infinite-dimensional V (ev is not surjective; state it and show it for F[x], using the non-spanning result from 01).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find the basis of ℝ² whose dual basis is a given pair of functionals; verify \( U^{00} = U \) for a line in ℝ³.
  - **C:** for finite-dimensional V, the map \( U \mapsto U^{0} \) is an inclusion-reversing bijection from the subspaces of V to the subspaces of V*.

## 04-dual-map.md — The dual map and the transpose

- **Hook:** a map T: V → W moves vectors forward; it moves measurements backward. Pulling back a functional on W along T gives one on V.
- **Definition** `def-dual-map`: \( T'(\psi) = \psi \circ T \). It is linear.
- **Properties** — `thm-dual-map-properties`: \( (S T)' = T' S' \); \( (\id_V)' = \id_{V^*} \); \( (aS + bT)' = aS' + bT' \); T invertible ⇒ \( (T')^{-1} = (T^{-1})' \).
- **Matrix** — `thm-matrix-of-dual-map`: \( \mtx{T'}{\sC^{*}}{\sB^{*}} = \left(\mtx{T}{\sB}{\sC}\right)\tp \). This is the reason for the transpose, and pays off Ch 0's `def-transpose`. Worked example with a 2×3 matrix.
- **Kernel and image** — `thm-kernel-image-dual-map`: \( \ker T' = (\im T)^{0} \); \( \im T' = (\ker T)^{0} \) (finite dimension for the second).
  - **Corollary** `cor-rank-dual-map`: rank T' = rank T; hence **row rank = column rank**, a third proof; compare with Ch 3 `thm-row-rank-equals-column-rank`.
  - **Corollary** `cor-injective-iff-dual-surjective`: T is surjective ⇔ T' is injective; T is injective ⇔ T' is surjective.
- **Naturality of the double dual** — `thm-evaluation-natural`, if deferred from 03.
- **Warning:** the dual map goes backwards, W* → V*. Composition order reverses, just as \( (AB)\tp = B\tp A\tp \).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute T' for D on \( \nR[x]_{\le 2} \) in dual bases and check that it is the transpose; find ker T' and im T' for a given 2×3 matrix map; prove \( (ST)' = T'S' \).
  - **C:** Fredholm alternative (finite dimension): T(x) = b is solvable ⇔ every ψ ∈ ker T' has ψ(b) = 0; interpret for Ax = b as "b is orthogonal to every solution of Aᵀy = 0" (without inner products: yᵀb = 0).

## 05-duality-subspaces-quotients.md — Duality for subspaces and quotients

- **Restriction** — `thm-dual-of-subspace`: the restriction map \( V^{*} \to U^{*} \) is surjective with kernel U⁰, hence \( U^{*} \cong V^{*}/U^{0} \) (First Isomorphism Theorem, Ch 4).
- **Quotient** — `thm-dual-of-quotient`: \( (V/U)^{*} \cong U^{0} \), via \( \psi \mapsto \psi \circ \pi \) (the dual map of the quotient map; injective because π is surjective).
- **Worked example:** for U a line in ℝ³, identify both isomorphisms concretely in bases.
- **Why "natural" matters here:** neither isomorphism uses a basis.
- **Codimension** — `def-codimension`: codim U = dim V/U. In finite dimension codim U = dim U⁰. This fits hyperplanes (codimension 1) — `thm-hyperplane-kernel-functional`: a subspace is a hyperplane ⇔ it is the kernel of a non-zero functional (any dimension).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** a basis of \( (V/U)^{*} \) inside V* for a concrete U ⊆ ℝ⁴; show two non-zero functionals have the same kernel iff they are proportional (recall 01 C); dimension computations.
  - **C:** every subspace of codimension k in V is an intersection of k hyperplanes, and not fewer (any dimension, with a finite-codimension hypothesis).

## 06-hyperplanes-and-systems.md — Hyperplanes and linear systems revisited

≈2,500–3,500 words. Pay off the chapter.

- **Every subspace of Fⁿ is the solution set of a homogeneous system** — `thm-subspace-is-solution-set`: take a basis of U⁰ as the rows. The number of equations needed is exactly n − dim U.
  - **Worked:** find equations for span((1, 1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1, 1)) in ℝ⁴ (the "implicit vs parametric" description, `exm-implicit-equations`).
  - **Converting between** parametric (span) and implicit (equations) descriptions, as an algorithm with a proof of correctness.
- **Solution sets of Ax = b** as affine subspaces: intersection of hyperplanes; relation to Ch 3 `thm-general-solution-structure`.
- **Duality and elimination:** the rows of A span \( (\nul A)^{0} \) (identifying row vectors with functionals) — `thm-row-space-annihilates-null-space`. Hence dim row(A) + dim nul(A) = n, again.
- **Sum and intersection** of subspaces by duality: \( U \cap W \) via the union of equations; \( U + W \) via spanning lists. `exm-intersection-via-equations`, contrasted with Ch 3's elimination method.
- **Annihilators and linear independence of functionals:** \( \varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_k \) are independent ⇔ the map \( V \to F^k \) is surjective (C-exercise-level theorem, placed here if not already in 01).
- **Summary and transfer block** for the chapter: the dictionary (subspace ↔ annihilator; injective ↔ surjective; kernel ↔ image; matrix ↔ transpose).
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** implicit equations for a given span; a spanning list for a given system; intersection of two planes in ℝ⁴ using equations.
  - **C:** for subspaces \( U_1, \dots, U_k \) of \( F^n \) over an **infinite** field, there is a single vector outside all proper ones (the Ch 1 exercise), now reproved with functionals (choose a functional vanishing on none); a proof that every system has a least number of equations equal to its codimension.
