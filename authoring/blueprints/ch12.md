# Blueprint: Chapter 12 — Positive Matrices and the Singular Value Decomposition

**Directory:** `src/ch12-psd-and-svd/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Positive Matrices and the Singular Value Decomposition". **Part:** continues "Part III · Inner Products and Spectral Theory" (no `part` key).

**Purpose.** Chapter 11 answered "when is there an orthonormal eigenbasis?". Two questions it left open are the subject here. First, among self-adjoint operators, which ones behave like non-negative numbers — and what does the analogy buy? Second, **every** matrix, square or not, normal or not, has an orthonormal-to-orthonormal description: the singular value decomposition, the most useful factorization in applied linear algebra. The chapter builds

- positive semidefinite and positive definite matrices, and the many equivalent tests;
- the square root and the Cholesky factorization;
- Gram matrices, and the exact criterion for a matrix to be one;
- simultaneous diagonalization of two forms, and the generalized eigenvalue problem;
- the Loewner order, and what monotonicity survives it;
- block positive matrices and Schur complements;
- the Schur product theorem, and the Hadamard, Fischer and Oppenheim inequalities;
- the SVD, in full and compact form, with the four subspaces read off it;
- polar decomposition, and Takagi's factorization of a complex symmetric matrix;
- Eckart–Young: the best low-rank approximation;
- the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse and the Penrose conditions;
- idempotents, and orthogonal versus oblique projections.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–11. The field is **ℝ or ℂ** throughout, as in Chapters 10 and 11. No analysis beyond what Chapter 11 already assumed. Chapter 9 is needed only in passing.

Add a row only if a section genuinely needs a new symbol, and say so in the report.

**Signature moves:**
- **Test with \( \inner{\A\x}{\x} \).** Positivity is a statement about one scalar function; most proofs evaluate it at a cleverly chosen \( \x \).
- **Factor as \( \B^{*}\B \).** Every positive semidefinite matrix is a Gram matrix, and that is usually the useful form.
- **Diagonalize, do it to the eigenvalues, come back.** The square root, the pseudoinverse and the functional calculus are all this move.
- **Apply the spectral theorem to \( \A^{*}\A \).** The SVD is the spectral theorem for \( \A^{*}\A \), read through \( \A \).
- **Truncate the sum.** Eckart–Young, compact SVD and the pseudoinverse all come from cutting \( \sum \sigma_i \u_i\v_i^{*} \) short.

## 01-positive-definite-matrices.md — Positive semidefinite and positive definite

- **Hook:** which self-adjoint operators deserve to be called non-negative? Three candidate answers — non-negative spectrum, \( \inner{T\v}{\v} \ge 0 \), \( T = S^{*}S \) — and they coincide.
- **Definition** `def-positive-semidefinite` (**major**): self-adjoint (over ℝ: symmetric) with \( \inner{\A\x}{\x} \ge 0 \) for all \( \x \); **positive definite** when \( > 0 \) for \( \x \ne \0 \). **Warning up front:** over ℝ, self-adjointness must be *assumed*; over ℂ it is *implied* by \( \inner{\A\x}{\x} \in \nR \) for all x (cite Ch 11's complex zero test). Give the real counterexample (a rotation has \( \inner{\A\x}{\x} = 0 \) for all x).
- **Theorem** `thm-psd-characterizations` (**major**, TFAE): \( \A \succeq 0 \); every eigenvalue \( \ge 0 \); \( \A = \B^{*}\B \) for some \( \B \); \( \A = \C^2 \) for a unique \( \C \succeq 0 \); every principal minor \( \ge 0 \). Route: spectral theorem for the first three, `thm-normal-square-root` for the fourth. `exr-self-adjoint-operators-c1` (Ch 11) already proves one implication — cite it.
- **Theorem** `thm-pd-characterizations` (TFAE): \( \A \succ 0 \); every eigenvalue \( > 0 \); \( \A = \B^{*}\B \) with \( \B \) invertible; **Sylvester's criterion**, every *leading* principal minor \( > 0 \). Prove Sylvester's criterion properly (induction with the bordered matrix, or via \( \L\D\L\tp \)); it is the one test a reader can run by hand.
  - **Warning:** for semidefiniteness the *leading* minors are not enough — \( \diag(0, -1) \) has leading minors \( 0, 0 \). **All** principal minors are needed. This is the classic trap; give it its own warning.
- **The TFAE grows:** add to the invertibility theorem the clause "\( \A^{*}\A \succ 0 \)" for square \( \A \) (cite `lem-kernel-normal-equations`).
- **Examples:** \( \I \); \( \diag \) of non-negatives; \( \x\x^{*} \); the Gram matrix of any list; a covariance-style matrix with integer entries; the 2×2 test \( a > 0 \) and \( ad - |b|^2 > 0 \).
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C: B decides definiteness for listed matrices (planted failures, including the leading-minor trap); C proves that \( \A \succeq 0 \) and \( \tr\A = 0 \) force \( \A = \0 \), and that the set of PSD matrices is a convex cone closed under congruence \( \A \mapsto \S^{*}\A\S \).

## 02-square-roots-and-cholesky.md — The square root and Cholesky

- **Square root** `thm-psd-square-root`: a unique \( \A^{1/2} \succeq 0 \) with \( (\A^{1/2})^2 = \A \); it is a polynomial in \( \A \) (Lagrange interpolation on the spectrum, Ch 5), hence commutes with everything \( \A \) commutes with. Specialize `thm-normal-square-root` rather than reproving; the polynomial clause is new and worth proving here.
- **Definition** `def-matrix-absolute-value`: \( \lvert\A\rvert = (\A^{*}\A)^{1/2} \), with \( \norm{\lvert\A\rvert\x} = \norm{\A\x} \) — the identity that makes §09's polar decomposition work. **Warning:** \( \lvert\A\rvert \) is not the entrywise absolute value, and \( \lvert\A\B\rvert \ne \lvert\A\rvert\lvert\B\rvert \) in general (give a 2×2).
- **Cholesky** `thm-cholesky` (**major**): \( \A \succ 0 \) iff \( \A = \L\L^{*} \) with \( \L \) lower triangular with positive diagonal, and then \( \L \) is unique. **This pays off Chapter 2 §06's promise**: derive it from `thm-ldlt` by absorbing \( \D^{1/2} \), and say so explicitly, in Chapter 2's words. Uniqueness by the QR-style argument.
  - **Worked:** a 3×3 Cholesky by hand with integer/√-friendly data, and the same matrix failing the algorithm when a diagonal entry goes negative — the algorithm *is* the test.
  - **Cost remark:** half of LU, and no pivoting needed; the reason belongs to Chapter 23 (prose only).
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C: B computes \( \A^{1/2} \) and Cholesky factors; C proves \( \A \succeq 0 \Rightarrow \tr(\A\B) \ge 0 \) for \( \B \succeq 0 \) (via \( \tr(\A\B) = \tr(\A^{1/2}\B\A^{1/2}) \)), and that \( \A^{1/2} \) commutes with every matrix commuting with \( \A \).

## 03-gram-matrices.md — Gram matrices

Short: ≈2,500–3,000 words.

- **Theorem** `thm-gram-iff-psd`: \( \G \) is the Gram matrix of some list in some inner product space **iff** \( \G \succeq 0 \); the list is determined up to an isometry, and can be taken in \( F^{r} \) with \( r = \rank\G \). Cite `thm-gram-matrix-properties` (Ch 10 §02) and pay off `cor-gram-determinant-nonnegative` (Ch 6 §07) in its sharp form: \( \det\G \ge 0 \) with equality iff the list is dependent.
- **Gram determinant as squared volume** `thm-gram-determinant-volume`: \( \det\G(\v_1, \dots, \v_k) \) is the squared \( k \)-volume of the parallelepiped, recovering Chapter 6's \( \sqrt{\det(\A\tp\A)} \) for a non-square \( \A \). One TikZ picture in ℝ³ of a parallelogram sitting in a plane.
- **Distance to a subspace by Gram determinants** `thm-gram-distance-formula`, with a worked example checked against Chapter 10 §03's projection answer — the same number twice, by different routes.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 04-simultaneous-diagonalization.md — Two forms at once

- **Hook:** Chapter 11 diagonalized commuting normal operators. Two symmetric matrices need not commute, yet if one is positive definite they can still be diagonalized together — by congruence, not by similarity. Be explicit that this is a *different* theorem from Chapter 11 §07 and say why.
- **Theorem** `thm-simultaneous-congruence`: \( \A \succ 0 \), \( \B \) self-adjoint \( \Rightarrow \) there is invertible \( \S \) with \( \S^{*}\A\S = \I \) and \( \S^{*}\B\S \) diagonal (real). Proof: whiten with \( \A^{-1/2} \), spectral-theorem the result, come back.
- **Generalized eigenvalue problem** `def-generalized-eigenvalue`, `thm-generalized-eigenvalues-real`: \( \B\x = \lambda\A\x \) has real \( \lambda \) and an \( \A \)-orthogonal eigenbasis. Worked 2×2 or 3×3, with the physical reading (stiffness and mass) in one honest sentence.
- **Warning:** two self-adjoint matrices with *neither* positive definite need not be simultaneously diagonalizable by congruence; give the standard 2×2 pair.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 05-the-loewner-order.md — The Loewner order

- **Definition** `def-loewner-order`: \( \A \succeq \B \) means \( \A - \B \succeq 0 \). Show it is a partial order (antisymmetry needs an argument — prove it).
- **What survives** `thm-loewner-basic`: congruence preserves it; \( \A \succeq \B \succeq 0 \Rightarrow \tr\A \ge \tr\B \), \( \det\A \ge \det\B \), \( \lambda_i(\A) \ge \lambda_i(\B) \) (the eigenvalue clause may be quoted from Chapter 16 or proved for the extreme eigenvalues only — decide and be honest); \( \A \succeq \B \succ 0 \Rightarrow \B^{-1} \succeq \A^{-1} \) (prove: the order-reversing inverse is the memorable one).
- **What fails** — the chapter's negative result, `exm-loewner-not-monotone`: \( \A \succeq \B \succeq 0 \) does **not** imply \( \A^2 \succeq \B^2 \). Give the standard 2×2 counterexample with the numbers computed. It *does* imply \( \A^{1/2} \succeq \B^{1/2} \); state that, prove it if it fits in half a page, otherwise attribute it forward to Chapter 20 (operator monotone functions) — no @ref.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 06-block-positive-matrices.md — Blocks and Schur complements

- **Theorem** `thm-block-psd-schur`: for \( \M = \begin{psmallmatrix} \A & \B \\ \B^{*} & \C\end{psmallmatrix} \) with \( \A \succ 0 \): \( \M \succ 0 \) iff the Schur complement \( \M/\A = \C - \B^{*}\A^{-1}\B \succ 0 \); and \( \M \succeq 0 \) iff \( \A \succeq 0 \), \( \im\B \subseteq \im\A \) and \( \C - \B^{*}\A^{+}\B \succeq 0 \) (the semidefinite version needs the pseudoinverse — either forward-reference §11 in prose or restrict the statement to the definite case and put the general one in §11).
  - **This pays off Chapter 7 §03's promise.** Read it and discharge it in its own words; cite `def-schur-complement`, `thm-block-ldu`, `thm-schur-determinant`.
- **Corollary** `cor-fischer-inequality`: \( \det\M \le \det\A\,\det\C \) for \( \M \succeq 0 \), from `thm-schur-determinant` plus \( \det(\M/\A) \le \det\C \).
- **Hadamard's inequality** `thm-hadamard-inequality`: \( \det\A \le a_{11}\cdots a_{nn} \) for \( \A \succeq 0 \), and the general \( \lvert\det\B\rvert \le \prod \norm{\b_j} \). Prove the second from the first via \( \A = \B^{*}\B \). One TikZ or one sentence on the geometric reading (a box has the largest volume among parallelepipeds with given edge lengths).
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 07-schur-product-theorem.md — Products entry by entry

Short: ≈2,500–3,000 words.

- **Definition** recall `A \circ B` (Hadamard) from NOTATION; note it is *not* matrix multiplication and that the book has used it nowhere yet.
- **Theorem** `thm-schur-product` (**major**): \( \A, \B \succeq 0 \Rightarrow \A \circ \B \succeq 0 \); positive definite if both are. Proof: \( \A = \sum \lambda_i\u_i\u_i^{*} \), \( \B = \sum \mu_j\w_j\w_j^{*} \), and \( (\u\u^{*}) \circ (\w\w^{*}) = (\u \circ \w)(\u \circ \w)^{*} \) — a one-line identity worth displaying on its own.
- **Oppenheim's inequality** `thm-oppenheim`: \( \det(\A \circ \B) \ge \det\A \prod b_{ii} \) for \( \A, \B \succeq 0 \). Proof by induction with Schur complements (§06).
- **Application:** a covariance-style example, and the remark that entrywise products preserve positivity while entrywise inverses do not.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 08-singular-value-decomposition.md — The SVD

- **Hook:** a non-square matrix has no eigenvalues, but \( \A^{*}\A \) does, and it is positive semidefinite. Follow that thought.
- **Theorem** `thm-svd` (**major**): every \( \A \in M_{m \times n}(F) \) is \( \A = \U\vSigma\V^{*} \) with \( \U, \V \) unitary and \( \vSigma \) diagonal with \( \sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_{\min(m,n)} \ge 0 \). **Definition** `def-singular-values`. Proof: spectral theorem on \( \A^{*}\A \), set \( \sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i} \), define \( \u_i = \A\v_i/\sigma_i \), check orthonormality, extend.
  - **Idea:** the unit sphere maps to an ellipsoid; the \( \sigma_i \) are its semi-axes and the \( \u_i \) their directions. One TikZ picture of the ℝ² case, tying back to Chapter 11's principal axes.
- **Uniqueness** `thm-singular-values-unique`: the \( \sigma_i \) are determined by \( \A \); \( \U, \V \) are not. Say exactly what the freedom is.
- **Compact SVD and the four subspaces** `thm-compact-svd`, `cor-svd-four-subspaces`: \( \A = \sum_{i \le r}\sigma_i\u_i\v_i^{*} \) with \( r = \rank\A \), and the \( \u_i, \v_i \) give orthonormal bases of all four subspaces of Chapter 10 §06 — the cleanest possible payoff of `thm-four-subspaces-orthogonal`.
- **Relations:** \( \sigma_i(\A) \) versus \( |\lambda_i(\A)| \) — equal for normal matrices (cite Ch 11), unrelated in general (the 2×2 with zero eigenvalues and non-zero singular values); \( \norm{\A}_F^2 = \sum\sigma_i^2 \); \( \norm{\A}_2 = \sigma_1 \) if the operator norm is defined here, else defer to Ch 15 in prose.
- **Worked:** a 2×3 SVD with exact arithmetic, every factor written out.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C, including the planted trap of confusing singular values with eigenvalues.

## 09-polar-decomposition.md — Polar decomposition and Takagi

- **Theorem** `thm-polar-decomposition`: every square \( \A \) is \( \W\lvert\A\rvert \) with \( \W \) unitary, and \( \W \) is unique when \( \A \) is invertible; the right-handed version \( \A = \lvert\A^{*}\rvert\W \). Two proofs to choose from: from the SVD in one line, or directly; give the SVD route and remark that it is the polar form \( z = e^{i\theta}r \) for matrices.
- **Corollary** `cor-unitary-nearest`: \( \W \) is the nearest unitary matrix to \( \A \) in the Frobenius norm (prove it; it uses `lem-frobenius-unitarily-invariant` and the trace inequality, and is the memorable application).
- **Takagi** `thm-takagi`: a complex **symmetric** \( \A \) (not Hermitian) is \( \U\vSigma\U\tp \) with \( \U \) unitary. State carefully how it differs from the SVD and from the spectral theorem; prove it, or give the proof for the invertible case and say what is left out.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 10-low-rank-approximation.md — Eckart–Young

- **Theorem** `thm-eckart-young` (**major**): the truncated SVD \( \A_k = \sum_{i \le k}\sigma_i\u_i\v_i^{*} \) minimizes \( \norm{\A - \B}_F \) over all \( \B \) of rank \( \le k \), and the minimum is \( \bigl(\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2\bigr)^{1/2} \). Prove the Frobenius case in full (the spectral-norm case may be stated and deferred to Chapter 20 in prose).
- **Worked:** a small matrix, its rank-1 approximation, and the error computed both ways.
- **Application, honest and brief:** why this is what "principal component analysis" and "compression" mean; one paragraph, with the statistics deferred to Chapter 23. No claims the book cannot support.
- **Warning:** the truncated SVD is optimal for *these* norms; it is not optimal for entrywise error, and low rank does not mean small.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 11-pseudoinverse.md — The Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse

- **Definition** `def-pseudoinverse` via the SVD: \( \A^{+} = \V\vSigma^{+}\U^{*} \), inverting the non-zero singular values. Well-definedness needs the uniqueness discussion of §08 — handle it.
- **Theorem** `thm-penrose-conditions`: \( \A^{+} \) is the unique matrix satisfying the four Penrose conditions; state them and prove uniqueness.
- **Theorem** `thm-pseudoinverse-least-squares`: \( \A^{+}\b \) is the minimum-norm least-squares solution. **This pays off Chapter 10 §04's promise** — read it, discharge it in its own words, and settle the notation: Chapter 10 §04 wrote \( \x_{\min} \), and this is the place to say \( \x_{\min} = \A^{+}\b \) once and for all.
- **Properties** `thm-pseudoinverse-properties`: \( \A\A^{+} = P_{\col\A} \), \( \A^{+}\A = P_{\row}\)-type, \( (\A^{+})^{+} = \A \), \( \A^{+} = \A^{-1} \) when invertible. **Warning:** \( (\A\B)^{+} \ne \B^{+}\A^{+} \) in general — give the 2×2.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 12-projections-revisited.md — Idempotents, orthogonal and oblique

Short: ≈2,500–3,000 words. Closes the chapter and the part.

- **Theorem** `thm-idempotent-orthogonal-iff-selfadjoint`: an idempotent \( P \) is an orthogonal projection iff \( P = P^{*} \) iff \( \norm{P} \le 1 \) (if the operator norm is available; otherwise use \( \norm{P\v} \le \norm{\v} \) for all v). **Pays off Chapter 3 §10** — read `def-projection-operator`, `thm-projection-direct-sum` and cite them; the oblique projections of Chapter 3 are exactly the idempotents that fail this test.
- **Every idempotent is a projection along its kernel** — recall, do not reprove.
- **Canonical form of an idempotent** `thm-idempotent-canonical-form`: similar to \( \I_r \oplus \0 \); and **unitarily** similar to a block form with the "obliqueness" visible in an off-diagonal block whose norm measures the angle between image and kernel. Keep this concrete: the 2×2 case in full.
- **Summary of the part:** a short closing subsection tying Chapters 10–12 together (inner product → adjoint → spectral theorem → SVD), with the named moves of the part.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.
