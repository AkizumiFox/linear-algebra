# Blueprint: Chapter 1 — Vector Spaces

**Directory:** `src/ch01-vector-spaces/`. **Chapter title:** "Vector Spaces".

**Purpose.** Define the central object, the vector space, and build the measuring tools: span, independence, basis and dimension. Everything after this chapter rests on the Steinitz Exchange Theorem and Basis Extension, so those proofs must be complete and unhurried.

**Rewrite, not patch.** Keep the listed labels. The old files are the starting point only; many of their proofs are informal (sifting as a "process", Steinitz as an informal "general step") and must be replaced by precise proofs.

**Lists, not sets.** Independence, spanning and bases are defined for **finite lists** \( (\v_1, \dots, \v_k) \). Repeats are allowed, and a list with a repeat is dependent. Say once, in 03-span, that for an arbitrary (possibly infinite) subset \( S \), "S spans" and "S is independent" mean the same for every finite sub-list; this is used in section 08. A basis is an ordered list. Explain in a Remark why lists: {v, v} = {v} as sets would hide a dependence, and coordinates need an order.

**Allowed prerequisites.** All of Chapter 0 (labels in `src/ch00-foundations/`: grep them). Gaussian elimination is **not** available (it is Chapter 3). To test independence of concrete vectors in Fⁿ, write out the equations and solve by hand, with small systems only. Forward pointer: "Chapter 3 gives a systematic method."

**Running examples** (use them repeatedly):
- **Spaces:** \( \nR^2 \); \( \nR^3 \); \( F[x]_{\le 2} \); \( M_2(\nR) \); the functions \( \nR \to \nR \).
- **Counterexamples:** the two coordinate axes (their union is not a subspace); \( x + 2y = 0 \) vs \( x + 2y = 3 \); polynomials of degree exactly 2.

## 03-span.md — Linear combinations and span

**Keep labels:** `def-linear-combination`, `exm-linear-combinations`, `def-span`, `thm-span-subspace`, `def-spanning-set`, `exm-spanning-set`.

- **Hook:** which vectors can we build from \( \v_1, \v_2 \)? The smallest subspace containing them.
- **Definitions:**
  - linear combination of a **list**;
  - span of a list;
  - span of an arbitrary subset S (all finite combinations);
  - **span(∅) = {0}**, and why (the empty sum convention, like 0! = 1).
- **Theorem** `thm-span-subspace`: span(S) is a subspace containing S, and is contained in every subspace containing S. Proof by three checks plus minimality. Also span(S) = the intersection of all subspaces containing S — `cor-span-intersection`.
- **Spanning list, finite-dimensional** — `def-finite-dimensional`: V is spanned by some finite list.
  - **Example:** \( F[x] \) is not finite-dimensional — `thm-polynomials-infinite-dimensional`, by the max-degree argument.
- **Examples:**
  - \( \e_1, \dots, \e_n \) span Fⁿ;
  - \( 1, x, x^2 \) span \( F[x]_{\le 2} \);
  - \( (1, 1), (1, -1) \) span ℝ², solved by hand;
  - the four matrix units span \( M_2 \).
  - **Degenerate:** span of a single vector (a line, or {0}).
- **Non-example:** \( (1, 2), (2, 4) \) do not span ℝ²; exhibit a vector not reached.
- **Useful lemmas:**
  - span is monotone;
  - \( \v \in \Span(S) \) ⇒ span(S ∪ {v}) = span(S) — `thm-span-absorb`;
  - span(span S) = span S.
- **Warning:** span is always a subspace, even if S is not. "Span" is not "the set S".
- **Check:** is \( x^2 + 1 \in \Span(x^2 + x, x + 1) \)? (No: \( a(x^2 + x) + b(x + 1) = x^2 + 1 \) forces a = 1, a + b = 0, b = 1, a contradiction.)
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** decide membership in spans (ℝ³ and polynomials); show two lists have the same span; describe span geometrically.
  - **C:** span(S ∩ T) ⊆ span S ∩ span T, possibly strict (example); \( \Span(S \cup T) = \Span S + \Span T \) (define the sum informally here, or defer; decide).

## 04-independence.md — Linear independence

**Keep labels:** `def-linear-independence`, `exm-linear-independence`, `thm-linear-dependence-lemma`, `thm-span-preservation`.

- **Hook:** a naive notion that fails. "No vector is redundant", made precise. Show that "no vector is a multiple of another" fails to capture it, with three coplanar vectors in ℝ³.
- **Slogan:** the only way to build \( \0 \) is the trivial way.
- **Definition:** a list \( (\v_1, \dots, \v_k) \) is **linearly independent** if \( a_1\v_1 + \dots + a_k\v_k = \0 \) implies **all** \( a_i = 0 \). Dependent: there exist scalars **not all zero** with the combination equal to \( \0 \). The empty list is independent.
- **Examples:**
  - the standard basis;
  - \( 1, x, x^2 \) (equal as polynomials means equal coefficients);
  - \( (1, 1), (1, -1) \);
  - \( \sin, \cos \) in \( \nR^\nR \), by evaluating at 0 and π/2 — the move "choose good points".
  - **Degenerate:** a list containing \( \0 \) is dependent; a single non-zero vector is independent.
- **Non-example:** \( (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1) \); \( 1, x, 1 + x \).
- **Template move:** "Let a₁v₁ + ⋯ + a_kv_k = 0. Then … hence all a_i = 0."
- **Linear Dependence Lemma** `thm-linear-dependence-lemma`: if \( (\v_1, \dots, \v_k) \) is dependent and \( \v_1 \neq \0 \), then some \( \v_j \in \Span(\v_1, \dots, \v_{j-1}) \); moreover removing it keeps the span. Precise proof: take the largest j with \( a_j \neq 0 \). `thm-span-preservation` follows.
- **Characterizations** — `thm-independence-unique-combination`: independent ⇔ every vector in the span has a unique representation. (This is used for coordinates.)
- **Independence of polynomials of distinct degrees** — `thm-distinct-degrees-independent`.
- **Warning:** pairwise independence does not imply independence; "not all zero" is not "all non-zero".
- **Warning:** independence depends on the field. \( (1, i) \) and \( (i, -1) \) in ℂ² are dependent over ℂ, but independent over ℝ.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** decide independence (ℝ³, polynomials, \( M_2 \), functions \( e^x, e^{2x} \)); for which t are \( (1, t), (t, 1) \) independent; prove that a sub-list of an independent list is independent.
  - **C:** if \( (\u, \v, \w) \) is independent, then so is \( (\u + \v, \v + \w, \w + \u) \) over ℝ, but not over 𝔽₂ (this is the change-of-field item); \( 1, \sqrt 2, \sqrt 3 \) are independent over ℚ.

**Keep labels:** `def-basis`, `exm-standard-bases`, `thm-unique-representation`, `def-coordinates`.

- **Hook:** combine spanning (enough vectors) with independence (no waste). A basis is a coordinate system.
- **Definition:** a **basis** is a list that is independent and spans. Ordered.
- **Unique representation** `thm-unique-representation` (⇔).
- **Coordinates** `def-coordinates`: \( \coord{\v}{\sB} \in F^n \).

## 06-dimension.md — The Exchange Theorem and dimension

**Keep labels:** `thm-steinitz`, `thm-basis-theorem`, `def-dimension`, `exm-dimensions`, `thm-size-bounds`, `thm-basis-extension`, `thm-dim-impl-eq`, `thm-maximal-indep`. (`thm-sift`, `exm-sifting`, `exm-sifting-r2` and `cor-basis-existence` move to 05.)

- **Hook:** naive notions of size fail. ℝ², \( M_2(\nR) \) and \( \nR[x]_{\le 3} \) are all infinite sets, so count a minimal generating list instead. But is that count well defined?
- **Goal stated first:** all bases have the same length.
- **Steinitz Exchange Theorem** `thm-steinitz`:
  - **Statement:** if \( (\u_1, \dots, \u_m) \) is independent and \( (\w_1, \dots, \w_n) \) spans V, then m ≤ n. Moreover, after reordering the w's, \( (\u_1, \dots, \u_m, \w_{m+1}, \dots, \w_n) \) spans V.
  - **Idea:** a TikZ picture of exchanging one vector at a time.
  - **Proof:** induction on k, with the claim "after k exchanges, \( (\u_1, \dots, \u_k, \w_{k+1}, \dots, \w_n) \) spans V (after reordering)". The step writes \( \u_{k+1} \) in the current spanning list. Some coefficient of a remaining w is non-zero, **because otherwise \( \u_{k+1} \) is a combination of \( \u_1, \dots, \u_k \), contradicting independence**; this also shows that n ≥ k + 1. Solve for that w and swap it out. The span is unchanged by `thm-span-absorb`.
- **Basis theorem** `thm-basis-theorem`, via Steinitz in both directions. `def-dimension`: dim V is the length of any basis. dim {0} = 0.
- **Examples** `exm-dimensions`:
  - dim Fⁿ = n, dim \( M_{m\times n} \) = mn;
  - **\( \dim F[x]_{\le n} = n + 1 \)** — warning;
  - \( \dim_\nC \nC = 1 \) vs \( \dim_\nR \nC = 2 \): dimension depends on the field;
  - symmetric 2×2 matrices have dimension 3.
- **Size bounds** `thm-size-bounds`.
- **Basis extension** `thm-basis-extension`: an independent list in finite-dimensional V extends to a basis. Proof by Steinitz directly, or by sifting \( (\u_1, \dots, \u_m, \text{basis}) \); keep both short and pick one.
- **Subspaces** — `thm-subspace-dimension`: U ⊆ V finite-dimensional ⇒ U finite-dimensional and dim U ≤ dim V. **Careful:** U is not given a basis; build independent lists in U, which have length ≤ dim V, and take a maximal one. Also `thm-dim-impl-eq`: U ⊆ V with dim U = dim V ⇒ U = V.
- **Count instead of check** — `thm-right-size-basis`: in a space of dimension n, an independent list of length n is a basis, and a spanning list of length n is a basis. Worked use: show \( (1, 1 + x, (1 + x)^2) \) is a basis of \( \nR[x]_{\le 2} \) by checking independence only.
- **Maximal independent / minimal spanning** — `thm-maximal-indep`.
- **Warnings:**
  - dim of \( F[x]_{\le n} \);
  - dimension depends on the field;
  - "U ⊆ V and dim U = dim V ⇒ U = V" needs finite dimension (forward pointer: the shift on sequences, in section 08).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find dim and a basis of subspaces (\( \{ \x \in \nR^4 : x_1 + x_2 = x_3 - x_4 = 0 \} \); trace-free 3×3 matrices; \( \{ p \in \nR[x]_{\le 3} : p(1) = p(-1) = 0 \} \)); extend a given independent list to a basis of ℝ⁴; use count-instead-of-check on \( (1, x - 1, (x - 1)^2) \).
  - **C:** dimension of the space of n×n symmetric / skew-symmetric matrices over ℝ, n(n+1)/2 and n(n−1)/2 (and the case char F = 2 remark); if V is a ℂ-space of dimension n, then \( \dim_\nR V = 2n \) (proved properly in section 09, so here give the hint: take a ℂ-basis and multiply by i); a finite field F with \( \dim_{\nF_p} F = k \) has \( p^k \) elements.

## 07-sums-and-direct-sums.md — Sums and direct sums

**Keep labels:** `def-sum-of-subspaces`, `thm-dimension-formula-subspace-dim`, `eq-linear-combo` (may be dropped if no longer needed), `def-direct-sum`, `exm-direct-sum-of-matrix`. **Rename** `thm-subapce-sum-theorem` to `thm-subspace-sum`.

- **Dimension formula** `thm-dimension-formula-subspace-dim`, by the **named move**:

  Keep the TikZ inclusion diagram.
- **Direct sum** `def-direct-sum`, defined for **k subspaces** as: every vector in the sum has a **unique** decomposition.

## 08-infinite-dimensional.md — Infinite-dimensional spaces

**New section**, ≈2,500 words.

- **Hook:** \( F[x] \), sequences and continuous functions have no finite basis. Do they have a basis at all?
- **Independence and span for arbitrary subsets** (recall 03).
  - **Basis of F[x]:** \( 1, x, x^2, \dots \).
  - **Sequence space** \( F^\nN \): the vectors \( \e_i \) are independent but **do not span**. The sequence (1, 1, 1, …) is not a finite combination: a warning about "infinite sums".
- **Zorn's Lemma**, stated (`thm-zorn`) with the definitions of partial order, chain and upper bound. No proof: say it is equivalent to the axiom of choice.
- **Every vector space has a basis** — `thm-every-space-has-basis`. More generally, every independent set extends to a basis — `thm-basis-extension-general`. Proof via Zorn on independent sets containing S.
- **Existence is non-constructive:** ℝ over ℚ has a basis (a Hamel basis), but nobody can write one down. ℝ over ℚ is infinite-dimensional. Proof: a finite-dimensional ℚ-space is countable; take the countability of ℚⁿ as known from a brief remark, or give a short argument.
- **Warnings:**
  - finite-dimensional facts that fail. An injective map from V to V need not be onto (shift on sequences: forward pointer to Ch 2; state it only in terms of subspaces here). A proper subspace can be "as big as" the space: the sequences with first entry 0 inside \( F^\nN \).
  - dim = ∞ is not a single size. Keep this as a one-sentence remark about cardinality, without developing it.
- **Scope statement:** the rest of the book is mostly finite-dimensional, and results say so.
- **Check:** is \( (1, x, x^2, \dots) \) a basis of the space of all power series? (No.)
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** show the sequences with finitely many non-zero terms have basis \( (\e_i) \); show \( \{ e^{kx} : k \in \nN \} \) is independent in \( \nR^\nR \) (hint: take the largest k and divide, or use growth); show the space of continuous functions on [0, 1] is infinite-dimensional.
