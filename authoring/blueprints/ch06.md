## 01-division.md — Division with remainder

- **Hook:** long division of polynomials, done by hand. Why does it always stop, and why is the answer unique?
- **Theorem** `thm-polynomial-division`: for f, g ∈ F[x] with g ≠ 0, there are **unique** q, r with f = qg + r and deg r < deg g.
  - **Existence:** induction on deg f, or well-ordering on the degrees of f − qg.
  - **Uniqueness:** compare degrees, using `thm-degree-of-product` (Ch 0).
  - **Bold "over a field":** the leading coefficient of g must be invertible. **Warning:** fails over ℤ (x² divided by 2x).
- **Worked long division** — `exm-long-division`; verify with sympy.
- **Remainder and Factor Theorems in general** — `thm-remainder-theorem`: the remainder of f on division by x − c is f(c). Recall Ch 3 `lem-factor-theorem-linear` and say this is the same statement, now as a corollary of general division.
- **Root bound in general** — `cor-root-bound-general`: a non-zero f of degree n has at most n roots. Recall `lem-root-bound`.
- **Polynomial functions** — `thm-polynomial-function-determines-polynomial`: over an infinite field, equal polynomial functions come from equal polynomials. Pays off Ch 0 (the text there now points to Ch 3 §07; acknowledge that source).
- **Divisibility** — `def-divisibility-polynomials`: associates are non-zero scalar multiples, and monic representatives.
- **Check.**

  - **B:** divide with remainder (over ℚ and over 𝔽₅); find the remainder of x¹⁰⁰ on division by x² − 1 without long division; show x − c divides xⁿ − cⁿ.

## 02-ideals-and-gcd.md — Ideals and greatest common divisors

- **Hook:** the set of all combinations af + bg has a smallest-degree non-zero member, and it divides everything in the set.
- **Ideal** `def-ideal-polynomials`: a non-empty subset closed under addition and under multiplication by **any** polynomial. Examples: ⟨p⟩; the polynomials vanishing at a point; {p : p(T) = 0} previewed for Ch 9. Non-example: F[x]≤n (a subspace but not an ideal).
- **Every ideal is principal** — `thm-ideals-principal`: I = ⟨d⟩ with d the unique monic generator of least degree (or I = {0}). Proof by division.
- **gcd** `def-gcd-polynomials`: the monic generator of ⟨f, g⟩.
  - `thm-gcd-properties`: it divides f and g, and every common divisor divides it — so it is the "greatest" in the divisibility sense.
  - **Bézout** — `cor-bezout-polynomials`: d = af + bg.
- **Euclidean algorithm** — `thm-euclidean-algorithm`, with a correctness proof. Worked `exm-euclid-polynomials` with back-substitution for a, b; verify with sympy.
- **Coprime** — `def-coprime`: gcd = 1 ⇔ af + bg = 1 has a solution. `thm-coprime-divides-product`: p | qr and gcd(p, q) = 1 ⇒ p | r.
- **lcm**, briefly; gcd · lcm = fg for monic f, g (exercise).
- **Warning:** the gcd does not depend on the field extension. gcd over ℚ = gcd over ℂ, since the Euclidean algorithm stays in ℚ[x]. Contrast: irreducibility **does** depend on the field.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** gcd and Bézout coefficients for two cubics; show a set is/isn't an ideal; gcd(xᵐ − 1, xⁿ − 1) = x^{gcd(m,n)} − 1 (with hint: the Euclidean algorithm on exponents).
  - **C:** the ideal of polynomials vanishing on a finite set S is ⟨∏_{s∈S}(x − s)⟩; F[x, y] has a non-principal ideal ⟨x, y⟩ (explain the failure precisely).

## 03-unique-factorization.md — Irreducible polynomials and unique factorization

- **Irreducible** `def-irreducible-polynomial`: non-constant, only trivial factorizations. **Bold "non-constant"** (units are not irreducible).
  - **Examples:** linear polynomials always; x² + 1 over ℝ but not ℂ; x² − 2 over ℚ but not ℝ; x² + x + 1 over 𝔽₂.
  - **Degree 2 and 3** — `thm-irreducible-deg-2-3`: reducible iff it has a root.
  - **Warning:** degree 4 without roots can be reducible, (x² + 1)² over ℝ.
- **Euclid's lemma** — `thm-euclid-lemma-polynomials`: p irreducible, p | qr ⇒ p | q or p | r. Proof via gcd.
- **Unique factorization** — `thm-unique-factorization-polynomials`: every non-constant f = c·p₁⋯p_k with monic irreducibles, unique up to order. Existence by strong induction on degree; uniqueness by Euclid's lemma and induction. Pays off Ch 0's mention.
- **Rational Root Theorem** — `thm-rational-root`, as a tool over ℚ. Worked irreducibility over ℚ of a cubic.
- **Eisenstein's criterion:** statement, with the proof as a C exercise scaffolded via reduction mod p. It needs Gauss's lemma, stated with proof in the exercise.
- **Factoring the same polynomial** over ℚ, ℝ, ℂ, 𝔽₂ and 𝔽₃: a worked table for x⁴ − 1 — `exm-factor-x4-minus-1`. Verify with sympy.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** decide irreducibility of listed polynomials over ℚ, ℝ, 𝔽₂, 𝔽₃; factor x⁶ − 1 over ℚ; list all irreducible polynomials of degree ≤ 3 over 𝔽₂.
  - **C:** there are infinitely many monic irreducibles over any field (Euclid's argument); Eisenstein with Gauss's lemma, scaffolded.

## 04-roots-and-multiplicity.md — Roots, multiplicity and derivatives

- **Multiplicity** `def-root-multiplicity`: the largest m with (x − c)^m | f.
  - `thm-roots-with-multiplicity`: the sum of multiplicities ≤ deg f; equality ⇔ f splits into linear factors over F.
- **Formal derivative** `def-formal-derivative`, linear with the product rule — `thm-formal-derivative-rules`, proved on monomials and extended by linearity (cite Ch 2 linear maps: D is a linear map, `exm-differentiation`).
- **Repeated root test** — `thm-repeated-root-derivative`: c is a multiple root ⇔ f(c) = f′(c) = 0. `cor-squarefree-gcd-derivative`: f has a repeated irreducible factor ⇒ gcd(f, f′) ≠ 1; the converse needs care in characteristic p.
  - **Warning:** in characteristic p, f′ = 0 is possible for non-constant f (x^p). State the correct version: if gcd(f, f′) = 1 then f has no repeated factor (any field); the converse holds in characteristic 0.
- **Taylor expansion** at c for polynomials over characteristic 0 — `thm-polynomial-taylor`, via the basis (1, x − c, …) of F[x]≤n (Ch 1 basis). Link: the dual basis from Ch 5 is \( p \mapsto p^{(k)}(c)/k! \).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find multiplicities; decide whether x⁴ − 2x² + 1 has repeated roots via gcd with f′; the multiplicity of 1 in xⁿ − nx + n − 1.
  - **C:** a polynomial over ℝ with all roots real has a derivative with all roots real (Rolle, stated as known); x^p − x over 𝔽_p has no repeated roots (derivative = −1).

## 05-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md — The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

- **Goal stated first:** every non-constant complex polynomial has a complex root. Recall Ch 0 `thm-fta-statement`.
- **Honest boundary box** (`.remark` titled "What we assume from analysis"): the extreme value theorem for continuous functions on a closed disc in ℂ ≅ ℝ², and continuity of polynomial functions. Nothing else.
- **Step 1:** |p(z)| → ∞ — `lem-polynomial-growth`, with an explicit radius R.
- **Step 2:** |p| attains a global minimum at some z₀ — `lem-minimum-modulus-attained`.
- **Step 3 (d'Alembert's lemma)** — `lem-dalembert`: if p(z₀) ≠ 0, there is z with |p(z)| < |p(z₀)|.
  - **Idea:** a picture of moving in the direction where the first non-zero term pushes toward 0.
  - Uses the existence of k-th roots in ℂ (Ch 0 `thm-de-moivre`).
- **FTA** — `thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra`. Replace the Ch 0 statement link: say "this proves `thm-fta-statement`".
- **Consequences:**
  - `cor-complex-polynomial-splits`: every complex polynomial splits.
  - **Real polynomials:** non-real roots come in conjugate pairs with equal multiplicity — `thm-real-roots-conjugate-pairs`.
  - **Irreducibles over ℝ** have degree 1 or 2 — `cor-real-irreducibles`; every real polynomial factors into linear and quadratic factors — `cor-real-factorization`.
  - Odd-degree real polynomials have a real root (from the factorization, no IVT needed).
- **Warning:** the FTA is an existence theorem; there is no formula for roots of degree ≥ 5 (stated, no proof). Numerical root-finding is not this theorem.
- **Forward pointer:** Ch 9 uses FTA to give every complex operator an eigenvalue. An alternative purely linear-algebra proof exists; mention only, no attribution.
- **Check.**

  - **B:** factor x⁴ + 4 over ℝ and ℂ; find the real factorization of x⁶ + 1; show a real cubic with one real root has a conjugate pair.

## 06-interpolation-and-crt.md — Interpolation and the Chinese Remainder Theorem

- **Lagrange basis** — `def-lagrange-basis`: \( \ell_i(x) = \prod_{j\neq i}(x − c_j)/(c_i − c_j) \). `thm-lagrange-interpolation`: the formula for the unique interpolant; the ℓᵢ form a basis of F[x]≤n. Pays off the Ch 3 pointer: recall `thm-interpolation-unique`.
- **Duality link:** the evaluation functionals at the cᵢ are the dual basis of (ℓᵢ). Recall Ch 5 `thm-dual-basis`.
- **Chinese Remainder Theorem for polynomials** — `thm-crt-polynomials`: for pairwise coprime m₁, …, m_k and any r₁, …, r_k, there is f ≡ rᵢ mod mᵢ, unique mod ∏ mᵢ. Proof via Bézout. Lagrange interpolation is the case mᵢ = x − cᵢ.
- **Hermite interpolation** as a CRT instance with (x − cᵢ)^{kᵢ} — `exm-hermite-via-crt`, verified with sympy.
- **Linear algebra view:** F[x]/⟨m⟩ is a vector space of dimension deg m (link to Ch 4 quotients); CRT is an isomorphism F[x]/⟨∏mᵢ⟩ ≅ ⊕ F[x]/⟨mᵢ⟩ — `thm-crt-isomorphism`, dimension count plus injectivity.
- **Check.**

  - **C:** interpolation fails over 𝔽_p with more than p nodes (why); Newton's divided-difference form and its triangular basis.

## 07-polynomials-of-operators.md — Polynomials of an operator

- **Hook:** plug an operator into a polynomial; the arithmetic of F[x] becomes the structure of T.
- **Evaluation map** — `thm-evaluation-homomorphism`: F[x] → 𝓛(V), p ↦ p(T), is linear and multiplicative ((pq)(T) = p(T)q(T)), so p(T) and q(T) commute. Recall Ch 2 `def-polynomial-of-operator`.
- **Annihilating polynomials exist** — `thm-annihilating-polynomial-exists`: for dim V = n ≥ 1, some non-zero p with deg p ≤ n² kills T (dim 𝓛(V) = n², `thm-linear-maps-isomorphic-to-matrices`). The annihilating polynomials form an ideal — `thm-annihilator-ideal`, hence generated by a unique monic polynomial. **Name it only as a forward pointer ("the minimal polynomial, Chapter 9")**; the full definition lives in Ch 9. **Coordinator note:** Ch 9 will define `def-minimal-polynomial` and cite this theorem.
- **Invariance** — `thm-kernel-image-of-polynomial-invariant`: T maps ker p(T) and im p(T) into themselves, proved directly (p(T) commutes with T). The word "invariant subspace" is defined as a major notion in Ch 9; here use it only in a forward pointer.
- **Kernel splitting lemma** — `thm-kernel-splitting`: if gcd(p, q) = 1, then ker (pq)(T) = ker p(T) ⊕ ker q(T); moreover the projections onto the summands are polynomials in T. Proof via Bézout: 1 = ap + bq. **This is the engine of primary decomposition in Ch 9.** Idea block and a full proof; worked example with T² = T (p = x, q = x − 1), recovering Ch 2 `thm-projection-direct-sum`, and with T² = I (char ≠ 2), recovering `thm-involution-decomposition`.
- **Warning:** the coprimality hypothesis is essential. T = [[0,1],[0,0]] with p = q = x.
- **Check.**

  - **B:** compute p(A) for a 2×2 A and a quadratic p; find a non-zero polynomial killing a 3×3 matrix by finding a dependence among I, A, A², A³ (Ch 3 toolkit); use the splitting lemma for T³ = T.
  - **C:** if T is invertible, T⁻¹ is a polynomial in T; if p(T) = 0 and p(0) ≠ 0 then T is invertible; the splitting lemma for k pairwise coprime factors.
