# Blueprint: Chapter 0 — Preliminaries

**Directory:** `src/ch00-foundations/` (the name stays, for URL stability). **Chapter title:** "Preliminaries".

**Purpose.** This chapter holds the language, proof habits and basic objects (sets, functions, relations, complex numbers, fields, polynomials, matrices, groups) that the rest of the book uses. It is written for a reader who has seen matrices computationally but not proof-based mathematics.

**Tone.** Friendly and brisk, but never skip the careful points that later chapters rely on. These are:
- negating quantifiers;
- implication vs converse;
- well-definedness on equivalence classes;
- a polynomial is not a function;
- matrix multiplication is not commutative, and cancellation fails.

**Rewrite, not patch.** The old files are the starting point for label names only. Read them to keep every existing label below that still fits. Rewrite the prose to `authoring/STYLE.md`. Old files that get renamed are deleted: the new file replaces them.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Nothing earlier in the book. School algebra, ℝ as familiar numbers, and informal use of ℕ, ℤ, ℚ are all fine. Chapter 0 must **not** use vector spaces, linear maps, determinants or eigenvalues. It may mention them in forward pointers ("in Chapter 3, …").

## 01-logic.md — Statements, implications and quantifiers

**Keep labels:** `def-quantifiers`, `exm-quantifiers`, `exm-nested-quantifiers`, `def-negation-of-quantifiers`, `exm-negation-basic`, `exm-negation-injectivity`, `exm-negation-surjectivity`.

- **Statements and connectives.** and / or (inclusive) / not, with truth tables in a small Markdown table.
  - **Implication** P ⇒ Q: vacuous truth, with the example "if n is odd and even, then n = 7".
  - **Converse vs contrapositive** — `def-implication-converse-contrapositive`. Theorem `thm-contrapositive-equivalent`: P ⇒ Q is equivalent to ¬Q ⇒ ¬P, via the truth table.
  - **Warning:** the converse can fail. Use "if A is invertible then Ax = 0 has only the trivial solution" as a forward pointer, and a numeric example: "x = 2 ⇒ x² = 4" has a false converse.
  - **If and only if.**
- **Quantifiers** ∀, ∃, and the order of quantifiers. `exm-nested-quantifiers`: "∀x ∃y: y > x" vs "∃y ∀x: y > x" over ℝ.
- **Negation rules** — `def-negation-of-quantifiers`. De Morgan for and/or. ¬(P ⇒ Q) ≡ P ∧ ¬Q.
  - **Worked negations:** injectivity, surjectivity, "S is linearly independent" as a forward pointer written purely formally ("there exist scalars not all zero with …"). Emphasize **not all zero** ≠ **all non-zero**.
- **Warning:** "not all zero" vs "all non-zero".
- **Checks:** negate "every student passed some exam"; is "∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 …" preserved under swapping?
- **Exercises:**
  - **A:** truth of statements; converse/contrapositive of a given implication; negate a quantified statement.
  - **B:** negate three definitions (bounded function, injective, "every vector is a combination of v₁, v₂"); determine the truth of nested-quantifier statements over ℤ vs ℝ.
  - **C:** show P ⇒ (Q ⇒ R) ≡ (P ∧ Q) ⇒ R; find P, Q making both P ⇒ Q and its converse false.

## 02-proofs.md — How proofs are built

**New section.**

- **Direct proof.** Example: the sum of two even integers is even.
- **Contrapositive.** Example: n² even ⇒ n even.
- **Contradiction.** Example: √2 is irrational (`thm-sqrt2-irrational`). Also "there are infinitely many primes" as a checkable example.
- **Proof by cases.** Example: n² + n is even.
- **Proving iff:** two directions (⇒)/(⇐).
- **Existence and uniqueness.** "Suppose x and x′ both satisfy …, show x = x′." Example: the identity element of a binary operation is unique (preview of fields).
- **Counterexamples.** To refute ∀, one witness suffices. Example: "every prime is odd".
- **Mathematical induction** — `thm-induction`, stated as a principle, not proved.
  - **Strong induction** — `thm-strong-induction`.
  - **Well-ordering principle** — `thm-well-ordering`, stated. Example: every n ≥ 2 has a prime factor (strong induction or well-ordering).
  - **Examples:** 1 + 2 + ⋯ + n = n(n+1)/2; 2ⁿ > n.
  - **Warning:** the "all horses are the same colour" fake induction — the step fails from n = 1 to 2.
- **Writing conventions** the book follows:
  - openers "Let … / Suppose …";
  - each step names its reason;
  - end with "This shows …";
  - no "obviously" hiding a gap.

  Include a short paragraph "How to read a proof in this book": the Idea block, then the Proof.
- **Check:** where does the fake induction break?
- **Exercises:**
  - **A:** name the technique for four statements.
  - **B:** contrapositive (if n² is odd then n is odd); induction (sum of the first n odd numbers is n²); contradiction (√3 irrational).
  - **C:** strong induction (every integer n ≥ 12 is 4a + 5b with a, b ≥ 0); well-ordering proof of a division-with-remainder statement for integers (this previews Ch 5).

- **Exercises:**

## 04-functions.md — Functions

**Replaces** `03-functions.md`. **Keep labels:** `def-function`, `def-injective-surjective-bijective`, `exm-injective-surjective-bijective`, `def-composition`, `exm-composition`, `thm-composition-associative`, `thm-composition-preserves`, `def-identity-function`, `def-inverse-function`, `thm-bijective-iff-invertible`, `exm-inverse-function`, `thm-inverse-of-composition`.

- **Function.** Domain, codomain, rule; equality of functions (same domain, codomain and values).
  - **Image and preimage** of sets — `def-image-preimage`.
  - **Warning:** f(f⁻¹(B)) ⊆ B may be strict, and f⁻¹(f(A)) ⊇ A may be strict. Give examples with x ↦ x².
- **Injective, surjective, bijective.** Worked checks on ℝ → ℝ maps, and the same rule with different codomains.
  - **Non-example by minimal change:** x ↦ x² on ℝ vs on [0, ∞).
  - **Standard move:** "to show injective, suppose f(a) = f(b)".
- **Composition.**
  - Associativity (`thm-composition-associative`).
  - Composition of injections/surjections/bijections (`thm-composition-preserves`).
  - Partial converses: g∘f injective ⇒ f injective; g∘f surjective ⇒ g surjective — `thm-composition-partial-converse`.
- **Identity, inverses.**
  - **Left and right inverses** — `def-left-right-inverse`. Theorem `thm-left-inverse-iff-injective` (for a nonempty domain) and `thm-right-inverse-iff-surjective`. The latter uses a choice of preimage; mention in one sentence that this uses the axiom of choice for infinite sets.

  - (g∘f)⁻¹ = f⁻¹∘g⁻¹ (`thm-inverse-of-composition`).
- **Finite sets:** a map between sets of the same finite size is injective ⇔ surjective — `thm-finite-injective-iff-surjective` (pigeonhole). This is the ancestor of "count instead of check" in Ch 3.
- **Checks:** give a function with a left inverse but no right inverse.
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** determine injective/surjective for four maps; find inverses; prove the partial converse.
  - **C:** show that a left inverse of a non-surjective map is not unique; prove that if X is finite, f: X → X injective ⇒ bijective, and give a counterexample for ℕ.

## 06-complex-numbers.md — Complex numbers

**New section.**

- **ℂ as ℝ²** with multiplication (a, b)(c, d) = (ac − bd, ad + bc); i² = −1.
- **Real and imaginary parts, conjugate, modulus.**
  - Theorem `thm-conjugate-properties`: conj(z + w), conj(zw), z·conj(z) = |z|², |zw| = |z||w|.
  - **Triangle inequality** — `thm-complex-triangle-inequality`, proved from |z + w|² = |z|² + 2 Re(z conj w) + |w|², with Re(u) ≤ |u|.
- **Inverse** 1/z = conj(z)/|z|² for z ≠ 0.
- **Polar form** and Euler's formula e^{iθ} (take cos θ + i sin θ as the definition of e^{iθ}).
  - Multiplication rotates and scales, with a TikZ picture.
  - De Moivre.
  - **n-th roots of unity** — `exm-roots-of-unity`, with a TikZ picture of the hexagon for n = 6. They sum to 0.
- **Fundamental Theorem of Algebra** — stated only (`thm-fta-statement`), with "proved in Chapter 5".
  - **Consequence:** every complex polynomial of degree n ≥ 1 splits into linear factors (state; the proof uses Chapter 5 division).

  Keep this light: it is a forward pointer, not used in any proof in Ch 0–4.
- **Warning:** no ordering on ℂ compatible with the field operations; √(−1)·√(−1) ≠ √((−1)(−1)).
- **Checks:** compute (1 + i)⁸ via polar form.

## 07-fields.md — Fields

**Replaces** `06-fields.md`. **Keep labels:** `def-field`, `exm-fields`, `exm-finite-fields`.

- **Hook:** what properties of ℚ, ℝ, ℂ does Gaussian elimination use? Adding, multiplying, dividing by non-zero numbers. So we give such a number system a name.
- **Slogan:** a field is a place where you can add, subtract, multiply and divide by anything non-zero, with the usual rules.
- **Definition** `def-field`: the axioms labelled (F1)–(F9) using the `.enumerate` label list (the old file used `(F\arabic*)`). **Bold 0 ≠ 1.**
- **Consequences** — `thm-field-basic-properties`: uniqueness of 0, 1, negatives and inverses; 0·a = 0; (−1)a = −a; ab = 0 ⇒ a = 0 or b = 0; cancellation. Prove several; the rest are exercises.
- **Examples:** ℚ, ℝ, ℂ.
  - **ℚ(√2)** — `exm-q-sqrt2-field`: closure and inverse via rationalizing. Keep `exm-q-adjoin-sqrt2`? No: that label lives in ch01, so leave it.
  - **𝔽_p = ℤ/pℤ** — `exm-finite-fields` with the construction via 05-relations. Theorem `thm-zp-field-iff-prime`: ℤ/nℤ is a field ⇔ n is prime. Proof:
    - (⇐) uses Bézout for integers. **State** Bézout for integers as a lemma (`lem-bezout-integers`) with a well-ordering proof (smallest positive element of {ax + ny}).
    - (⇒) zero divisors.
  - **𝔽₂ tables.**
  - **Degenerate:** the axiom 0 ≠ 1 rules out the one-element "field".
- **Non-examples:** ℤ (2 has no inverse, name the clause); M₂(ℝ) (not commutative, and non-zero non-invertible elements); ℤ/6ℤ (2·3 = 0).
- **Characteristic** — `def-characteristic`: the smallest n with n·1 = 0, else 0. `thm-characteristic-prime`: the characteristic is 0 or a prime.
  - **Warning:** in 𝔽₂, 1 + 1 = 0, so "x = −x ⇒ x = 0" fails. Forward pointer: this is why "char ≠ 2" appears in Ch 13.
- **Why the axioms:** division is what makes elimination and bases work. Forward pointer: over ℤ, "linear algebra" (modules) behaves differently.
- **Checks:** is {a + b√3 : a, b ∈ ℚ} a field? Inverse of 3 in 𝔽₇.
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** prove the remaining basic properties; solve 3x + 5 = 2 in 𝔽₇; decide which are fields (ℤ/9ℤ, ℚ(√2), {a + bi : a, b ∈ ℤ}, 𝔽₅).
  - **C:** build a 4-element field 𝔽₂[α] with α² = α + 1 (addition and multiplication tables) and show ℤ/4ℤ is not isomorphic to it (ℤ/4ℤ is not a field); show a finite field has characteristic p; show in a field of characteristic p, (a + b)^p = a^p + b^p (Frobenius). Hint: binomial coefficients.

## 08-polynomials.md — Polynomials

**Replaces** `05-polynomials.md`. **Keep labels:** `def-polynomial`, `def-degree`, `def-polynomial-ring`, `exm-elements-of-rx`, `def-polynomials-bounded-degree`, `exm-elements-of-rx-leq-2`, `def-polynomials-exact-degree`, `exm-comparing-notations`.

- **Polynomial over F.** Formally a finitely supported coefficient sequence, written a₀ + a₁x + ⋯. Two polynomials are equal iff all coefficients agree.
- **Degree.** **Convention deg 0 = −∞** and why: so that deg(pq) = deg p + deg q always holds.
  - Theorem `thm-degree-of-product` and `thm-degree-of-sum` (deg(p + q) ≤ max), with a proof. Uses "F has no zero divisors" from 07-fields.
  - F[x] has no zero divisors — `cor-polynomial-no-zero-divisors`.
  - F[x]≤n and F[x]=n (existing labels).
  - **Warning:** F[x]=n is not closed under addition. Forward pointer to subspaces.
- **Evaluation** p(c) and roots.
  - **Warning:** a polynomial is not a function. Over 𝔽₂, x² + x vanishes at every point but is not the zero polynomial (`exm-polynomial-vs-function-f2`).
  - **Forward pointer:** over an infinite field they do agree, proved in Ch 5.
- **Polynomials in a matrix**, mentioned only as a forward pointer; the definition comes after matrices.
- **Division with remainder**, stated as a forward pointer to Ch 5. Do not prove it here.
- **Checks:** degree of (2x + 1)(3x² + x) over 𝔽₆? (ℤ/6ℤ is not a field: the leading coefficient 2·3 = 0, so the degree drops. A nice warning that uses the field axiom.)
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** multiply/add and give degrees; show deg(p ∘ q) = deg p · deg q for non-constant p, q; list all polynomials of degree ≤ 2 over 𝔽₂ and which are functions equal to 0.
  - **C:** show a non-zero polynomial of degree n over a field has at most n roots **assuming the factor theorem** (p(c) = 0 ⇒ p = (x − c)q, stated as proved in Ch 5). Better: avoid it and ask to prove the factor theorem directly for a degree-2 case. Decide when writing, and state any assumption explicitly. ## 09-matrices.md — Matrices

**Replaces** `07-matrices.md`. **Keep labels:** `def-matrix`, `exm-matrices`, `def-zero-matrix`, `def-identity-matrix`, `def-transpose`, `def-symmetric-matrix`, `def-diagonal-matrix`, `def-upper-triangular`, `exm-zero-identity`, `exm-special-matrices`, `def-matrix-addition`, `def-additive-inverse`, `exm-matrix-addition`, `thm-matrix-addition-properties`, `def-matrix-multiplication`, `exm-matrix-multiplication`, `exm-matrix-multiplication-dimensions`, `thm-matrix-multiplication-properties`, `def-scalar-multiplication`, `def-trace`, `exm-trace`, `thm-transpose-properties`, `thm-trace-properties`. Also `exm-similarity` and `exm-row-equivalence`, moved here from relations and rewritten (see 05).

- **Matrices over F; M_{m×n}(F); entries.** Special matrices.
- **Addition and scalar multiplication**, with properties.
- **Matrix multiplication.**
  - **Hook:** composing two substitutions of variables (linear changes of variables) forces the row-times-column rule. Show with a 2×2 substitution.
  - **Three views** of AB: the entry formula; column j of AB = A·(column j of B); row i of AB = (row i of A)·B. Also Ax as a combination of the columns of A — `thm-matrix-times-vector-columns`. **This is essential** for Ch 2 and 3.
  - **Properties:** associativity (with proof via sums), distributivity, identity.
  - **Warnings:** AB ≠ BA (2×2 example); AB = 0 with A, B ≠ 0; AC = BC does not imply A = B.
- **Transpose;** (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ with proof. Symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices.
- **Trace;** tr(AB) = tr(BA) with proof. **Warning:** tr(ABC) ≠ tr(BAC) in general, with an example.
- **Inverse matrices** — `def-invertible-matrix`.
  - Uniqueness of the inverse; (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹; (Aᵀ)⁻¹ = (A⁻¹)ᵀ — `thm-inverse-matrix-properties`.
  - The 2×2 inverse formula with ad − bc ≠ 0 — `thm-two-by-two-inverse`. Prove it by multiplying out for the ⇐ direction; state that the converse (not invertible if ad − bc = 0) is shown by exhibiting a non-zero x with Ax = 0 — do it.
  - **Warning:** for square matrices AB = I ⇒ BA = I is **true** but not obvious; proved in Ch 3. Do not use it before then. Until then, "invertible" means both AB = I and BA = I.
- **Powers and polynomials of a square matrix** p(A) — `def-polynomial-of-matrix`. The constant term becomes c·I.
  - **Warning:** plugging A into a polynomial identity valid for numbers can fail when the matrices don't commute: (A + B)² ≠ A² + 2AB + B².
- **Similarity and row equivalence**, as the rewritten examples:
  - **similarity** "A ~ B iff B = P⁻¹AP for some invertible P" is an equivalence relation (`exm-similarity`), with a proof;
  - **row equivalence** — `exm-row-equivalence`: defined by "B = EA with E invertible", also an equivalence relation. Forward pointer to Ch 2 (Gaussian elimination).
- **Checks:** find 2×2 A ≠ 0 with A² = 0 (the matrix [[0,1],[0,0]]; mention it will reappear often).
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**

## 10-groups.md — Groups and permutations (brief)

**New section.** Short: ≈2,000–2,500 words.

- **Hook:** GL_n(F), the nonzero elements of a field under multiplication, rotations of a square, and bijections of a set all share one structure.
- **Group** — `def-group`. Abelian. Examples: (ℤ, +), (F, +), (F \ {0}, ·), GL_n(F) (defined here as the invertible n×n matrices), S_n.
  - **Non-examples:** (ℤ, ·); M_n(F) under multiplication.
- **Basic facts** — `thm-group-basic-properties`: unique identity, unique inverses, (ab)⁻¹ = b⁻¹a⁻¹, cancellation.
- **Subgroups** — `def-subgroup` with the test.
- **Homomorphisms and isomorphisms** — `def-group-homomorphism`. The kernel is a subgroup. Keep this very short.
- **Permutations:** S_n, cycle notation, composition order (the right-hand permutation is applied first).
  - **Transpositions generate S_n**, stated with a proof by induction on n — `thm-transpositions-generate`.
  - **Sign** is **not** defined here; that is Ch 6.
- **Warning:** composition order in S_n and in matrix multiplication.
- **Check:** compute (1 2 3)(1 2) as a product in S₃.
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** Cayley table of S₃ (or a partial table); show SL₂-type subsets are subgroups (upper triangular invertible 2×2 matrices); a homomorphism ℝ → ℝ_{>0}, x ↦ eˣ.
  - **C:** a group where every element satisfies g² = e is abelian; order of a product of disjoint cycles.
