# Blueprint: Chapter 7 — Block Matrices

**Directory:** `src/ch07-block-matrices/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Block Matrices".

**Purpose.** Partitioning a matrix into blocks turns large computations into small ones and makes structure visible. It shows direct sums as block-diagonal matrices and invariant subspaces as block-triangular ones. This chapter builds block arithmetic rigorously and uses it for:
- rank inequalities;
- Schur complements (inverses and determinants);
- the Kronecker product;
- commutators and trace-zero matrices, ending with Shoda's theorem (every trace-zero matrix over a field of characteristic 0 is a commutator).

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–6: ranks (Ch 2), matrices of maps, similarity, quotients and trace (Ch 3), determinants including `thm-det-block-triangular` (Ch 6 §08). **No eigenvalues** (forward pointers only), with one exception: Shoda's theorem needs "every matrix is similar to one with a zero first diagonal entry unless it is scalar", which is proved directly with cyclic-vector-free arguments here.

**Notation:**
- block matrices \( \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} \);
- \( A \oplus B \) for block-diagonal \( \operatorname{diag}(A, B) \) (add to NOTATION.md: "direct sum of matrices"; note the relation to direct sums of spaces);
- Schur complement \( M/A = D - CA^{-1}B \);
- Kronecker \( A \otimes B \) (already in NOTATION.md);
- \( \operatorname{vec}(X) \) (add a macro `\vecop` rendering "vec");
- commutator \( [A, B] = AB - BA \).

## 01-block-multiplication.md — Partitions and block multiplication

- **Hook:** multiplying two 4×4 matrices whose top-right 2×2 blocks are zero. Most of the work disappears.
- **Partition** — `def-block-partition`: row and column partitions; conformable partitions.
- **Block multiplication** — `thm-block-multiplication`: products computed blockwise, with a full index proof via the entry formula (Ch 0 `def-matrix-multiplication`). The **order of block products matters**: \( AE + BG \), not \( EA + GB \).
- **Special cases:**
  - columns/rows as blocks recover Ch 0 `thm-three-views-of-product`;
  - the outer-product expansion \( AB = \sum_k \mathbf{a}_k \mathbf{b}_k^{\mathsf T} \) (column k of A times row k of B) — `cor-outer-product-expansion`.
- **Block diagonal** — `def-block-diagonal`: products, powers, inverses and polynomials act blockwise (`thm-block-diagonal-arithmetic`).
- **Block triangular:** products stay block triangular; the inverse of an invertible block upper triangular matrix — `thm-block-triangular-inverse` — has an explicit formula when A and D are invertible.
- **Maps in adapted bases:**
  - `thm-direct-sum-block-diagonal`: if V = U ⊕ W and T maps U into U and W into W, then [T] in a basis adapted to the decomposition is block diagonal;
  - `thm-invariant-subspace-block-triangular`: if T maps U into itself, [T] is block upper triangular, with the lower-right block the matrix of the induced map on V/U. Recall Ch 3 §09 exercise and pay it off; the phrase "invariant subspace" is given its official major-notion definition in Ch 8, so here say "T maps U into U".
- **Warning:** blocks do not commute. \( \det \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} \neq \det(AD - BC) \) in general, with an example (verify).

## 02-rank-of-block-matrices.md — Rank and block matrices

- **Rank inequalities** via blocks:
  - rank [A B] ≤ rank A + rank B;
  - rank [[A, 0], [0, D]] = rank A + rank D;
  - rank [[A, B], [0, D]] ≥ rank A + rank D;
  - `thm-block-rank-inequalities`.
- **Sylvester and Frobenius** revisited in block form — `thm-frobenius-rank-block-proof`: a proof of Frobenius's inequality via the rank of a 2×2 block matrix and block row/column operations. Recall Ch 3 `thm-sylvester-rank-inequality` and the Ch 3 §08 exercise.
- **Block elimination** — `thm-block-elimination-rank`: multiplying on the left or right by invertible block triangular matrices preserves rank; clearing blocks.
- **Rank of AB − BA-type constructions:** a worked example.

## 03-block-determinants-and-schur-complements.md — Schur complements

- **Hook:** solve [[A, B], [C, D]][x; y] = [f; g] by eliminating x. The matrix D − CA⁻¹B appears.
- **Schur complement** — `def-schur-complement`: M/A = D − CA⁻¹B (A invertible) and M/D = A − BD⁻¹C.
- **Block LDU factorization** — `thm-block-ldu`: M = [[I, 0], [CA⁻¹, I]] [[A, 0], [0, M/A]] [[I, A⁻¹B], [0, I]]. Verify by block multiplication.
- **Consequences:**
  - `thm-schur-determinant`: det M = det A · det(M/A); cites Ch 6 `thm-det-block-triangular` and multiplicativity.
  - `thm-schur-rank`: rank M = rank A + rank(M/A).
  - `thm-block-inverse-formula`: M is invertible ⇔ M/A is, with the explicit inverse; the lower-right block of M⁻¹ is (M/A)⁻¹.
- **Matrix determinant lemma** revisited and **Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury** — `thm-woodbury`: (A + UCV)⁻¹, proved by comparing the two Schur complements of one block matrix. Recall Ch 6 `thm-matrix-determinant-lemma`.
- **Commuting blocks:** if CD = DC, then det M = det(AD − BC) (`thm-det-commuting-blocks`); proof via D invertible first, then a polynomial/perturbation argument over an infinite field, with a finite-field caveat or an algebraic proof. Choose a rigorous route (over any field: adjoin an indeterminate x, use D + xI and the fact that det(D + xI) is a monic, hence non-zero, polynomial; cite Ch 6's commutative-ring remarks).
- **Worked** 4×4 inverse via Schur complements — `exm-block-inverse-4x4` (verify with sympy).
- **Warning:** M/A needs A invertible. M can be invertible while A is not (e.g. [[0, 1], [1, 0]]); then use M/D or a permutation first.
- **Forward pointers:** positive definiteness via Schur complements (Ch 12); Gaussian elimination as repeated Schur complements (Ch 23).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute a Schur complement and det; apply Woodbury to a rank-one update; the inverse of [[I, B], [0, I]].
  - **C:** det(I_m + AB) = det(I_n + BA) (Sylvester's determinant identity) via one block matrix; the leading-principal-submatrix criterion for LU revisited (Ch 2 §06 exercise) with Schur complements.

## 04-kronecker-product.md — The Kronecker product

- **Hook:** the matrix equation AX = XB, or AXB = C, as a linear system in the entries of X.
- **Definition** `def-kronecker-product`.
- **Properties** — `thm-kronecker-properties`: bilinear, associative; (A ⊗ B)(C ⊗ D) = AC ⊗ BD (the mixed product rule, with a proof by blocks); transpose; inverse.
- `thm-kronecker-rank-trace-det`: rank multiplies; tr(A ⊗ B) = tr A tr B; det(A ⊗ B) = (det A)^m (det B)^n for A n×n, B m×m. Proof via A ⊗ B = (A ⊗ I)(I ⊗ B) and block structure; det(A ⊗ I_m) = (det A)^m by a permutation to block diagonal (careful sign argument, or via elementary matrices).
- **vec** — `def-vec-operator`; `thm-vec-identity`: vec(AXB) = (Bᵀ ⊗ A) vec X. **Worked:** solve AX + XB = C as a linear system for 2×2 matrices (`exm-sylvester-equation-vec`, verified with sympy). Forward pointer: solvability of AX − XB = C in terms of eigenvalues in Ch 11/19.
- **Warning:** A ⊗ B ≠ B ⊗ A (but they are permutation-similar — `prp-kronecker-swap-similar`).
- **Forward pointer:** tensor products of maps in Ch 14 are the basis-free version.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** compute Kronecker products; verify the mixed product rule; vec form of AX = B.
  - **C:** (A ⊗ B)⁻¹ = A⁻¹ ⊗ B⁻¹; Kronecker sums A ⊗ I + I ⊗ B and the equation AX + XBᵀ = C.

## 05-commutators-and-shoda.md — Commutators and trace-zero matrices

- **Commutator** `def-commutator`; tr [A, B] = 0.
  - Question: is every trace-zero matrix a commutator?
  - Pays off Ch 3's Heisenberg exercise: I is never a commutator in characteristic 0.
- **Lemma** `lem-nonscalar-similar-zero-diagonal-entry`: a non-scalar matrix is similar to one with a zero (1,1) entry — and more: `lem-trace-zero-similar-zero-diagonal` (over a field of characteristic 0, or with |F| large enough: state the precise hypothesis used), every trace-zero matrix is similar to one with all diagonal entries zero. Proof by induction via a vector v with v, Av independent (exists when A is not scalar) and a block reduction.
- **Shoda's theorem** — `thm-shoda`: over a field of characteristic 0, A is a commutator [X, Y] ⇔ tr A = 0. Proof:
  - take A with zero diagonal (lemma);
  - X = diag(x₁, …, xₙ) with distinct entries;
  - solve [X, Y] = A entrywise: y_ij = a_ij/(x_i − x_j) for i ≠ j.
  - Needs n distinct elements of F: fine in characteristic 0.
- **Remark:** the statement holds over every field; the proof is harder, and we do not give it.
- **Consequence:** the subspace spanned by commutators is exactly the trace-zero matrices (any field), proved directly with matrix units.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** write a given trace-zero 2×2 matrix as a commutator; show the span of commutators is sl_n (matrix units); over 𝔽₂, find a trace-zero 2×2 matrix and write it as a commutator by hand.
  - **C:** a linear functional φ on M_n(F) with φ(AB) = φ(BA) is a multiple of trace (recall Ch 3 §10 exercise); if [A, [A, B]] = 0 then [A, B] is nilpotent (scaffold the trace argument, characteristic 0).
