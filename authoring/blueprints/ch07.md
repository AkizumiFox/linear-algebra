# Blueprint: Chapter 7 — Determinants

**Directory:** `src/ch07-determinants/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Determinants".

**Purpose.** Build the determinant from what it must do, then prove everything it does. The route:
- signed volume motivates three properties: multilinear, alternating, normalized;
- permutations and sign;
- uniqueness and existence via the Leibniz formula;
- row operations and multiplicativity;
- cofactor expansion, the adjugate and Cramer's rule;
- minors and rank, Cauchy–Binet;
- special determinants;
- orientation and volume;
- the characteristic polynomial.

**Old draft:** `drafts/ch03-determinants.md` has only `thm-det-properties`. Read it; keep that label if its content fits `05`, then delete the draft file when this chapter lands (coordinator does the deletion).

**Signature moves:**
- **No Sarrus as a proof tool** (warn: it does not generalize to 4×4).
- **Determinant facts from the definition**, never from a formula that presupposes them (no circularity).
- **Multiplicativity via uniqueness:** for fixed A, the map B ↦ det(AB) is alternating multilinear, so it is det(A)·det(B).
- **Elementary matrices as building blocks** (alternative proof).
- **Induction on size** for special determinants.

**Commutative rings.** The characteristic polynomial needs determinants of matrices with entries in F[x]. The chapter works over a field F, but §04–§06 must **flag explicitly** (in one remark each) which proofs use only addition, subtraction and multiplication (no division). §10 then applies those results over F[x]. The Leibniz formula, uniqueness, multiplicativity via uniqueness, transpose invariance and cofactor expansion all qualify; results via elimination do not. Do not introduce ring theory beyond "a commutative ring: the field axioms minus inverses" (a one-paragraph remark in §04).

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–6. S_n and transpositions (Ch 0 `thm-transpositions-generate`); elementary matrices and the TFAE (Ch 3); linear maps (Ch 2); matrices of maps, similarity (Ch 4); polynomials (Ch 6). **No eigenvalues** (forward pointers only).

**Notation:**
- \( \det A \), \( \lvert A \rvert \) avoided (clash with absolute value);
- \( \sgn \sigma \);
- \( M_{ij} \) for the minor (the determinant of A with row i and column j deleted, described in words), and \( C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}M_{ij} \) for the cofactor;
- \( \adj A \);
- \( p_A(x) = \det(xI - A) \) (monic).

Add rows to NOTATION.md for M_ij and C_ij.

## 01-area-and-volume.md — Signed area and volume

## 02-multilinear-alternating-forms.md — Multilinear alternating forms

- **Definitions:**
  - `def-multilinear-form`: linear in each argument separately.
  - `def-alternating-form`: zero whenever two arguments are **equal** (any two positions, not necessarily adjacent: state the definition this way).
  - `def-skew-symmetric-form`: swapping two arguments changes the sign.
- **Theorem** `thm-alternating-properties`, for alternating multilinear forms:
  - swapping changes the sign;
  - adding a multiple of one argument to another leaves the value unchanged;
  - scaling an argument scales the value;
  - dependent arguments give 0.
- **Warning:** alternating ⇒ skew-symmetric always; skew-symmetric ⇒ alternating needs char ≠ 2. Over 𝔽₂, (u, v) ↦ u₁v₁ is skew-symmetric and not alternating (verify).
- **Examples:**
  - n = 2 forms on F² (a 1-dimensional space of them);
  - the "cross-product component" forms on F³;
  - **non-example:** the dot product (bilinear, not alternating).
- **The space** of alternating n-linear forms on Fⁿ is a vector space — `thm-alternating-forms-space`. Its dimension is 1, proved in §04 (forward pointer within the chapter).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** decide multilinear / alternating for listed functions; show that an alternating bilinear form on F² is c(ad − bc); show that f(u, v, w) = u · (v × w) is alternating.
  - **C:** an alternating k-linear form on V with k > dim V is zero; the char-2 distinction worked fully.

## 03-permutations-and-sign.md — Permutations and sign

- **Recall** S_n, cycle notation and transpositions from Ch 0 (`thm-transpositions-generate`).
- **Inversions** `def-inversion`; **sign** `def-sign-permutation`: sgn σ = (−1)^{inversions}.
- **Theorem** `thm-sign-multiplicative`: sgn(στ) = sgn σ · sgn τ. Proof via the product formula \( \prod_{i<j} (x_{\sigma(j)} - x_{\sigma(i)})/(x_j - x_i) \) over ℚ (or via counting pairs); choose one rigorous route and give the Idea.
- **Consequences:**
  - `cor-sign-transposition`: sgn of a transposition = −1;
  - `cor-parity-well-defined`: σ is a product of an even number of transpositions or of an odd number, never both. **Pays off the Ch 0 promise.**
  - The sign of a k-cycle is (−1)^{k−1}.
- **Sign is a group homomorphism** S_n → {±1} (recall Ch 0 `def-group-homomorphism`); the alternating group A_n is its kernel.
- **Worked:** the sign of a permutation via its cycle type and via inversion count (`exm-sign-two-ways`); verify with python.
- **Warning:** "number of transpositions" is not unique, only its parity. Composition order in S_n (Ch 0).
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** signs of listed permutations; show sgn σ⁻¹ = sgn σ; count inversions of the reversal permutation (n(n−1)/2).
  - **C:** the number of even permutations is n!/2 (n ≥ 2); sgn σ = (−1)^{n − number of cycles}.

## 04-existence-and-uniqueness.md — Existence and uniqueness of the determinant

- **Goal:** there is exactly one alternating n-linear form D on Fⁿ with D(e₁, …, eₙ) = 1.
- **Uniqueness** — `thm-alternating-form-uniqueness`: any alternating n-linear f satisfies f(a₁, …, aₙ) = f(e₁, …, eₙ) · Σ_σ sgn σ ∏ a_{σ(j)j}. Proof by expanding multilinearity (terms with repeated indices vanish; the rest reorder with sign). An Idea block with the 2×2 expansion written out.
- **Existence** — `thm-leibniz-formula-alternating`: the Leibniz sum is multilinear and alternating. Proof: linear in each column; if two columns are equal, pair σ with σ∘(i j), which has the opposite sign and the same product.
- **Definition** `def-determinant`: det A = the Leibniz sum over columns. `cor-alternating-forms-one-dimensional`, paying off §02.
- **Transpose** — `thm-det-transpose`: det Aᵀ = det A (reindex by σ⁻¹). Hence everything about columns holds for rows.
- **Triangular matrices** — `thm-det-triangular`: product of the diagonal.
- **Remark (commutative rings):** every proof in this section uses only +, −, ×, so it holds for matrices with entries in any commutative ring, e.g. F[x] (used in §10). Define commutative ring in one sentence.
- **Warning:** det is not linear: det(A + B) ≠ det A + det B; det(cA) = cⁿ det A; det(−A) = (−1)ⁿ det A.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** evaluate 3×3 determinants by Leibniz (6 terms) and verify; det of a permutation matrix is sgn σ (recall Ch 3 `def-permutation-matrix`); show det of a matrix with a zero row is 0 from the definition.
  - **C:** a matrix with more than n² − n zeros has determinant 0; the number of terms in the Leibniz formula vs the cost of elimination.

## 05-properties.md — Row operations and multiplicativity

**Keep** `thm-det-properties` from the old draft if it fits (a summary theorem of row-operation effects); otherwise use the new labels.

- **Row operations** — `thm-det-row-operations`: swap ⇒ ×(−1); scale a row by c ⇒ ×c; add a multiple ⇒ unchanged. Also for columns. Elementary matrices: det P_ij = −1, det D_i(c) = c, det(I + cE_ij) = 1 (recall Ch 3 notation).
- **Computing by elimination** — `exm-det-by-elimination`: 4×4 via row reduction with bookkeeping; verify with sympy. Cost remark.
- **Invertibility** — `thm-det-nonzero-iff-invertible`, via the RREF (Ch 3 TFAE). **Grow the TFAE:** add "det A ≠ 0" to `thm-invertible-tfae`, as a new theorem `thm-invertible-tfae-det` that cites the old one.
- **Multiplicativity** — `thm-det-multiplicative`, via **uniqueness**: fix A, then B ↦ det(AB) is alternating multilinear in the columns of B with value det A at I. Commutative-ring remark: this proof has no division. "Alternatively," a short proof by elementary matrices.
- **Corollaries:** det(A⁻¹) = 1/det A; similar matrices have equal determinants — `cor-det-similarity-invariant`; the determinant of an operator — `def-det-operator`, well defined (recall Ch 2 `thm-similar-iff-same-operator`); det(ST) = det S det T.
- **det is a group homomorphism** GL_n(F) → F^×; SL_n(F) is its kernel. Pays off Ch 0 §10's promise.
- **Warning:** det(AB) = det(BA) even when AB ≠ BA; but det(A + B) has no formula.
- **Check.**

  - **B:** determinants by elimination; for which t is a matrix invertible; det of the operator p ↦ p(x + 1) on ℝ[x]≤3 (upper triangular with ones: 1).
  - **C:** the determinant of an orthogonal-type real matrix (AᵀA = I) is ±1; det of the operator X ↦ AX on M_n(F) is (det A)ⁿ (hint: choose the basis of matrix units column by column).

## 06-cofactor-expansion.md — Cofactor expansion, the adjugate and Cramer's rule

- **Minors and cofactors** — `def-minor-cofactor`.
- **Laplace expansion** — `thm-laplace-expansion`: along any row or column. Proof: linearity in column j reduces to columns equal to eᵢ; then compute that determinant by moving row i and column j to the front, with sign (−1)^{i+j} (count the transpositions carefully). Commutative-ring remark.
- **Worked:** a 4×4 expansion along a row with zeros — `exm-laplace-4x4`; verify with sympy.
- **Adjugate** — `def-adjugate`. **Theorem** `thm-adjugate-identity`: A·adj A = adj A·A = (det A)I. Proof: an expansion along row i of the matrix with row k replaced by row i (a matrix with two equal rows) — the "look-alike matrix" move.
  - `cor-inverse-formula`: A⁻¹ = adj A / det A.
  - **Warning:** adj A exists even when A is not invertible, and A·adj A = 0 then.
- **Cramer's rule** — `thm-cramers-rule`, from the adjugate or by linearity. **Warning:** it is a theoretical tool and computationally terrible (n! vs n³).
- **Consequence:** entries of A⁻¹ are rational functions of the entries of A; integer matrices with det ±1 have integer inverses — `cor-integer-inverse`.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** adjugate and inverse of a 3×3; solve a 3×3 system by Cramer; det by expansion along the best row.
  - **C:** det(adj A) = (det A)^{n−1}; rank of adj A is n, 1 or 0 according as rank A is n, n − 1 or less.

## 07-minors-and-rank.md — Minors, rank and Cauchy–Binet

- **Submatrices and k×k minors** — `def-submatrix-minor` (general rows I and columns J).
- **Rank via minors** — `thm-rank-via-minors`: rank A is the largest k with a non-zero k×k minor. Proof via independent columns and a non-zero minor inside them (use Ch 3 rank theory).
- **Cauchy–Binet** — `thm-cauchy-binet`: det(AB) = Σ_{|S|=m} det A_{[m],S} det B_{S,[m]} for A m×n, B n×m. Proof via multilinearity in the columns of AB (a generalization of the uniqueness argument). Special cases: m > n ⇒ 0; m = n ⇒ multiplicativity.
  - **Worked** 2×3 times 3×2 — `exm-cauchy-binet`; verify with sympy.
  - **Consequence:** det(AAᵀ) ≥ 0 over ℝ, as a sum of squares — `cor-gram-determinant-nonnegative`; forward pointer to Gram matrices in Ch 11/13.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** find the rank of a matrix with a parameter via minors; verify Cauchy–Binet numerically; the Lagrange identity (|a|²|b|² − (a·b)² = Σ (a_ib_j − a_jb_i)²) from Cauchy–Binet.

## 08-special-determinants.md — Special determinants

## 10-characteristic-polynomial.md — The characteristic polynomial

- **Definition** `def-characteristic-polynomial`: p_A(x) = det(xI − A), computed by the Leibniz formula with entries in F[x] (commutative-ring remarks from §04–§06).
- **Theorem** `thm-charpoly-coefficients`: p_A is monic of degree n, the coefficient of x^{n−1} is −tr A, and the constant term is (−1)ⁿ det A. Proof via the Leibniz formula (only the identity permutation contributes degree ≥ n − 1).
- **Similarity invariance** — `thm-charpoly-similarity-invariant`: via multiplicativity over F[x] (valid by the remark), since xI − P⁻¹AP = P⁻¹(xI − A)P. `def-charpoly-operator`: the characteristic polynomial of an operator.
- **Worked:** p_A for 2×2 (x² − (tr A)x + det A) and 3×3; companion-type example; verify with sympy.
- **Payoff pointer:** the roots of p_A are the eigenvalues (Ch 9). Stated only; eigenvalue is not defined here. Note that p_A(c) = 0 ⇔ cI − A is not invertible, proved here from §05, **without naming eigenvalues**.
- **Warning:** p_A determines neither similarity nor the matrix: I₂ vs [[1,1],[0,1]] (recall Ch 4's warning). Also: substituting A into det(xI − A) is not "det(AI − A) = 0". Name the fake proof of Cayley–Hamilton and say why it is invalid, with a forward pointer to Ch 9.
- **Check.**
- **Exercises:**
  - **A.**
  - **B:** characteristic polynomials of listed matrices; show p_{Aᵀ} = p_A; show p_{AB} = p_{BA} for invertible A (the general case is Ch 9).
  - **C:** the characteristic polynomial of a companion matrix C(p) is p (by expansion along the last column), with a verification for degree 4; the constant term of p_A for a nilpotent 2×2 A.
