# Blueprint: Chapter 14 — Tensors and Exterior Algebra

**Directory:** `src/ch14-tensors/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Tensors and Exterior Algebra". **Part:** continues "Part IV · Forms and Multilinear Algebra" (no `part` key; Chapter 13 opens the part).

**Purpose.** Chapter 13 studied one bilinear form at a time. This chapter builds the space in which *all* multilinear maps become linear ones: the tensor product. Everything the book has done piecewise — the Kronecker product of Chapter 7, the determinant of Chapter 6, the Clifford algebra of Chapter 13 §12 — turns out to be a shadow of one construction. The chapter builds

- multilinear maps, and the problem they pose;
- the tensor product by its universal property, then by construction, with bases and dimension;
- tensor products of maps, and the Kronecker product identified as their matrix;
- contraction and the trace, basis-free;
- index notation, and what "transforms like a tensor" means;
- the tensor algebra;
- symmetric powers;
- exterior powers, and the determinant as the top one;
- the Grassmann algebra and decomposable vectors;
- the Clifford algebra constructed at last, discharging Chapter 13's debt;
- a closing application: tensors in quantum mechanics, entanglement as non-decomposability.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 0–13. This chapter needs Chapter 3 §09 (quotients) heavily and Chapter 4 (duals) constantly; both are early, so no field restriction is inherited. **The field is a general `F` throughout**, except where a statement genuinely needs characteristic 0 or `char ≠ 2` — say which and why, in the manner Chapter 13 established.

Part (a) is exactly what the constructions here need. Cite it; do not restate it. What the book *did* lack is the free vector space on an arbitrary set — §02 supplies `def-free-vector-space` and `lem-free-vector-space`, and the later quotient constructions should use those.
- Duals: `def-dual-space`, `def-linear-functional`, `thm-dual-basis`, `def-dual-map`, `thm-matrix-of-dual-map`, `thm-evaluation-natural` (Ch 4).
- Multilinearity: `def-multilinear-form`, `def-alternating-form`, `thm-skew-symmetric-alternating` (Ch 6 §02). **These are for forms `V^k → F`;** the chapter needs maps into a general `W`, so extend the definition and say you are extending it.
- Determinants: `def-determinant`, `thm-det-multiplicative`, `thm-det-transpose`, and the Leibniz formula — **`thm-leibniz-formula` does not exist**; find the real label in `src/ch06-determinants/04-existence-and-uniqueness.md` before citing.

**Notation.** `V \otimes W`, `\v \otimes \w`, `\Lambda^k V`, `\v_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \v_k`, `\Sym^k V` are already in NOTATION.md. New symbols this chapter needs — the tensor algebra `T(V)`, the exterior algebra `\Lambda V`, contraction, and the symmetric algebra — must be registered as they are introduced. Watch for collisions: `\T` is the bold matrix T, `\Lambda` is taken by Chapter 9's rotation-scaling block `\vLambda` (bold) — plain `\Lambda^k V` is free, but say so.

**Signature moves:**
- **Define by universal property, then build a model.** State what the object must do, prove uniqueness up to unique isomorphism from that alone, and only then construct one. Every section here follows this shape, and it is the chapter's pedagogical spine.
- **A quotient kills exactly the relations you want.** Free object modulo relations, three times over (tensor, symmetric, exterior).
- **Check a multilinear map is well defined by defining it on the free object first.**
- **Count a basis by counting index tuples** — increasing tuples for exterior, non-decreasing for symmetric.

## 01-multilinear-maps.md — Multilinear maps

- **Definition** `def-multilinear-map` (**major**): extend Chapter 6 §02's forms to maps `V_1 × … × V_k → W`; recall, do not redefine, and note the change.
- Examples: the determinant as an alternating `n`-linear form; matrix multiplication; the evaluation pairing; the cross product; a non-example (a linear map on the product, which is *not* bilinear — this confusion is worth its own warning, with the two definitions side by side).
- **The space of multilinear maps** `def-space-of-multilinear-maps`, `thm-multilinear-maps-dimension`: dimension `dim W · ∏ dim V_i`, by a basis-of-values argument. This is the count the tensor product will reproduce, so it is worth having first.
- **The problem** — state it plainly: multilinear maps are not linear maps, so none of the machinery of Chapters 3–5 applies to them. The chapter's whole job is to fix that.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 02-tensor-product.md — The tensor product

- **Universal property** `def-tensor-product` (**major**): a space `V ⊗ W` with a bilinear `⊗ : V × W → V ⊗ W` such that every bilinear map factors uniquely through it. **Theorem** `thm-tensor-unique`: any two such are isomorphic by a unique isomorphism commuting with the maps — prove it, it is the template for the whole chapter.
- **Construction** `thm-tensor-exists`: free vector space on `V × W` modulo the bilinearity relations. This needs the quotient machinery of Ch 3 §09, including `thm-quotient-universal-property` (which exists — see the correction above), and a free vector space on an arbitrary set, which the book genuinely lacks and §02 supplies.
- **Basis and dimension** `thm-tensor-basis`: `(v_i ⊗ w_j)` is a basis; `dim(V ⊗ W) = dim V · dim W`. Reconcile with §01's count.
- **Warnings, all three worth having:** not every element of `V ⊗ W` is a simple tensor `v ⊗ w` (give the standard `e₁⊗e₁ + e₂⊗e₂` and prove it is not simple); `v ⊗ w = 0` iff one factor is zero; `v ⊗ w = v' ⊗ w'` does not mean `v = v'`, `w = w'`.
- **Worked:** compute in `F² ⊗ F³` with coordinates, and identify the result with a 2×3 matrix — the identification §03 will generalize.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 03-tensor-products-of-maps.md — Maps, and the Kronecker product explained

- **Definition** `def-tensor-of-maps`, `thm-tensor-of-maps-well-defined`: `S ⊗ T` on `V ⊗ W`, via the universal property.
- **Theorem** `thm-kronecker-is-tensor` (**major**): in the bases `(v_i ⊗ w_j)` ordered lexicographically, the matrix of `S ⊗ T` is exactly the Kronecker product `[S] ⊗ [T]` of Chapter 7. **This discharges both of Chapter 7 §04's promises** — read them and answer in their words, and check the index convention of `def-kronecker-product` matches the ordering you choose (if it does not, say which order makes it true).
- **Corollaries** for free: `thm-kronecker-properties`'s mixed product rule, and the trace and determinant formulas of `thm-kronecker-rank-trace-det`, now with basis-free proofs. Cite Chapter 7 and say which facts become obvious.
- `thm-tensor-hom-iso`: `V* ⊗ W ≅ \cL(V, W)` naturally, with the finite-dimensionality hypothesis stated. This is the identification everything later uses.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 04-contraction-and-trace.md — Contraction

Short: ≈2,500–3,000 words.

- **Definition** `def-contraction`: the map `V* ⊗ V → F` induced by evaluation, and its extension to mixed tensors.
- **Theorem** `thm-trace-is-contraction`: under `thm-tensor-hom-iso`, contraction is the trace. **This is the basis-free definition of trace the book has wanted since Chapter 3 §10** — find what Chapter 3 says about trace being basis-independent and connect the two explicitly.
- Mixed tensors `def-tensor-type`, type `(p, q)`; contraction on a chosen pair of slots; the warning that *which* slots matter.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 05-index-notation.md — Coordinates, and what "transforms like a tensor" means

Short: ≈2,500–3,000 words.

- Components `T^{i_1…i_p}_{j_1…j_q}`, the summation convention, upper versus lower indices as `V` versus `V*` (cite Ch 4).
- **Theorem** `thm-tensor-transformation-law`: how components change under a change of basis, with the contravariant and covariant patterns derived, not asserted.
- **The classical definition** — "a tensor is an array that transforms like this" — stated, and proved equivalent to the modern one for finite-dimensional spaces: `thm-classical-equals-modern`. Say honestly why the older definition persists in physics.
- **Warning:** a matrix is not automatically a tensor; the transformation law is the content. Give an array that fails it.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 06-tensor-algebra.md — The tensor algebra

- `def-tensor-power`, `def-tensor-algebra`: `T(V) = ⊕_k V^{⊗k}`, graded, associative, non-commutative; the universal property `thm-tensor-algebra-universal` (maps into any associative unital algebra).
- **Why it is the right free object:** it is the free associative unital algebra on `V`. Prove the universal property; it is what §§07, 08 and 10 all quotient.
- Dimension is infinite even for finite-dimensional `V`; grading; `dim V^{⊗k} = n^k`.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 07-symmetric-powers.md — Symmetric powers

- `def-symmetric-power` by the quotient of `V^{⊗k}` killing `v⊗w − w⊗v`, with the universal property for symmetric multilinear maps.
- **Basis** `thm-symmetric-power-basis`: non-decreasing index tuples; `dim = \binom{n+k-1}{k}`. Prove the count.
- **Polynomials:** `thm-symmetric-algebra-is-polynomials`, `Sym(V) ≅ F[x_1, …, x_n]`, which is the payoff — connect to Chapter 5's polynomial ring.
- **Characteristic warning** *(corrected — the drafter sharpened this and was right)*: the symmetric power and the *symmetric tensors* (the invariants) never differ in **dimension** — the invariants of a permutation module have the orbit sums as a basis over every field, so both are `binom(n+k-1, k)`. What fails in characteristic `p` is the **natural map** from the invariants to the symmetric power: it is an isomorphism when `char F = 0` or `char F > k` (the inverse divides by `k!`), and for `char F = 2`, `k = 2` it is neither injective nor surjective, witnessed by `v₁⊗v₂ + v₂⊗v₁ ↦ 2v₁v₂ = 0`. Do not define the symmetric power as the invariants.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 08-exterior-powers.md — Exterior powers

- `def-exterior-power` (**major**) by the quotient killing `v ⊗ v`, and the universal property for alternating maps. Note deliberately that killing `v⊗v` (not `v⊗w + w⊗v`) is the characteristic-free choice — exactly Chapter 13 §03's distinction between alternating and skew.
- **Basis** `thm-exterior-power-basis` (**major**): strictly increasing tuples; `dim Λ^k V = \binom{n}{k}`; `Λ^k V = 0` for `k > n`.
- **Independence detects wedges** `thm-wedge-nonzero-iff-independent`: `v_1 ∧ … ∧ v_k ≠ 0` iff the list is independent. This is the tool for everything that follows.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 09-determinant-as-top-exterior-power.md — The determinant, again

- **Theorem** `thm-det-is-top-exterior` (**major**): `Λ^n V` is one-dimensional, and `Λ^n T` is multiplication by `det T`. **This is the third construction of the determinant in the book** — Chapter 6 built it twice (alternating forms, Leibniz). Read Chapter 6's uniqueness theorem and say exactly what this adds: not a new value, but the statement that the determinant *is* an induced map, from which multiplicativity is functoriality.
- `cor-det-multiplicative-again`, `cor-laplace-from-wedge`: recover multiplicativity in one line and Laplace expansion from `Λ^k`.
- **Minors and Plücker:** `thm-wedge-coordinates-are-minors` — the coordinates of `v_1 ∧ … ∧ v_k` are the `k × k` minors. Connect to Chapter 6's minors-and-rank section.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 10-grassmann-and-clifford.md — The exterior algebra, and Clifford at last

- `def-exterior-algebra`, `Λ V = ⊕ Λ^k V`, dimension `2^n`, graded-commutative.
- **Decomposable vectors** `def-decomposable`, and the fact that not every element of `Λ^2 V` is decomposable — give `e₁∧e₂ + e₃∧e₄` and prove it, via `ω ∧ ω ≠ 0`. This is the same phenomenon as §02's non-simple tensors and should be cross-referenced.
- **Clifford, constructed** `thm-clifford-exists` (**major**): `Cl(q) = T(V) / ⟨v ⊗ v − q(v)·1⟩`; verify the universal property of Chapter 13 §12's `def-clifford-algebra`; hence existence for every `V` and `q`. Then `thm-clifford-dimension-proved`: `dim Cl(q) = 2ⁿ`, by exhibiting `Λ V` as a model and showing the spanning set of Chapter 13's `prp-clifford-spanning` is independent. **This discharges Chapter 13 §12's explicitly recorded debt** — read the passage titled "What is proved here, and what is not" and answer it directly, saying which half was missing and is now supplied.
- Note that `Cl(q)` and `Λ V` are isomorphic as *vector spaces*, never as algebras unless `q = 0`.
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.

## 11-tensors-in-quantum-mechanics.md — An application

Short: ≈2,500–3,000 words. Closes the chapter and Part IV.

- **Setting, honestly bounded:** a quantum system is a complex inner product space; composite systems are tensor products. State the physical postulate as a postulate, not as something proved, in the way Chapter 13 §11 handled Minkowski space.
- **Entanglement is non-decomposability** — `def-entangled`, and the Bell state `(e₀⊗e₀ + e₁⊗e₁)/√2` shown non-simple by §02's argument. The linear algebra is entirely §02's; the interpretation is one paragraph.
- **Schmidt decomposition** `thm-schmidt-decomposition`: every vector of `V ⊗ W` is `∑ σ_i v_i ⊗ w_i` with orthonormal families and `σ_i > 0` — and observe that this **is the SVD of Chapter 12 §08** read through `thm-tensor-hom-iso`. Prove it that way; it is the cleanest payoff available and ties Part III to Part IV.
- `def-schmidt-rank`, and: rank 1 ⇔ not entangled.
- **Closing summary of Part IV.**
- **Check. Exercises** A/B/C.
