# Blueprint: Chapter 20 — Matrix Inequalities

**Directory:** `src/ch20-inequalities/` (new). **Chapter title:** "Matrix Inequalities". **Part:** closes "Part V · Matrix Analysis" — add `src/ch20-inequalities` to `config/config.json` after `src/ch19-perturbation`, **with a minimal two-line edit** (never round-trip the JSON). The coordinator does this, not the drafters.

**Purpose.** Five chapters have deferred things to this one, and they are all the same thing seen from different sides: an inequality between two matrices that holds for **every** unitarily invariant norm, or for **every** convex function, at once. The organizing fact is majorization. Chapter 16 §08 defined it and proved Schur's theorem; Chapter 18 §07 proved Birkhoff's. This chapter closes the circle (Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya), turns majorization into norm inequalities (symmetric gauge functions, Ky Fan dominance), and then treats the order-theoretic side: operator monotone and operator convex functions, Loewner's theorem, and the trace inequalities of Golden and Thompson.

**The one import, decided by the author.** Loewner's theorem is promised *proved* here (Ch 16 §11 says so four times). Its analytic half cannot be proved with (A1)–(A6). The book therefore does what it did for the fundamental theorem of algebra's cousins: **it quotes one named theorem at its point of use and proves everything else.** The quoted fact is the Nevanlinna–Pick theory of Pick functions:

> **(A7) (Pick and Nevanlinna.)** Let \(I \subseteq \nR\) be an open interval and \(f \colon I \to \nR\). If the Pick (Loewner) matrix \(\bigl[f[t_i, t_j]\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{n}\) of divided differences is positive semidefinite for every \(n\) and all \(t_1, \dots, t_n \in I\), then \(f\) is the restriction to \(I\) of a function analytic on \(\nC \setminus (\nR \setminus I)\) mapping the upper half-plane into its closure; and every such function has the integral representation of `thm-loewner-statement` (ii).

**Rules for (A7).** It is stated **once**, in §10, in a block of its own, named at every use, and the chapter says plainly which half of Loewner's theorem rests on it. **The elementary half — that operator monotonicity of every order is exactly positivity of every Loewner matrix — is proved here in full** (§09). Nothing else in the chapter may use (A7). Record it in the index's imported-analysis list beside (A1)–(A6), marked as this chapter's own.

**Conventions to state once and obey.**
- \(\vlambda(\A)\) is the decreasing eigenvalue list of a Hermitian \(\A\) (NOTATION), \(\sigma(\A)\) the decreasing singular value list, \(\x^{\downarrow}\) the decreasing rearrangement.
- **Majorization** \(\prec\) and **weak majorization** \(\prec_w\) are Ch 16 §08's (`def-majorization`); weak majorization there is the top-sums version. This chapter also needs \(\prec^{w}\), majorization from below (every **bottom** \(k\) sum of \(\x^{\downarrow}\) is at least that of \(\y^{\downarrow}\)) — define it where first used, and warn that the two are different.
- **A unitarily invariant norm is written \(\uinorm{\A}\)** (triple bars). Add the macro `\uinorm` to `latex/macros.tex` (coordinator's job) as `\left|\!\left|\!\left| #1 \right|\!\right|\!\right|`.
- **Symmetric gauge functions are \(\Phi\)**; Ch 17 §04's gauge \(p_K\) of a convex set is a different object with the same word, and §03 must say so once.
- **The Loewner order** is \(\A \succeq \B\) (Ch 12 §05); entrywise order never appears in this chapter.
- Matrices are complex unless a statement says otherwise; \(f\) applied to a Hermitian matrix is the spectral functional calculus of Ch 11 §08.

## 01-hardy-littlewood-polya.md — Doubly stochastic maps
- Recall `def-majorization` and `prp-doubly-stochastic-image-majorized` (the easy half, already proved in Ch 18).
- `def-t-transform`: \(\T = (1-t)\I + t\Q\) with \(\Q\) a transposition matrix, \(t \in [0,1]\). It is doubly stochastic; it averages two coordinates and fixes the rest.
- `lem-t-transform-step`: if \(\x \prec \y\) and \(\x \ne \y^{\downarrow}\), there is a T-transform \(\T\) and a vector \(\y'= \T\y\) with \(\x \prec \y'\) and \(\y'\) agreeing with \(\x\) in **strictly more** coordinates. (Standard route: sort both; take \(j\) largest with \(y_j > x_j\) and \(k > j\) smallest with \(y_k < x_k\); average those two. Verify every inequality.)
- `thm-hardy-littlewood-polya` (**major**): \(\x \prec \y\) **iff** \(\x = \D\y\) for some doubly stochastic \(\D\), and then \(\D\) may be taken a product of at most \(n-1\) T-transforms.
  - **Pays Ch 18 §07 and §09.** Quote them.
  - With `thm-birkhoff`, \(\D\) is also a convex combination of permutation matrices, so \(\x \prec \y\) iff \(\x \in \conv\{\P_\sigma\y\}\). State that as `cor-majorization-convex-hull`, and note the picture: the majorization order is containment of permutation polytopes.
- Worked example, exact: a 4-vector pair, the T-transforms exhibited, the doubly stochastic matrix written out.
- Warning: \(\D\) is not unique, and "doubly stochastic" cannot be weakened to "row-stochastic".

## 02-convex-functions-and-majorization.md — Convex functions of a majorization
- `thm-karamata` (**major**): \(\x \prec \y\) implies \(\sum_i\phi(x_i) \le \sum_i\phi(y_i)\) for every convex \(\phi \colon \nR \to \nR\).
  - Route, three lines: \(x_i = \sum_j d_{ij}y_j\) by §01, `thm-jensen` rowwise, then sum and use the column sums. **This is the payoff of §01.**
  - Converse (`prp-majorization-from-convex`): if the inequality holds for every convex \(\phi\), then \(\x \prec \y\). Test functions \(\pm t\) and \(t \mapsto (t - c)_{+}\). (Check the second gives the top-\(k\) sums; the standard identity is \(\sum_i (x_i - c)_+ \ge \sum_{i \le k} x_i^{\downarrow} - kc\) with equality at \(c = x_k^{\downarrow}\).)
  - Weak version: \(\x \prec_w \y\) gives the same for every **increasing** convex \(\phi\).
- `def-schur-convex`, `def-schur-concave`; `thm-symmetric-convex-is-schur-convex`: a symmetric convex function is Schur-convex (via `cor-majorization-convex-hull` and Jensen).
- `cor-product-schur-concave`: for non-negative vectors, \(\x \prec \y\) implies \(\prod x_i \ge \prod y_i\). **Pays Ch 16 §08.** (Route: \(-\log\) is convex, plus a limit argument for zero entries — handle \(0\) honestly.)
- `lem-peierls` : for Hermitian \(\A\) with spectrum in \(I\), convex \(f\) and a unit vector \(\u\), \(f(\inner{\A\u}{\u}) \le \inner{f(\A)\u}{\u}\). (Spectral resolution plus Jensen.)
- `thm-trace-convex` (**major**): \(\A \mapsto \tr f(\A)\) is convex on the Hermitian matrices with spectrum in \(I\), for every convex \(f\). **Pays Ch 17 §10 and §11.** Route: apply `lem-peierls` at an orthonormal eigenbasis of \(\theta\A + (1-\theta)\B\) and sum. Note in the aftermath that this is much weaker than operator convexity (§08).
- Negative: a symmetric function that is not Schur-convex; two incomparable vectors.
- Worked exercises on Schur-convexity of \(\sum x_i^2\), \(\max x_i\), the entropy \(-\sum x_i\log x_i\) (Schur-concave).

## 03-symmetric-gauge-functions.md — Symmetric gauge functions
- `def-symmetric-gauge`: a norm \(\Phi\) on \(\nR^n\) with \(\Phi(\P\x) = \Phi(\x)\) for every permutation matrix and \(\Phi(\varepsilon_1x_1, \dots, \varepsilon_nx_n) = \Phi(\x)\) for signs \(\varepsilon_i = \pm1\). **Say once that Ch 17 §04's gauge \(p_K\) is a different object.**
- Examples: \(\norm{\cdot}_p\) for \(1 \le p \le \infty\); the Ky Fan gauges \(\Phi_k(\x) = \sum_{i\le k}\lvert x\rvert^{\downarrow}_i\); a non-example (a norm that is not sign-invariant, or not permutation-invariant).
- `prp-gauge-monotone`: \(\lvert x_i\rvert \le \lvert y_i\rvert\) for all \(i\) implies \(\Phi(\x) \le \Phi(\y)\). (Route: one coordinate at a time, \(x_i = \theta y_i + (1-\theta)(-y_i)\), convexity.)
- `lem-weak-majorization-dominated`: for non-negative \(\x, \y\), \(\x \prec_w \y\) iff there is \(\z\) with \(\x \le \z\) entrywise and \(\z \prec \y\). (Prove it; this is the bridge and it is the step most likely to be fudged.)
- `thm-gauge-monotone-under-majorization` (**major**): \(\x \prec_w \y\) (non-negative entries) implies \(\Phi(\x) \le \Phi(\y)\); and \(\x \prec \y\) implies it for all real vectors.
- `def-dual-gauge` and `thm-dual-gauge`: \(\Phi^{*}\) is a symmetric gauge and \(\Phi^{**} = \Phi\). Cite Ch 17 §04's duality; only the symmetry needs work.

## 04-unitarily-invariant-norms.md — Unitarily invariant norms
- `def-unitarily-invariant-norm`: a norm on \(M_{m\times n}\) with \(\uinorm{\U\A\V} = \uinorm{\A}\) for all unitary \(\U, \V\). **Pays Ch 15 §08's "which norms are invariant under unitary multiplication".**
- Examples: the Frobenius norm, the spectral norm, the **Ky Fan \(k\)-norms** \(\uinorm{\A}_{(k)} = \sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(\A)\) — **pays Ch 16 §06's promise**, and the trace norm \(k = n\); the Schatten \(p\)-norms.
- Non-example: the entrywise maximum; the operator 1-norm (largest column sum) is not unitarily invariant. Verify with a rotation.
- `lem-ui-norm-of-diagonal`: \(\uinorm{\A}\) depends only on \(\sigma(\A)\), and \(\Phi_{\uinorm{\cdot}}(\x) \coloneqq \uinorm{\diag(\x)}\) is a symmetric gauge.
- `thm-von-neumann-correspondence` (**major**): \(\uinorm{\cdot} \mapsto \Phi\) is a bijection between unitarily invariant norms on \(M_{m\times n}\) and symmetric gauges on \(\nR^{\min(m,n)}\), with \(\uinorm{\A} = \Phi(\sigma(\A))\).
  - The hard half is that \(\A \mapsto \Phi(\sigma(\A))\) satisfies the triangle inequality. Route: `lem-singular-value-subadditive` (\(\sigma(\A+\B) \prec_w \sigma(\A) + \sigma(\B)\), from `cor-ky-fan-subadditive` applied to the Hermitian dilations `prp-hermitian-dilation`, or from the Ky Fan maximum principle for singular values — verify which is cleanest) plus §03's `thm-gauge-monotone-under-majorization`.
- `prp-ui-norm-properties`: \(\uinorm{\A^{*}} = \uinorm{\A}\); \(\uinorm{\A\B} \le \norm{\A}_2\uinorm{\B}\) and \(\uinorm{\A\B} \le \uinorm{\A}\norm{\B}_2\) (the two-sided bound Ch 19 §09's toolkit proved for two norms — cite it and generalize); normalization \(\uinorm{\e_1\e_1^{*}} = 1\) makes \(\norm{\A}_2 \le \uinorm{\A} \le \uinorm{\A}_{(n)}\).
- Warning: not every unitarily invariant norm is submultiplicative (\(2\norm{\cdot}_2\) is, \(\tfrac12\norm{\cdot}_2\) is not; find the honest example and check it).

## 05-von-neumann-trace-inequality.md — The trace inequality
- `thm-von-neumann-trace` (**major**): \(\lvert\tr(\A^{*}\B)\rvert \le \sum_i \sigma_i(\A)\sigma_i(\B)\), with equality iff \(\A\) and \(\B\) have simultaneous singular value decompositions with matching order (state the equality case only if the drafter proves it).
  - Route: SVDs, then \(\tr(\A^{*}\B) = \sum_{i,j}\sigma_i\tau_j w_{ij}\) with \(W\) built from the unitaries; the matrix \((\lvert w_{ij}\rvert)\) is **doubly substochastic**; then Birkhoff/HLP as in Ch 19 §05, or the rearrangement inequality. **Verify the substochastic claim.**
- `cor-trace-duality`: \(\uinorm{\A}^{*} = \Phi^{*}(\sigma(\A))\) — the dual of a unitarily invariant norm with respect to the Frobenius pairing is the norm of the dual gauge. Hence the trace norm and the spectral norm are dual, and the Frobenius norm is self-dual.
- Worked example, exact: two 2×2 matrices where the inequality is strict, and the aligned pair where it is equality.

## 06-ky-fan-dominance.md — Ky Fan dominance
- `thm-ky-fan-dominance` (**major**): \(\sigma(\A) \prec_w \sigma(\B)\) **iff** \(\uinorm{\A} \le \uinorm{\B}\) for every unitarily invariant norm. (⇐: take the Ky Fan \(k\)-norms. ⇒: §03.)
- `cor-eckart-young-ui` (**major**): the truncated SVD \(\A_k\) minimizes \(\uinorm{\A - \B}\) over all \(\B\) of rank at most \(k\), for **every** unitarily invariant norm, and the minimum is \(\Phi(\sigma_{k+1}, \dots)\). **Pays Ch 12 §10, Ch 15 §08 and Ch 16 §09, by the route Ch 12 predicted:** the engine is \(\sigma_{i}(\A - \B) \ge \sigma_{k+i}(\A)\) for rank \(\B \le k\) (from `thm-weyl-singular-values`), then dominance.
- `cor-polar-nearest-ui`: the unitary polar factor minimizes \(\uinorm{\A - \Q}\) over unitary \(\Q\), in every unitarily invariant norm. **Pays Ch 12 §09.** (Route: \(\uinorm{\A - \Q} = \uinorm{\W(\lvert\A\rvert - \W^{*}\Q)}\), then the singular values of \(\lvert\A\rvert - \U\) for unitary \(\U\) dominate those of \(\lvert\A\rvert - \I\); verify.)
- Negative: \(\sigma(\A+\B) \prec_w \sigma(\A)+\sigma(\B)\) cannot be strengthened to \(\prec\) (the totals differ); an example.
- Quick application: every unitarily invariant norm is monotone under compression \(\uinorm{\P\A\P} \le \uinorm{\A}\) for an orthogonal projection \(\P\), if that is short.

## 08-operator-convexity.md — Operator convex functions
- Recall `def-operator-monotone` (b) and `prp-square-operator-convex`.
- `thm-operator-convex-examples`: \(t^2\), \(t^{-1}\) on \((0,\infty)\), \(-\log t\), and \(t\log t\) — prove what is provable directly (\(t^2\) and \(t^{-1}\) are Ch 12/16; \(-\log\) and \(t \log t\) may wait for §10's representation, in which case say so and move them there).

## 09-loewner-matrices.md — Loewner matrices
- **This section is the elementary heart of Loewner's theorem, and it is proved in full.**
- `def-divided-difference`: \(f[s,t] = (f(s) - f(t))/(s - t)\) for \(s \ne t\), \(f[t,t] = f'(t)\); `def-loewner-matrix` \(L_f(t_1,\dots,t_n) = \bigl(f[t_i,t_j]\bigr)\), symmetric.
- `thm-derivative-of-a-matrix-function` (**major**): for \(\A = \diag(t_1,\dots,t_n)\) with **distinct** \(t_i\) in the interval, Hermitian \(\H\), and \(f \in C^1\),
  \[
  \frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0} f(\A + s\H) = L_f(t_1,\dots,t_n) \circ \H ,
  \]
  the Hadamard product (`def-hadamard-product`).
  - Route: the eigenvalues of \(\A + s\H\) are simple for small \(s\), so Ch 19 §11's `thm-simple-eigenvalue-derivative` gives \(\lambda_i'(0) = h_{ii}\); for the eigenprojections, differentiate the spectral resolution, or differentiate \(f\) through \(f(\A+s\H) = \sum_i f(\lambda_i(s))\P_i(s)\) using the adjugate formula of Ch 19 §11 for \(\P_i(s)\). **The drafter must supply a complete proof; this is the section's hardest step.** An alternative route that avoids eigenprojections: prove it first for \(f(t) = t^k\) by expanding \((\A + s\H)^k\) and checking the entries against divided differences of \(t^k\), then for polynomials by linearity, then for \(C^1\) functions by a density/limit argument on a compact interval. **Take whichever route can be written honestly; say which.**
- `thm-loewner-matrix-criterion` (**major**): for \(f \in C^1\) on an interval \(I\), \(f\) is monotone of order \(n\) (order-preserving on Hermitian \(n\times n\) matrices with spectrum in \(I\)) **iff** every Loewner matrix \(L_f(t_1,\dots,t_n)\) with \(t_i \in I\) is positive semidefinite.
  - Route: (⇐) uses `thm-schur-product` (the Schur product theorem, Ch 12 §07): \(L \circ \H \succeq 0\) when both factors are; integrate along the segment \(\B + s(\A - \B)\) — or, to avoid integration, use a connectedness/monotonicity argument on \(s \mapsto \inner{f(\B + s(\A-\B))\x}{\x}\), whose derivative is \(\ge 0\) (the mean value theorem, (A6)). (⇒) take \(\H\) rank one to force \(L \succeq 0\).
  - Corollary `cor-monotone-order-two`: the \(2\times2\) case is \(f' \ge 0\) plus a concavity-flavoured condition; use it to re-derive Ch 16 §11's examples and to show \(t^2\) fails at order 2. Verify.
- Worked examples: \(L_f\) for \(f(t) = \sqrt t\) (a Cauchy matrix, positive semidefinite) and for \(f(t) = t^2\) (indefinite already at \(n = 2\)).

## 10-loewners-theorem.md — Loewner's theorem
- **Matrix-valued integrals, built here.** `def-matrix-integral`: for continuous \(\M \colon [a,b] \to M_n(\nC)\), \(\int_a^b \M(t)\,\dd t\) entrywise, as a limit of Riemann sums (uniform continuity on a compact interval, (A3)/(A4)). `lem-integral-preserves-loewner`: if \(\M(t) \succeq 0\) for every \(t\) then \(\int \M \succeq 0\) — the positive semidefinite cone is closed (Ch 12) and a limit of sums of PSD matrices stays in it. Improper integrals as limits of proper ones. Keep this to one page; it is scaffolding, not a theory.
- **(A7)**, stated in its own block, named, with the plain statement of what it gives and what it costs. The chapter's only import beyond (A1)–(A6).
- `thm-loewner` (**major**): the three conditions of Ch 16 §11's `thm-loewner-statement` are equivalent.
  - (ii) ⇒ (i): `cor-shifted-inverse-operator-monotone` plus `lem-integral-preserves-loewner`. **Fully proved here** — this is the direction Ch 16 §11 said the book lacked "the analysis that carries the Loewner order through an integral" for. Say so.
  - (i) ⇒ Loewner matrices positive semidefinite: §09, for every \(n\). (State the \(C^1\) hypothesis and where it comes from; if the drafter cannot remove it, the theorem is stated for \(C^1\) functions and the text says that operator monotone functions are automatically smooth, quoting that as part of (A7) rather than proving it.)
  - Loewner matrices ⇒ (iii) ⇒ (ii): **(A7)**.
- `cor-power-operator-monotone` (**major**): \(t \mapsto t^{p}\) is operator monotone on \((0,\infty)\) exactly for \(0 \le p \le 1\). **Pays Ch 12 §05 and Ch 16 §11.**
  - Use the explicit representation \(t^{p} = c_p\int_0^{\infty}\frac{t}{t+s}s^{p-1}\,\dd s\) with \(c_p = \sin(p\pi)/\pi\); the scalar identity is a calculus fact — **name it where it is used**, and prove what can be proved (the substitution \(s = tu\) reduces it to the constant). The failure for \(p > 1\) follows from §09's order-2 criterion.
- `cor-log-operator-monotone`: \(\log\) is operator monotone on \((0,\infty)\) (representation \(\log t = \int_0^\infty\bigl(\frac{1}{1+s} - \frac{1}{t+s}\bigr)\dd s\); verify), and `exm-exp-not-monotone`: \(e^t\) is not, with a 2×2 counterexample. **Pays Ch 12 §05's "companion fact".**
- `cor-operator-convex-log`: \(-\log\) and \(t\log t\) are operator convex, if §08 deferred them.
- Warning: operator monotone is much stronger than monotone; the classification says the operator monotone functions on \((0,\infty)\) form a very thin set (analytic, and determined by a measure).

## 11-matrix-means-and-inequalities.md — Means and matrix Cauchy–Schwarz
- `thm-matrix-cauchy-schwarz`: \(\uinorm{\A^{*}\B}^2 \le \uinorm{\A^{*}\A}\,\uinorm{\B^{*}\B}\) for every unitarily invariant norm (the Cauchy–Schwarz of matrices; route through §06 dominance or the Schatten case — **prove what is provable and scope the rest honestly**).
- `thm-matrix-am-gm`: \(2\uinorm{\A^{*}\B} \le \uinorm{\A^{*}\A + \B^{*}\B}\); and the singular-value form \(2\sigma_i(\A^{*}\B) \le \sigma_i(\A^{*}\A + \B^{*}\B)\) if clean.
- `def-geometric-mean`: \(\A \# \B = \A^{1/2}(\A^{-1/2}\B\A^{-1/2})^{1/2}\A^{1/2}\) for positive definite \(\A, \B\); `thm-geometric-mean-properties`: symmetry \(\A\#\B = \B\#\A\), monotonicity, the AM–GM inequality \(\A\#\B \preceq \tfrac12(\A+\B)\), and the extremal characterization as the largest \(\X = \X^{*}\) with \(\begin{psmallmatrix}\A & \X\\ \X & \B\end{psmallmatrix} \succeq 0\) (Ch 12 §06's block criterion). Symmetry is the surprising part — prove it.
- The harmonic mean and the chain \(\bigl(\tfrac{\A^{-1}+\B^{-1}}{2}\bigr)^{-1} \preceq \A\#\B \preceq \tfrac{\A+\B}{2}\), using §10's operator monotonicity of \(t^{1/2}\) and `prp-inverse-operator-monotone`.
- Warning: \(\A^{1/2}\B^{1/2}\) is not even Hermitian in general, which is why the geometric mean has the form it does.

## 12-golden-thompson.md — Golden–Thompson, and what is not proved
- `lem-lie-product`: \(e^{\A+\B} = \lim_{k\to\infty}\bigl(e^{\A/k}e^{\B/k}\bigr)^{k}\) for all \(\A, \B\) (Ch 9's exponential and Ch 15's norm bounds; prove it — the standard telescoping estimate).
- `lem-trace-power-inequality`: for positive semidefinite \(\X, \Y\) and \(m = 2^{r}\), \(\tr\bigl((\X\Y)^{m}\bigr) \le \tr\bigl(\X^{m}\Y^{m}\bigr)$. (Route: repeated Cauchy–Schwarz for the trace inner product; verify carefully, including that \(\tr((\X\Y)^m)\) is real and non-negative.)
- `thm-golden-thompson` (**major**): for Hermitian \(\A, \B\), \(\tr e^{\A+\B} \le \tr\bigl(e^{\A}e^{\B}\bigr)\).
- `exm-three-matrices-fails`: the three-matrix version is false. **Find and verify an explicit counterexample** (2×2 or 3×3, exact or numerically certain), and say plainly if only a numerical certificate is given.
- `thm-klein` (or Peierls–Bogoliubov, whichever the drafter proves cleanly): the Klein inequality \(\tr\bigl(f(\A) - f(\B) - (\A-\B)f'(\B)\bigr) \ge 0\) for convex differentiable \(f\), and the non-negativity of the relative entropy \(\tr\A(\log\A - \log\B) \ge 0\) for density matrices, as its corollary. Prove both; they are §02's Peierls lemma plus the spectral resolution.
- **Lieb's concavity theorem** — \((\A, \B) \mapsto \tr\bigl(\X^{*}\A^{p}\X\B^{q}\bigr)\) is jointly concave for \(p + q \le 1\) — is **stated with credit and not proved**; say what it needs and that nothing in the book depends on it. Chapter 23 is not promised anything here.
- **Closing "Summary and transfer"** for the chapter, and for Part V as a whole: the named moves (a majorization is a doubly stochastic map; a symmetric gauge turns a weak majorization into a norm inequality; a Hermitian dilation turns singular values into eigenvalues; the Schur product theorem turns a Loewner matrix into monotonicity; a limit of Riemann sums carries the Loewner order through an integral; Lie's product formula turns a sum in the exponent into a product).
