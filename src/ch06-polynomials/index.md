# Polynomials

Integers and polynomials share one arithmetic. Both can be divided with a remainder, and from that single tool everything else follows: greatest common divisors, Bézout identities, unique factorization into primes or irreducibles, and the Chinese Remainder Theorem. This chapter develops that arithmetic for \( F[x] \) in full, because from Chapter 9 on the structure of an operator is read off from polynomials: which polynomials kill it, how they factor, and which of their factors are coprime.

Along the way we keep two promises made in Chapter 0: we prove division with remainder, stated there only for orientation, and the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, that every non-constant complex polynomial has a root. We also prove that polynomials factor uniquely into irreducibles, just as integers factor uniquely into primes. The chapter ends by plugging an operator into a polynomial, where the arithmetic of \( F[x] \) turns into a splitting of the space into pieces.

From Chapter 1, the standard bases of the polynomial spaces and their dimensions (@exm-standard-bases, @exm-dimensions), counting instead of checking (@thm-right-size-basis, @thm-size-bounds), and direct sums (@def-direct-sum, @thm-direct-sum-criteria), which is what the kernel splitting lemma of §07 produces. From Chapter 2, polynomials of an operator (@def-polynomial-of-operator). From Chapter 3, the factor theorem and root bound for linear factors (@lem-factor-theorem-linear, @lem-root-bound) and interpolation (@thm-interpolation-unique). From Chapter 4, quotient spaces and the First Isomorphism Theorem (@def-quotient-space, @thm-first-isomorphism). From Chapter 5, dual bases (@thm-dual-basis).

## Roadmap

- **Division with remainder.** Long division always works over a field, with a unique quotient and remainder; the remainder and factor theorems in general.
- **Ideals and greatest common divisors.** Every ideal of \( F[x] \) is generated by one polynomial; gcds, Bézout identities and the Euclidean algorithm.
- **Irreducible polynomials and unique factorization.** The primes of \( F[x] \), and why factorization into them is unique.
- **Roots, multiplicity and derivatives.** Repeated roots detected by the formal derivative, and Taylor expansion of a polynomial.
- **The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra.** A complete proof, and what it says about real polynomials.
- **Interpolation and the Chinese Remainder Theorem.** The Lagrange formula, solving simultaneous congruences, and the quotient-space picture behind both.
- **Polynomials of an operator.** Every operator on a finite-dimensional space satisfies a polynomial, and coprime factors split the space.

## Named moves

- **Divide and look at the remainder.** Most statements about divisibility reduce to one division.
- **The smallest-degree element generates.** In an ideal, a non-zero element of least degree divides every other element.
- **Evaluate at a root.** Plugging a root of the divisor into \( f = qg + r \) kills \( qg \) and reads off information about the remainder.
- **Compare degrees.** Uniqueness of quotients, factorizations and interpolants usually comes from a degree count.
- **Bézout gives projections.** When \( \gcd(p, q) = 1 \), write \( 1 = ap + bq \); evaluated at an operator, the two terms split the space.
